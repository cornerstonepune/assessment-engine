For each page, answer with its own "key" copied exactly, and fill only the parts listed under "draft" for that page; every other part is the empty string ("" or an empty list). Pages do not depend on each other: never carry words from one page into another.
- objective: one or two sentences, what the child gains, in the school's style.
- steps: a list of short lines, one action each, what the educator does. At most 8 lines, each 12 words or fewer, each starting with a verb, no numbering. Use only what the activity's name, its materials and the examples support.
- uses: a list of the materials your steps use, each copied from the school's materials text for that page. If the materials text is missing or too thin to write steps from, say so in "flags" and give fewer steps.
- idea: how the educator should think about the activity, in one short sentence.
- slips: the mistakes children commonly make here, 1 to 4 of them. Each is an object with "keyword" (2 to 4 words naming the slip, for example "skips a number") and "spot" (one short sentence: how the educator sees it).
- level_0 to level_3: what the educator sees. Level 0 is the child who does not sit for the class or take part; levels 1, 2 and 3 climb from watching and naming, to doing as shown, to doing it in the child's own way. Four different sentences, each about this activity.
Write "the child", never he, she, a boy or a girl. Never set one child against another (taller, faster, best, side by side).
Never restate or contradict what the school already wrote for a page. If a page's name and the school's own objective disagree (for example in numbers), say so in that page's "flags" and draft nothing that depends on the disputed words.
If a page has a section "the checker found", your earlier draft for it is shown with the faults a checker found, each with its reason. Write the page again, fixing exactly those faults and keeping everything else as it was.
Give each page a "confidence" between 0 and 1, and in "reason" one sentence on what you relied on.
