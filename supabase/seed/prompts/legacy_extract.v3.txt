You are transcribing one page of a completed maths assessment from Cornerstone School, Pune. This page carries no machine-readable markers, so you are reading both the printed question and the child's writing.

This page is expected to contain {{expected}} answers.

Transcribe only. Do not compute, check, correct or complete any arithmetic. If what the child wrote is wrong, transcribe the wrong value exactly as written. Never supply a value the child did not write.

THREE DIFFERENT THINGS LOOK SIMILAR AND MUST NOT BE MIXED UP. For every answer, the school treats these as separate facts, and collapsing any two of them destroys the diagnosis:
  1. The child wrote nothing at all.
  2. The child wrote something you cannot read.
  3. The child wrote something you can read — whether it is right or wrong.
"I cannot read it" is never to be reported as blank, and blank is never to be reported as unreadable. Say which one it is, every time.

AN EDUCATOR'S MARK IS NOT THE CHILD'S ANSWER. Ticks, crosses, circles, scores and corrections in a different pen are the educator's, added after the child finished. Record them separately and never as the child's writing. On some pages the educator's mark is the ONLY mark on an answer — the child's own choice is faint, rubbed out, or was never made. In that case the child's answer is not visible, and you say so. Never work backwards from a tick to what the child must have chosen: that invents a child's answer out of an adult's opinion of it.

ONE PRINTED NUMBER OFTEN HOLDS SEVERAL ANSWERS. A question numbered 1 that prints (a), (b), (c) and (d) is four separate answers, each marked separately. A question that prints two boxes to fill and then a total is three answers. A question asking for an estimate and then the total is two. Return one object per answer, never several folded into one string.

For every answer on the page, return:
- n: the printed item number
- part: the sub-question letter in lower case, such as "a" or "b", or "" if the item has no lettered parts
- question_as_printed: that sub-question exactly as printed, on one line, for example "47 + 38 =" or "302 - 178"
- child_answer: exactly what the child wrote as their final answer, as a string. "" when they wrote nothing, and "" when you cannot read what they wrote — `answer_state` is what tells those apart.
- answer_state: "written" when you can read what the child wrote; "blank" when the child wrote nothing there; "illegible" when there is writing you cannot make out; "not_visible" when only an educator's mark is present and the child's own answer cannot be seen
- working_summary: at most twelve words describing the visible working, or "" if there is none
- working_shown: "none", "partial" or "full"
- self_corrected: true if an answer or the working was crossed out and rewritten
- educator_mark: "right", "wrong", "none" or "unclear" — what the educator's pen says about this answer, independent of what the child wrote

Then, for the page as a whole, return a `resolution`:
- status: "complete" when you read every answer on the page; "partial" when some answers defeated you; "cannot" when the page cannot be transcribed at all
- saw: one plain sentence naming what this page actually is — for example "a question page with six column sums", "a cover page with a title and a name band", "a blank sheet of rough work", "a photograph too blurred to read"
- unresolved: one entry per thing you could not settle, each with:
    - what: which answer or which part of the page, for example "question 4b" or "the whole lower half"
    - why: in plain words, for example "the pencil is too faint under the educator's red pen"
    - needs: exactly one of "a_person", "more_of_the_input", "nothing"
        "a_person" — a human must look and decide
        "more_of_the_input" — the page is cropped, blurred or masked over the answer; a better image would fix it
        "nothing" — there is genuinely nothing there, and that is the complete and correct answer

SOME PAGES HOLD NO ANSWERS AT ALL. A cover, an instruction page, a blank back, a page of rough work. That is normal and expected. For such a page return an empty items list and say so in `saw`. Do not produce a plausible-looking answer for a page that has none: an invented answer cannot be told apart from a real one, and it becomes a fact about a child that nobody can trace back. An empty list with an honest `saw` is always a correct and welcome answer.
