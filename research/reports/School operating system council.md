# Own the evidence, amplify the teacher

The evidence supports building Cornerstone's whole-school system as an evidence spine that makes teachers sharper and faster, not as an AI that teaches, marks or judges children on its own. Every credible 2024–26 learning gain from AI came from narrow, answer-key-grounded tools with adults in charge — real-time AI coaching of human tutors raised mastery by 9 percentage points among the weakest tutors' pupils — while unrestricted chat cut unassisted exam scores by 17%, and the screen-first and "guides instead of teachers" models that scaled lost parents' trust, closed, or fell far short on public state tests. No product surveyed, global or Indian, keeps a longitudinal per-child skill-and-evidence record at its core, so that record is the thing to build; models, speech recognition and commodity operations are the things to buy, behind one-file adapters and export clauses. Returns will be real but modest: good formative systems move outcomes about 0.2–0.3 standard deviations, mostly for the weakest children, and the only high-security trial of AI lesson planning saved 25 minutes a week, so teacher hours come back only when a task is retired — code marking computable answers, data entered once, reports and parent notes drafted from evidence — and only if the review queue does not consume them. That queue is the load-bearing part: checking everything decays into a rubber stamp, checking only what the model doubts trusts poorly calibrated confidence, and the defensible design checks by decision class, calibrates on Cornerstone's own children and seeds blind audits. It is also the school's legal accuracy control: India's DPDP Rules spare schools parental consent only for educational and safety processing, with children's-data duties in force from 13 May 2027, and the EU AI Act — which classes learning-outcome evaluation as high-risk and bans emotion inference in schools — is the clearest public specification of "not a black box". At a few hundred children, "self-learning" can honestly mean nightly, auditable updates of each child's state and each question's difficulty, and teacher corrections that score and graduate each prompt; it cannot mean machine-discovered prerequisites or local proof of what works, which the statistics at this size cannot support. The teachers to hire bring diagnostic judgement and the confidence to overrule the machine, not prompting skill.

## Proven AI gains sit on the adult side of the desk

### Design, not model power, decides whether AI helps children

The 2026 offers from the frontier labs are productivity suites for adults with guard-railed chat for students, and none comes with independent evidence of learning. OpenAI's ChatGPT for Teachers is free and adult-only, reached more than 300,000 US educators by August 2026, and its own telemetry shows report cards and lesson planning made up about **1.7 million of 1.9 million "time-saving" messages** ([WinsSolutions](https://www.winssolutions.org/openai-chatgpt-for-teachers-expansion/)). Google's LearnLM was evaluated by expert-rater preference, and its paper reports no student achievement data ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16429)); Khan Academy has reported no Khanmigo efficacy results ([Khan Academy](https://blog.khanacademy.org/khan-academy-efficacy-results-november-2024/)), and the registered J-PAL trial of Khanmigo shows none ([J-PAL](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/initiative-project/ai-powered-tutoring-unleashing-full-potential-personalized-learning-khanmigo)). **No vendor publishes an independent K-12 learning-outcome study of its own school product.**

The causal evidence comes from academic trials of purpose-built tools, and its sign depends on design. In a randomised trial of about 1,000 Turkish high-school students, unrestricted GPT-4 raised practice scores 48% but **cut unassisted exam scores 17%**, while the same model constrained to give hints rather than answers was indistinguishable from control ([PNAS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12232635/)). Harvard's physics tutor, built to "only give away one step at a time" from a supplied answer key, more than doubled learning gains against in-class active learning, though in an elite sample ([Hechinger Report](https://hechingerreport.org/proof-points-ai-tutor-harvard-physics/); [ETC Journal](https://etcjournal.com/2025/11/10/review-of-kestin-et-al-s-june-2025-harvard-study-on-ai-tutoring/)). A World Bank programme in Nigeria gained about 0.3 SD with teacher-curated prompts and teachers watching for hallucinations, and its authors "cannot isolate the effect of the chatbot alone" ([World Bank](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/developmenttalk/addressing-the-learning-crisis-with-generative-ai--lessons-from-)). The strongest result for young children put the AI beside the adult: Tutor CoPilot coached more than 700 human tutors of 1,000 elementary maths pupils in real time and raised topic mastery **4 points overall and 9 points for the lowest-rated tutors**, for about $20 per tutor per year ([Stanford NSSA](https://nssa.stanford.edu/studies/tutor-copilot-human-ai-approach-scaling-real-time-expertise)). The syntheses agree. The OECD's 2026 Digital Education Outlook finds that without pedagogical guidance "outsourcing tasks to GenAI simply enhances performance with no real learning gains" ([OECD via Youthrex](https://youthrex.com/report/oecd-digital-education-outlook-2026-exploring-effective-uses-of-generative-ai-in-education/)), and the Brookings task force concluded in January 2026 that the risks of generative AI in children's education currently "overshadow its benefits" ([Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/a-new-direction-for-students-in-an-ai-world-prosper-prepare-protect/)).

The famous failures share a root: the product led, and pedagogy, evidence, governance or consent followed. None failed because a model was not clever enough. Summit Learning put young children in front of screens for long stretches and met walkouts and parent revolts; research at the time found personalised-learning students "less likely to say there was an adult at school who knew them well" ([Chalkbeat](https://www.chalkbeat.org/2018/12/19/21106372/could-the-forces-that-fought-the-common-core-bring-down-personalized-learning/)). Los Angeles signed a contract worth up to $6 million for one chatbot to serve students, parents and staff; a whistleblower reported that student data went into every prompt, that whole households' records were processed to answer about one child, and that prompts ran on servers in seven countries, and the project shut within three months of launch ([The 74](https://www.the74million.org/article/chatbot-los-angeles-whistleblower-allhere-ai/); [EdWeek](https://www.edweek.org/technology/los-angeles-unifieds-ai-meltdown-5-ways-districts-can-avoid-the-same-mistakes/2024/07)). Alpha School's two hours of daily software supervised by "guides" predicted 65% English and 60% maths proficiency at its Arizona charter and achieved **28% and 10%**; only one of the six states that reviewed it approved a charter ([ProPublica](https://www.propublica.org/article/alpha-school-texas-vouchers-ai)). inBloom lost district after district — one of them after a clean independent security review — because parents objected to sharing children's data "with no attention paid to parental notification or consent" ([EdWeek](https://www.edweek.org/technology/inbloom-to-shut-down-amid-growing-data-privacy-concerns/2014/04)). Byju's sold to parents before it proved learning ([Cornell](https://business.cornell.edu/hub/2024/07/01/what-investors-should-learn-from-fall-edtech-unicorn-byjus/)). Bridge International produced large gains — 0.89 additional years of schooling for primary pupils — with teachers paid about a fifth of public-school pay reading scripted lessons from tablets, and drew lasting charges that it "disempowered" them ([Education Next](https://www.educationnext.org/dramatic-learning-gains-for-mcdonalds-of-african-private-schools-study-by-nobel-economist-finds/); [Tes](https://www.tes.com/news/teachers-disempowered-highly-scripted-lessons-delivered-tablets)). Cornerstone's paper-first design already sidesteps the screen-time revolt, and its rule that prompts receive images, never names, addresses the exact failure that ended the Los Angeles project.

### Compute is nearly free; reading young children is not

The price of a fixed level of AI capability has fallen about **13× a year since 2023** ([Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/publications/the-plunging-price-of-thought)), though reasoning-tier models carry a premium of about 31.5× that is barely falling ([Du](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28576)). At Anthropic's September 2026 batch prices ([Claude pricing](https://claude.com/pricing)), reading 60,000 worksheets a year — 300 children, five sheets a week, 40 weeks, at about 3,000 input and 300 output tokens each — costs roughly $270 on Sonnet 5 and $540 on Opus 5.5 by this report's arithmetic: under a cent a sheet. Small multimodal models now run offline on phones, with an acknowledged loss of analytical depth ([eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/google-pixel-10-offline-ai-gemma-model-2026/)), which makes capture that survives an outage realistic by 2027–28. The binding constraint is accuracy on young children. The FERMAT benchmark found frontier models do better when handwritten maths is replaced by print, even for grades 7–12 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.07244)); the only study of children's handwritten maths expressions found zero-shot performance "inadequate" ([AIED 2024](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-64315-6_28)); and no benchmark covers Nursery–Grade 4 writing in any script. Speech follows the same pattern: the best systems transcribe unscripted adult Hindi at **5.0%** and Marathi at **9.4%** word error ([Voice of India](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19151)), but recognition errors for young children run far higher, up to 35% for English-speaking kindergarteners ([The Learning Agency](https://the-learning-agency.com/guides-resources/closing-the-child-speech-recognition-gap-evidence-limitations-and-paths-forward/)). Cornerstone's own photographs plus teacher corrections are therefore a scarce asset: the only route to a reader tuned to its children, and the source of an accuracy figure no vendor in this research publishes.

### Teachers keep diagnosis, so hire for it

By 2028 the automatable core of teaching is document work — drafting materials, first-pass marking of closed items, transcription, drafting reports and letters, assembling class views — which is what teachers already hand to AI. Reading a child's attention in the room, deciding what one child needs today, motivation, safeguarding, parent relationships and ruling on ambiguous evidence stay human; the evidence for AI there is absent or negative. The "teacher as orchestrator" idea holds only if the system leaves teachers the decisions. A 2026 analysis names the risk "pedagogical deskilling through disuse" when diagnosis and feedback are pre-decided, though it is conceptual rather than measured ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2026.1844085/full)). A 2025 randomised trial in a Turkish private-school chain (193 teachers, 2,800+ students) found achievement unchanged overall but **students of weaker teachers declined**, because those teachers used AI material unrevised while stronger ones treated it as a first draft ([Hechinger Report](https://hechingerreport.org/proof-points-ai-in-teaching/)). In the EEF's ChatGPT trial, teachers rarely refined outputs with further prompts; they took the first draft and edited it by hand ([EEF/NFER](http://www.mmiweb.org.uk/gaiandre/literature/EEF.pdf)). A school system should therefore present finished, checkable artefacts with approve-and-correct controls, never a chat box. Asked what they want from analytics, teachers chose "seeing students' thought processes" over everything else, misconceptions included ([Holstein et al.](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED618924.pdf)) — which is what a photographed page with its working shows.

Hiring follows from that division. The scarce skill is diagnostic judgement plus the willingness to read evidence and overrule the machine, not prompting. Experienced teachers judge children more accurately than novices and use about 5.3 cues per judgement against 2.5, including "deep cues" such as a child's certainty and attentiveness ([Kosel et al.](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1278472/full)), and judgements are more accurate when teachers know exactly what they are judging ([Südkamp et al.](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ993888)). Interview with real anonymised sheets and a proposed prescription: can the candidate find the misconception, catch the machine's misreading and say what the child needs next? Prefer strong maths and early-literacy pedagogy to "AI skills". If Cornerstone affiliates with CBSE, its Grade 3 teachers will also teach the Board's new Computational Thinking and AI curriculum, which "mandates CT + AI + Ethics + Unplugged Learning" for Classes 3–8 from 2026-27 ([CBSE](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/Circulars/2026/18_Circular_2026.pdf)).

Staff the school as a team of three kinds. Class educators make every judgement about a child. Assistant educators run capture, queues, printing and structured practice under the educator's plan, following the EEF rule that assistants "supplement – not replace – the teacher" ([EEF](https://d2tic4wvo1iusb.cloudfront.net/production/eef-guidance-reports/teaching-assistants/deployment_of_teaching_assistants_-_guidance_report_v1.1.0.pdf)) — and this is the tier where Tutor CoPilot shows AI helps adults most. One operations-and-data coordinator owns the system, data hygiene and workload measurement. A five-year Arizona study found no average test-score effect from team-based staffing, but teamed teachers were **about 7 points less likely to leave** against a 27% baseline turnover ([CRPE](https://crpe.org/team-based-teaching-improve-learning-arizona/)), which is worth more than a small score effect to a school adding a grade a year. Two Indian realities sharpen the design. Private-school teachers on long contracts earn about 60% of the average government salary and nearly half work without written contracts ([Newslaundry, citing TISS](https://www.newslaundry.com/2025/09/22/mapping-indias-10-million-teachers-in-six-charts)), so the record, not a departing teacher's memory, must carry the class. And Maharashtra's Marathi Act applies across all boards, with school staff expected to have basic Marathi ([LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/lawschool/articles/maharashtra-government-three-language-policy-critical-analysis-298829)).
