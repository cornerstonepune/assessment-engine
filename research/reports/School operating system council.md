# Own the evidence, amplify the teacher

The evidence supports building Cornerstone's whole-school system as an evidence spine that makes teachers sharper and faster, not as an AI that teaches, marks or judges children on its own. Every credible 2024–26 learning gain from AI came from narrow, answer-key-grounded tools with adults in charge — real-time AI coaching of human tutors raised mastery by 9 percentage points among the weakest tutors' pupils — while unrestricted chat cut unassisted exam scores by 17%, and the screen-first and "guides instead of teachers" models that scaled lost parents' trust, closed, or fell far short on public state tests. No product surveyed, global or Indian, keeps a longitudinal per-child skill-and-evidence record at its core, so that record is the thing to build; models, speech recognition and commodity operations are the things to buy, behind one-file adapters and export clauses. Returns will be real but modest: good formative systems move outcomes about 0.2–0.3 standard deviations, mostly for the weakest children, and the only high-security trial of AI lesson planning saved 25 minutes a week, so teacher hours come back only when a task is retired — code marking computable answers, data entered once, reports and parent notes drafted from evidence — and only if the review queue does not consume them. That queue is the load-bearing part: checking everything decays into a rubber stamp, checking only what the model doubts trusts poorly calibrated confidence, and the defensible design checks by decision class, calibrates on Cornerstone's own children and seeds blind audits. It is also the school's legal accuracy control: India's DPDP Rules spare schools parental consent only for educational and safety processing, with children's-data duties in force from 13 May 2027, and the EU AI Act — which classes learning-outcome evaluation as high-risk and bans emotion inference in schools — is the clearest public specification of "not a black box". At a few hundred children, "self-learning" can honestly mean nightly, auditable updates of each child's state and each question's difficulty, and teacher corrections that score and graduate each prompt; it cannot mean machine-discovered prerequisites or local proof of what works, which the statistics at this size cannot support. The teachers to hire bring diagnostic judgement and the confidence to overrule the machine, not prompting skill.

## Proven AI gains sit on the adult side of the desk

### Design, not model power, decides whether AI helps children

The 2026 offers from the frontier labs are productivity suites for adults with guard-railed chat for students, and none comes with independent evidence of learning. OpenAI's ChatGPT for Teachers is free and adult-only, reached more than 300,000 US educators by August 2026, and its own telemetry shows report cards and lesson planning made up about **1.7 million of 1.9 million "time-saving" messages** ([WinsSolutions](https://www.winssolutions.org/openai-chatgpt-for-teachers-expansion/)). Google's LearnLM was evaluated by expert-rater preference, and its paper reports no student achievement data ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16429)); Khan Academy has reported no Khanmigo efficacy results ([Khan Academy](https://blog.khanacademy.org/khan-academy-efficacy-results-november-2024/)), and the registered J-PAL trial of Khanmigo shows none ([J-PAL](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/initiative-project/ai-powered-tutoring-unleashing-full-potential-personalized-learning-khanmigo)). **No vendor publishes an independent K-12 learning-outcome study of its own school product.**

The causal evidence comes from academic trials of purpose-built tools, and its sign depends on design. In a randomised trial of about 1,000 Turkish high-school students, unrestricted GPT-4 raised practice scores 48% but **cut unassisted exam scores 17%**, while the same model constrained to give hints rather than answers was indistinguishable from control ([PNAS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12232635/)). Harvard's physics tutor, built to "only give away one step at a time" from a supplied answer key, more than doubled learning gains against in-class active learning, though in an elite sample ([Hechinger Report](https://hechingerreport.org/proof-points-ai-tutor-harvard-physics/); [ETC Journal](https://etcjournal.com/2025/11/10/review-of-kestin-et-al-s-june-2025-harvard-study-on-ai-tutoring/)). A World Bank programme in Nigeria gained about 0.3 SD with teacher-curated prompts and teachers watching for hallucinations, and its authors "cannot isolate the effect of the chatbot alone" ([World Bank](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/developmenttalk/addressing-the-learning-crisis-with-generative-ai--lessons-from-)). The strongest result for young children put the AI beside the adult: Tutor CoPilot coached more than 700 human tutors of 1,000 elementary maths pupils in real time and raised topic mastery **4 points overall and 9 points for the lowest-rated tutors**, for about $20 per tutor per year ([Stanford NSSA](https://nssa.stanford.edu/studies/tutor-copilot-human-ai-approach-scaling-real-time-expertise)). The syntheses agree. The OECD's 2026 Digital Education Outlook finds that without pedagogical guidance "outsourcing tasks to GenAI simply enhances performance with no real learning gains" ([OECD via Youthrex](https://youthrex.com/report/oecd-digital-education-outlook-2026-exploring-effective-uses-of-generative-ai-in-education/)), and the Brookings task force concluded in January 2026 that the risks of generative AI in children's education currently "overshadow its benefits" ([Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/a-new-direction-for-students-in-an-ai-world-prosper-prepare-protect/)).

The famous failures share a root: the product led, and pedagogy, evidence, governance or consent followed. None failed because a model was not clever enough. Summit Learning put young children in front of screens for long stretches and met walkouts and parent revolts; research at the time found personalised-learning students "less likely to say there was an adult at school who knew them well" ([Chalkbeat](https://www.chalkbeat.org/2018/12/19/21106372/could-the-forces-that-fought-the-common-core-bring-down-personalized-learning/)). Los Angeles signed a contract worth up to $6 million for one chatbot to serve students, parents and staff; a whistleblower reported that student data went into every prompt, that whole households' records were processed to answer about one child, and that prompts ran on servers in seven countries, and the project shut within three months of launch ([The 74](https://www.the74million.org/article/chatbot-los-angeles-whistleblower-allhere-ai/); [EdWeek](https://www.edweek.org/technology/los-angeles-unifieds-ai-meltdown-5-ways-districts-can-avoid-the-same-mistakes/2024/07)). Alpha School's two hours of daily software supervised by "guides" predicted 65% English and 60% maths proficiency at its Arizona charter and achieved **28% and 10%**; only one of the six states that reviewed it approved a charter ([ProPublica](https://www.propublica.org/article/alpha-school-texas-vouchers-ai)). inBloom lost district after district — one of them after a clean independent security review — because parents objected to sharing children's data "with no attention paid to parental notification or consent" ([EdWeek](https://www.edweek.org/technology/inbloom-to-shut-down-amid-growing-data-privacy-concerns/2014/04)). Byju's sold to parents before it proved learning ([Cornell](https://business.cornell.edu/hub/2024/07/01/what-investors-should-learn-from-fall-edtech-unicorn-byjus/)). Bridge International produced large gains — 0.89 additional years of schooling for primary pupils — with teachers paid about a fifth of public-school pay reading scripted lessons from tablets, and drew lasting charges that it "disempowered" them ([Education Next](https://www.educationnext.org/dramatic-learning-gains-for-mcdonalds-of-african-private-schools-study-by-nobel-economist-finds/); [Tes](https://www.tes.com/news/teachers-disempowered-highly-scripted-lessons-delivered-tablets)). Cornerstone's paper-first design already sidesteps the screen-time revolt, and its rule that prompts receive images, never names, addresses the exact failure that ended the Los Angeles project.

### Compute is nearly free; reading young children is not

The price of a fixed level of AI capability has fallen about **13× a year since 2023** ([Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/publications/the-plunging-price-of-thought)), though reasoning-tier models carry a premium of about 31.5× that is barely falling ([Du](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28576)). At Anthropic's September 2026 batch prices ([Claude pricing](https://claude.com/pricing)), reading 60,000 worksheets a year — 300 children, five sheets a week, 40 weeks, at about 3,000 input and 300 output tokens each — costs roughly $270 on Sonnet 5 and $540 on Opus 5.5 by this report's arithmetic: under a cent a sheet. Small multimodal models now run offline on phones, with an acknowledged loss of analytical depth ([eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/google-pixel-10-offline-ai-gemma-model-2026/)), which makes capture that survives an outage realistic by 2027–28. The binding constraint is accuracy on young children. The FERMAT benchmark found frontier models do better when handwritten maths is replaced by print, even for grades 7–12 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.07244)); the only study of children's handwritten maths expressions found zero-shot performance "inadequate" ([AIED 2024](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-64315-6_28)); and no benchmark covers Nursery–Grade 4 writing in any script. Speech follows the same pattern: the best systems transcribe unscripted adult Hindi at **5.0%** and Marathi at **9.4%** word error ([Voice of India](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19151)), but recognition errors for young children run far higher, up to 35% for English-speaking kindergarteners ([The Learning Agency](https://the-learning-agency.com/guides-resources/closing-the-child-speech-recognition-gap-evidence-limitations-and-paths-forward/)). Cornerstone's own photographs plus teacher corrections are therefore a scarce asset: the only route to a reader tuned to its children, and the source of an accuracy figure no vendor in this research publishes.

### Teachers keep diagnosis, so hire for it

By 2028 the automatable core of teaching is document work — drafting materials, first-pass marking of closed items, transcription, drafting reports and letters, assembling class views — which is what teachers already hand to AI. Reading a child's attention in the room, deciding what one child needs today, motivation, safeguarding, parent relationships and ruling on ambiguous evidence stay human; the evidence for AI there is absent or negative. The "teacher as orchestrator" idea holds only if the system leaves teachers the decisions. A 2026 analysis names the risk "pedagogical deskilling through disuse" when diagnosis and feedback are pre-decided, though it is conceptual rather than measured ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2026.1844085/full)). A 2025 randomised trial in a Turkish private-school chain (193 teachers, 2,800+ students) found achievement unchanged overall but **students of weaker teachers declined**, because those teachers used AI material unrevised while stronger ones treated it as a first draft ([Hechinger Report](https://hechingerreport.org/proof-points-ai-in-teaching/)). In the EEF's ChatGPT trial, teachers rarely refined outputs with further prompts; they took the first draft and edited it by hand ([EEF/NFER](http://www.mmiweb.org.uk/gaiandre/literature/EEF.pdf)). A school system should therefore present finished, checkable artefacts with approve-and-correct controls, never a chat box. Asked what they want from analytics, teachers chose "seeing students' thought processes" over everything else, misconceptions included ([Holstein et al.](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED618924.pdf)) — which is what a photographed page with its working shows.

Hiring follows from that division. The scarce skill is diagnostic judgement plus the willingness to read evidence and overrule the machine, not prompting. Experienced teachers judge children more accurately than novices and use about 5.3 cues per judgement against 2.5, including "deep cues" such as a child's certainty and attentiveness ([Kosel et al.](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1278472/full)), and judgements are more accurate when teachers know exactly what they are judging ([Südkamp et al.](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ993888)). Interview with real anonymised sheets and a proposed prescription: can the candidate find the misconception, catch the machine's misreading and say what the child needs next? Prefer strong maths and early-literacy pedagogy to "AI skills". If Cornerstone affiliates with CBSE, its Grade 3 teachers will also teach the Board's new Computational Thinking and AI curriculum, which "mandates CT + AI + Ethics + Unplugged Learning" for Classes 3–8 from 2026-27 ([CBSE](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/Circulars/2026/18_Circular_2026.pdf)).

Staff the school as a team of three kinds. Class educators make every judgement about a child. Assistant educators run capture, queues, printing and structured practice under the educator's plan, following the EEF rule that assistants "supplement – not replace – the teacher" ([EEF](https://d2tic4wvo1iusb.cloudfront.net/production/eef-guidance-reports/teaching-assistants/deployment_of_teaching_assistants_-_guidance_report_v1.1.0.pdf)) — and this is the tier where Tutor CoPilot shows AI helps adults most. One operations-and-data coordinator owns the system, data hygiene and workload measurement. A five-year Arizona study found no average test-score effect from team-based staffing, but teamed teachers were **about 7 points less likely to leave** against a 27% baseline turnover ([CRPE](https://crpe.org/team-based-teaching-improve-learning-arizona/)), which is worth more than a small score effect to a school adding a grade a year. Two Indian realities sharpen the design. Private-school teachers on long contracts earn about 60% of the average government salary and nearly half work without written contracts ([Newslaundry, citing TISS](https://www.newslaundry.com/2025/09/22/mapping-indias-10-million-teachers-in-six-charts)), so the record, not a departing teacher's memory, must carry the class. And Maharashtra's Marathi Act applies across all boards, with school staff expected to have basic Marathi ([LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/lawschool/articles/maharashtra-government-three-language-policy-critical-analysis-298829)).

## No product holds a per-child evidence record, so borrow the parts and build the spine

Across the school platforms surveyed — PowerSchool, Infinite Campus, Veracross, Arbor, SIMS, Compass, Toddle, Teachmint, LEAD, Entab, Fedena and Kriyo — **none keeps a longitudinal per-child skill state as its core record**. The closest are standards tags on portfolios and AI-drafted reports, as in the India-born IB platform Toddle, which also advertises that its AI "takes the first pass at grading student work" without stating how that pass is checked ([Toddle](https://www.toddleapp.com/ib-pyp/)). Operations are solved many times over; the evidence record is not. The strategy the evidence supports is to build the spine and borrow proven parts, domain by domain.

### Curriculum: code the spine finely and treat prerequisites as hypotheses

Every framework used for reporting or external assessment has a coded, stage-bounded statement as its unit, and frameworks that resisted coding later bolted it on: Te Whāriki cut its learning outcomes from 118 to 20 ([McLachlan](https://www.hekupu.ac.nz/article/te-whariki-revisited-how-approaches-assessment-can-make-valued-learning-visible)), and the IB replaced its PYP scope-and-sequence documents with phased subject continuums in 2025 ([ManageBac](https://www.managebac.com/blog/ib-pyp-updates-strands-scope-sequence)). India's NCF gives Cornerstone a ready three-tier spine — Curricular Goal, Competency, Learning Outcome — and says teachers "should have the autonomy to define Learning Outcomes" ([NCF-FS 2022](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCF_for_Foundational_Stage_20_October_2022.pdf)). The competency layer is too coarse to locate a child: Foundational maths is one curricular goal with **13 competencies spanning five years**, while Cambridge's earlier primary framework carries roughly 55 coded Stage 1 maths objectives alone, each a small step such as "1Nn3 Count objects up to 20, recognising conservation of number" ([Cambridge framework](https://chartwell.edu.rs/images/Primary_Maths_Curriculum_Framework.pdf)). NCF codes should therefore be the grouping and reporting nodes, with school-defined small-step outcomes beneath them. For ladders, Australia's numeracy progression is the model: each sub-element has "as many levels … as can be supported by evidence", children "may straddle two or more levels", and it "has not been designed as a checklist" ([ACARA](https://education.nsw.gov.au/content/dam/main-education/en/home/teaching-and-learning/curriculum/literacy-and-numeracy/resources-for-schools/lnlp/national-numeracy-learning-progression-v3-for-publication.pdf)). New Zealand's PaCT shows how teacher judgements become a Rasch-scaled range rather than a point ([PaCT](https://assets.curriculumprogresstools.education.govt.nz/public/Uploads/info_about_PaCT.pdf)).

Prerequisite order has experimental support within a strand: kindergartners taught length activities in trajectory order beat peers taught the same activities in reverse (n = 189), and level-by-level teaching beat skipping to the target (g ≈ 0.55) ([Sarama et al.](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1323924.pdf); [Clements et al.](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1234710.pdf)). Cross-strand links everywhere, including the Common Core Coherence Map, are expert assertions ([Achieve the Core](https://achievethecore.org/page/2801/learn-more-about-the-coherence-map)), so each "precedes" edge should carry its provenance — expert, trialled or validated. Borrow documentation practice from Reggio Emilia and the concrete–pictorial–abstract toolkit from Singapore, but not as structure: Reggio has "little empirical research directly measuring the impact" ([The Education Hub](https://theeducationhub.org.nz/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/The-Reggio-Emilia-approach.pdf)), and Singapore's own researchers find the CPA sequence "not linear and highly fluid" in practice ([Chang, Lee & Koay](https://math.nie.edu.sg/ame/matheduc/tme/tmeV17_1/paper1.pdf)).

For generated pedagogy, Oak National Academy is the one operator that publishes its machinery: a fixed lesson schema, a 9,000-word prompt, retrieval from its own quality-assured corpus first, and teachers who "must verify outputs for accuracy" ([Aila FAQs](https://support.thenational.academy/aila-faqs)), plus an auto-evaluator whose agreement with expert teachers rose only **from 0.17 to 0.32** (quadratic weighted kappa) after fixes ([Oak](https://www.thenational.academy/blog/how-do-we-ensure-our-ai-generated-resources-are-high-quality)). Expect the same: an evaluator triages, and human approve-and-lock is the quality mechanism. Avoid AI documents about individual children: in a test of four leading teacher assistants, identical behaviour-plan prompts produced more punitive plans for Black-coded names ([Chalkbeat](https://www.chalkbeat.org/2025/08/06/ai-teacher-assistants-promote-racial-bias-study-finds/)), and Common Sense Media warns that "professional-looking output may mask errors" ([Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/sites/default/files/featured-content/files/csm-ai-risk-assessment-ai-teacher-assistants-final.pdf)). And budget for implementation as well as design. Pratham's Teaching at the Right Level — five coarse levels plus disciplined regrouping — produced gains from 0.15 to 0.70 SD where dedicated time and mentoring existed, and was not adopted where teachers were only trained ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22746)); mastery learning's average +5 months shrank to +1 when schools did not actually hold topics until children were proficient ([EEF Toolkit 2018](https://dera.ioe.ac.uk/id/eprint/33734/1/EEF-Teaching-Learning-Toolkit-October-2018.pdf)).

### Assessment: short, repeated, named — and never one number

The large interim tests (MAP Growth, Star, i-Ready) reach whole-test reliabilities of 0.90–0.98, but sub-skill scores fall as low as 0.75 ([NWEA](https://www.nwea.org/uploads/MAP-Growth-Technical-Report-2025.pdf)), and their kindergarten evidence is their weakest: i-Ready's kindergarten classification is rated only "partially convincing", and Star's kindergarten alternate-form reliability is 0.71 ([NCII](https://charts.intensiveintervention.org/screening/tool/?id=7534542c4f422f85); [Star manual](https://flfast.org/content/contentresources/en/SMRPTechnicalManual_FL.pdf)). They also take 25–60 minutes of a child's attention per subject. They are the wrong tool under age 8. The format the field converged on for 4–7-year-olds is the one-minute, one-to-one oral probe (DIBELS, ASER), but even that drifts — kindergarten phonemic segmentation re-correlates at only 0.49 after a delay ([DIBELS manual](https://dibels.uoregon.edu/sites/default/files/DIBELS8-TechnicalManual_04152020.pdf)) — so no single sitting should decide anything.

Eedi's discipline of naming every wrong answer is the one to borrow. Its misconception graph maps more than 8,000 misconceptions from 200 million responses under a CC BY 4.0 licence ([Eedi](https://www.eedi.com/news/eedis-misconception-graph-is-now-a-public-good)), and a two-year RCT found d = 0.30 at 24 months, though in Years 7–8 and with selective attrition ([Eedi RCT](https://www.eedi.com/news/two-year-rct-earns-eedi-a-gold-efficacy-certificate-from-eduevidence)). Eedi's own 2026 research also shows the limit: children reach correct answers through flawed reasoning, and detectors that catch 57–84% of such cases raise **four to eight false alarms for every genuine detection** at realistic prevalence ([arXiv 2606.23205](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23205); [arXiv 2605.23925](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23925)). That is the evidence behind Cornerstone's three signals — blank, wrong, and wrong-with-working — and behind routing a suspected misconception to a follow-up item rather than a verdict. India's strongest evidence comes from Educational Initiatives' Mindspark: **0.37 SD in maths in 4.5 months** in Delhi, with 45 minutes of software plus 45 minutes of small-group teaching per session ([AER](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Faer.20171112)), and 0.22 SD after 18 months across Rajasthan government schools, with gains proportional to time on the platform ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w34205/revisions/w34205.rev0.pdf)).

For paper, copy Gradescope's rule that every AI-suggested answer group is confirmed by an instructor ([Apporto](https://www.apporto.com/gradescopes-ai-assisted-grading-explained)) and Remark's treatment of cross-outs and multiple marks as first-class exceptions ([Remark](https://remarksoftware.com/products/office-omr/)), which argues for a "struck out and rewritten" state beside the three signals. **No product publishes handwriting accuracy for children**, and Indian vendors claim 95–98% on handwritten sheets without stating a method ([Eklavvya](https://www.eklavvya.com/ai-answersheet-evaluation-school/); [ConveGenius](https://convegenius.com/plugging-learning-gaps-at-scale.html)). For reporting, ACER keeps one scale per learning area and states that reading and maths units are not comparable ([ACER](https://schoolsupport.acer.org/hc/en-au/articles/22255011145241-Scale-scores-and-achievement-bands)), and growth percentiles need roughly 5,000 students ([Castellano & Ho](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED551292.pdf)), so a small school should report movement between described bands on each subject's own ladder. Make mastery a counted, strict and visible rule. "N consecutive correct" performs as well as Bayesian thresholds when observations are clean ([Pelánek](https://www.fi.muni.cz/~xpelanek/publications/mastery-modeling.pdf)), and code-verified answers are clean; field data favour a 0.98 mastery threshold over the customary 0.95 ([EDM 2025](https://educationaldatamining.org/EDM2025/proceedings/2025.EDM.short-papers.4/index.html)); and in Cognitive Tutor logs, students whom teachers reassigned outside the software's mastery process scored lower on post-tests ([Israni et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.08616)). Promise what formative assessment delivers — a weighted mean of about **0.20 SD**, not the often-quoted 0.4–0.7 ([Kingston & Nash](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ951173)) — and never a single composite grade, which a century of research finds unreliable and multidimensional ([Guskey](https://tguskey.com/wp-content/uploads/Grading-3-Five-Obstacles-to-Grading-Reform.pdf)).

### Observation: fewer, specific notes confirmed by more than one adult

The observation record has been stable for a decade: media plus a descriptive note, tagged to children and optionally to framework statements, with a staff-only flag ([Tapestry](https://tapestry.info/features/online-learning-journals/); [brightwheel](https://help.mybrightwheel.com/en/articles/11899456-log-observations-to-assess-student-development)). Storypark's AI fact sheet is the disclosure model: it names OpenAI and Anthropic, US hosting and 30-day partner retention, and says it sends a child's first name, age, gender and past stories but never images ([Storypark](https://help.storypark.com/en/articles/10114616-storypark-assist-fact-sheet)). Cornerstone's images-not-names rule is stricter and should stay. England's 2021 early-years reform is the burden lesson: once practitioners no longer had to prove judgements with physical evidence, **82–89% of settings** moved time from observation and tracking to children, at the documented cost that progress for children with special needs became harder to plot against broad goals ([DfE evaluation](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6537e5503099f900117f308f/EYFS_2021_Reforms_Evaluation_Report.pdf)). Keep fine-grained nodes available for children who need them without demanding that grain for everyone.

Structured, repeated teacher judgement is valid: Work Sampling ratings made three times a year correlated 0.50–0.75 with standardised tests and predicted them better than demographics did ([Erikson Institute](https://www.erikson.edu/wp-content/uploads/AERA-FCD-WSS-summary.pdf)), and across 122 studies teacher judgement correlates about 0.65 with measured attainment ([Kaufmann](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11271880/)). But much of the variance in teacher-administered ratings belongs to the teacher, not the child ([Waterman et al.](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ947506)), and a 3–10-minute Learning Story captures roughly a thousandth of a child's day ([Blaiklock](https://oece.nz/wp-content/uploads/Blaiklock_2008.pdf)). The fix is collaborative: several observations from more than one familiar adult ([Wanoa & Johnston](https://ojs.victoria.ac.nz/nzaroe/article/download/6333/5496/8635)), confirmed as specific statements ("counted seven objects with one-to-one correspondence") rather than global levels. Avoid quotas — New Zealand's informal "one story per child per month" became a stressor ([Niles](https://www.hekupu.ac.nz/article/assessment-practices-within-new-zealand-early-childhood-settings)) — and avoid ClassDojo-style behaviour points, which critics describe as turning children into "balance sheets of behaviour" ([EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/732702)).

Voice capture is ahead of the evidence. TeachScribe, the one voice-first early-years product, claims teachers are 15 times more likely to record a voice observation than a written one but publishes no accuracy figure ([TeachScribe](https://teachscribe.com/eyfs-observation-software/)). For teachers speaking Marathi, Hindi and English, Sarvam and Gemini 3 Pro lead the one large independent benchmark, AI4Bharat's MIT-licensed IndicConformer (Hindi 8.2%, Marathi 13.1% word error) is the self-hostable fallback, OpenAI's GPT-4o Transcribe fails on Marathi at 55.6% ([Voice of India](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.19151)), and the government's Bhashini API is licensed for proofs of concept only ([Bhashini](https://bhashini.gitbook.io/bhashini-apis)). Keep the choice in one adapter file, transcribe in a code-mixed mode, keep the audio for playback at confirmation, and score the pipeline on whether it gets children's names right, since a misattributed observation is the worst failure.

### Operations: buy the commodity layer only on export terms

Attendance, fees, transport, notices and timetabling are commodities; the risks are lock-in, vendor failure and security. PowerSchool lost records for a reported 62 million students through a single compromised support-portal credential with no multi-factor authentication ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/10/what-powerschool-isnt-saying-about-its-massive-student-data-breach/)). Indian ERPs publish no prices and, apart from Fedena's advertised API, no API documentation ([Fedena](https://fedena.com/)); LEAD's integrated system advertises "79% learning mastery" with no stated method ([LEAD](https://leadgroup.co.in/)); Teachmint now leads with classroom hardware ([Teachmint](https://www.teachmint.com/)). Cornerstone's own incumbent needs checking: Kriyo's App Store listing shows an active product used by 5,700+ schools ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/in/app/kriyo-preschool-daycare-app/id1384125107)), but kriyo.com now redirects to an unrelated site ([kriyo.com](https://www.kriyo.com/)) — reason enough to make scheduled child-level export a condition of renewal now. Timetable generation is solved by the free FET solver, which handles simple timetables in minutes ([FET](https://lalescu.ro/liviu/fet/)); what commercial tools charge for is daily cover and publishing changes ([aSc](https://www.asctimetables.com/)), which a trigger-sequence-notify flow can do.

Parent communication has the clearest causal evidence of any operational task. Weekly one-sentence, improvement-focused teacher messages cut the share of students failing to earn course credit **from 15.8% to 9.3%**, with teachers spending under 30 minutes a week on them ([Kraft & Rogers](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6297c2b5c8bc35721cc7a65c/t/685d1b50a1fe313d1d091445/1750932305165/Kraft+Rogers+2015+The+underutilized+potential+of+teacher-to-parent+communication+EER.pdf)), and automated alerts on missed work and absences cut course failures 38% ([Bergman & Chan](https://ideas.repec.org/p/ces/ceswps/_6493.html)). Content, not channel, did the work: send few, specific, per-child messages through the WhatsApp parents already use rather than another feed; Indian school WhatsApp groups already bury teachers in late-night demands ([CBGA](https://www.cbgaindia.org/blog/whatsapp-school-opportunities-challenges/)). The government stack is optional — DIKSHA's Sunbird base is a set of open building blocks ([Sunbird](https://www.sunbird.org/)) — and the school's duty there is to carry each child's UDISE+ PEN and, where parents consent, the APAAR ID generated from it ([APAAR](https://apaar.education.gov.in/)).

### Learner modelling: simple, calibrated models win at school scale

Across nine datasets, well-featured logistic regression led in low- and medium-data settings, deep knowledge tracing only in the largest, and classic Bayesian knowledge tracing lagged ([Gervet et al.](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1273917.pdf)); with forgetting added, BKT matched or beat deep knowledge tracing on the data where the deep model first looked far superior ([Khajah et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.02416)). The largest primary-maths practice system, Math Garden (now Prowise Learn), runs on Elo ratings for more than 400,000 Dutch children, and had to switch one task to a fixed item order because adaptive selection was rewarding a wrong strategy ([Brinkhuis et al.](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1187391.pdf)). ALEKS locates a learner in a 350-concept algebra domain with 25–30 questions ([ALEKS](https://www.aleks.com/about_aleks/knowledge_space_theory)). The effects are human-shaped. Intelligent tutors compared with regular K-12 maths teaching show g = 0.01–0.09 ([Steenbergen-Hu & Cooper](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1054449)), and every trial with a credible standardised-test gain — ASSISTments 0.18 SD and Cognitive Tutor 0.20 SD in its second year only ([Escueta et al.](https://oreopoulos.faculty.economics.utoronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/escueta-et-al-education-technology-nber-wp-23744-2017.pdf)), Mindspark 0.37 SD ([AER](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Faer.20171112)) — had teachers or instructors structurally in the loop, with none isolating the software's share. Ryan Baker's reading of the field is the one to adopt: data mining should drive human decisions, "intelligence amplification rather than artificial intelligence" ([Baker](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1096875)).

|---|---|---|---|

## Evidence changes shape by subject and stage, and tests wait until Grade 3

NEP 2020's 5+3+3+4 design places Cornerstone's Nursery–Grade 4 across two stages: Foundational (ages 3–8, three pre-school years plus Grades 1–2) and Preparatory (Grades 3–5), with Middle (6–8) and Secondary (9–10) ahead ([NEP 2020](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/nep/NEP_2020.pdf)). For the first stage the framework leaves no room: "Explicit tests and examinations are completely inappropriate assessment tools for this Stage"; evidence comes from observing children and analysing their artefacts, worksheets included, with the teacher choosing the tools and the periodicity, and a Holistic Progress Card kept every year ([NCF-FS 2022](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCF_for_Foundational_Stage_20_October_2022.pdf)). From Grade 3, "a variety of formative oral and written assessments" join observation, with a year-end readiness summative; the Middle Stage adds MCQ and constructed-response tests, practical tests and projects; the Secondary Stage adds open-book tests and board examinations offered more than once a year ([NCF-SE 2023](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf)). The design consequence is one evidence spine — child, competency, a dated append-only event, a human confirmation — whose event types change by subject and stage: machine-read QR sheets, audio, photographs, short video, observation checklists, rubric judgements, comparative-judgement positions, and the child's and parent's own words. A Foundational worksheet is an artefact, never a test score.

### Reading and writing: machines pre-score, teachers decide

Reading evidence at these ages is oral, one-to-one and short. ASER's four levels — letters, words, a Grade 1 text, a Grade 2 story — are the same across 19 languages and unchanged since 2006 ([ASER 2024](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/ASER_2024_Final-Report_13_2_24-1.pdf)); England's phonics check has children read 40 words, half of them pseudo-words, to an adult they know, with the pass mark published only afterwards ([DfE](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/phonics-screening-check-assessment-and-reporting-arrangements-ara/2025-phonics-screening-check-assessment-and-reporting-arrangements)). Machine listening is good in aggregate and weak in detail: automated words-correct-per-minute correlated **0.96** with expert scores for Ghanaian children reading English ([Henkel et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17606)), but word-level miscue detection reached a precision of only **0.52** in Dutch primary schools ([Gao et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07060)). Indian deployments are large and unmeasured: Wadhwani AI's oral-reading tool has logged 34 million assessments in Gujarat and Rajasthan and IIT Bombay's TARA reached 7 lakh Kendriya Vidyalaya students, but neither publishes word-level accuracy ([Wadhwani AI](https://www.wadhwaniai.org/programs/oral-reading-fluency/); [IIT Bombay](https://rnd.iitb.ac.in/tara_mobile_app)). The one Indian child benchmark found — 10–14-year-olds speaking code-switched Hindi and English in classrooms — put Whisper at 18% word error ([HiACC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12329218/)), and Microsoft's own staff describe its reading auto-detection as "not a substitute for human evaluation" ([Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/1407296/validity-and-authenticity-of-auto-detect)). So keep audio as evidence, let speech recognition pre-fill fluency rates and propose miscues only after a local evaluation in each language, and keep the teacher's ear on anything that changes teaching or a report. Running records need eight to ten reads for a reliable accuracy score ([D'Agostino et al.](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1290680&q=running+records)), so use them repeatedly and only for children below benchmark. The ladder also needs rungs well below grade level: in rural Maharashtra only **37.1% of Std III children read a Std II text** and 31.3% can do two-digit subtraction with regrouping, and private schools now do no better than government ones (37.5% against 37.0%) ([ASER Maharashtra](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/Maharashtra-1.pdf)).

In the Foundational Stage, writing develops "through drawing, labelling, inventive spelling" ([NCF-FS](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/focus-group/NCF-FS_2022EN.pdf)), so the photograph is the evidence and the teacher calls the developmental stage. Transcription is the wrong goal: OCR on the handwriting of children flagged as possibly dysgraphic managed about 43.5% character accuracy ([Vydeki et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13595)). From Grade 3, comparative judgement — teachers repeatedly choosing the better of two scripts — builds a school-wide writing scale without a rubric fight, and it is collaborative by construction. No More Marking's AI judges agreed with teachers **81–82% of the time, against 87% between teachers**, with a split of 90% AI and 10% human judgements; the largest disagreements came from teachers swayed by handwriting ([No More Marking](https://substack.nomoremarking.com/p/so-can-ai-assess-writing)). That evidence comes from UK children's English, so Cornerstone would need its own teacher-versus-teacher baseline before AI judges Marathi, Hindi or Indian children's English. Automated writing feedback averages g = 0.55, but its meta-analysis contains **no primary-school studies** ([Fleckenstein et al.](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/artificial-intelligence/articles/10.3389/frai.2023.1162454/full)), which places it in Grades 6–10 alongside CBSE rubrics "developed and shared with students in advance" ([CBSE 2026-27](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/CurriculumMain27/SecPart1/Curriculum_SecP1_2026-27.pdf)).

### Science, social science and the arts: concepts on paper, process by observation

NCF-SE assesses The World Around Us and science for concepts plus process — observing, hypothesising, testing, classifying — and social science for reasoning with evidence, insisting that current-affairs work "must never be only on the basis of general knowledge" ([NCF-SE 2023](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf)). Concepts travel on paper: short answers, labelled diagrams, tables, maps and caselets fit the existing QR-sheet pipeline, marked against a rubric of acceptable ideas rather than one computed value, so teachers confirm more often than in arithmetic. Process does not travel: designing a fair test, measuring, handling materials safely and contributing to group inquiry are observation-checklist events, with a photograph as context rather than proof. From Grade 9 these become CBSE internal assessment, in which periodic tests, "multiple assessment", an HPC-aligned portfolio and subject enrichment each count for a quarter of the 20 internal marks ([CBSE 2026-27](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/CurriculumMain27/SecPart1/Curriculum_SecP1_2026-27.pdf)). Art evidence is process in a dated portfolio with the child's own reflection; NCF-SE starts from "the fundamental belief that all students can be creative" and introduces a scored rubric only in a Grade 7 music example ([NCF-SE 2023](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf)). A score on a young child's picture measures fine-motor maturity and invites the comparison the HPC forbids. AI can date, caption and transcribe a child's spoken reflection; it should never judge aesthetic quality.

### Physical education: observe movement, never grade bodies

PE is where defensible and harmful measurement sit furthest apart. NCF-SE wants children "assessed primarily through demonstrated performance … against clear criteria for marking", with written tests only for knowledge; its Grade 5 example has a teacher following one player at a time for about four minutes of a 30-minute dodgeball game ([NCF-SE 2023](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf)). The defensible measure from age 3 to 10 is observed movement competence: the TGMD-3 scores 13 fundamental skills — run, hop, gallop, throw, catch and others — as criteria present or absent ([Carballo-Fazanes et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7915997/)). Its reliability study found no difference between live and slow-motion video scoring, but agreement between expert and novice raters was only poor to good, so a short clip that a second teacher moderates is the single best reliability fix. The need is real in India: 71% of 19,797 children aged 7–10 could not throw and catch properly in a 2010–11 survey ([EduSports](https://edusports.sportzvillage.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/EduSports_2nd_Annual_Health_Survey.pdf)), and only 34.4% of 141,840 children met aerobic standards in 2025 data ([Outlook India](https://www.outlookindia.com/healthcare-spotlight/two-thirds-of-schoolchildren-lack-basic-aerobic-fitness-a-survey-finds)). From about age 8, a CAPL-2-style physical-literacy profile adds confidence, motivation, knowledge and daily activity to competence, and was validated in 2026 on 846 children aged 8–11 ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2026.1922697/full)), reported as goals against the child's own earlier profile.

BMI reporting is the harm to avoid. A California cluster trial covering about 30,000 pupils found that BMI letters to families "did not reduce obesity rates" and increased body dissatisfaction ([UC Berkeley](https://publichealth.berkeley.edu/news-media/research-highlights/study-bmi-reports-dont-improve-student-health)); Maryland bars fitness scores for grading, placement or "assessing students in third grade and below" ([MSDE](https://www.marylandpublicschools.org/about/Documents/DCAA/PE/HealthRelatedFitnessTesting.pdf)); California no longer requires schools to collect height or weight ([CDE](https://www.cde.ca.gov/ta/tg/pf/)). India's Khelo India app points the other way — BMI, a balance test and plate tapping twice a year from age 5, feeding a national fitness index and talent identification ([CBSE circular](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/Circulars/2019/83_Circular_2019.pdf)) — so if it is required, run it as a separate, consented health screen outside the learning record. Timings, distances and counts are code, not AI. Pose estimation agreed with human raters 66–88% of the time on locomotor skills in 3–8-year-olds ([Chow et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7429431)), reached an ICC of 0.96 on a single skill only under controlled filming ([Makaruk et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12358364/)), and a scoping review found its reliability "rarely reported" and its ethics undiscussed ([F1000Research](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13248934/)): it is a moderation aid at most, with video kept short-lived and purpose-limited.

### Socio-emotional learning: observe, never monitor

The line is between observation — episodic, purposeful, recorded by a person, visible to the child and parents, and used to change provision — and monitoring, which is continuous, automated, inferential and used to rate the child. The EU has banned AI emotion inference in education since February 2025 ([EU AI Act](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/5/)), and India's DPDP Act bars behavioural monitoring of children outside its educational and safety exemption. Leuven-style two-minute scans, three or four times a year, check which activities engage children rather than scoring a child ([Laevers](https://www.gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=121630&p=0)); pictorial self-assessment ("did you enjoy it, was it easy") records the child's own voice from Nursery, as the HPC requires ([PARAKH](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/themes/parakh/hpc-files/how-to-fill-pdf/How-to-fill-the-HPC-(Foundational-Stage).pdf)); validated questionnaires wait until about age 7–8, when children become able reporters of their own wellbeing ([Patalay et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4067682/)), and are aggregated for programme review. AI can turn a term's teacher notes into a draft narrative that the teacher edits and signs; it should not score wellbeing.

Two threads run through all four stages. The portfolio links the NCF-FS child folder to CBSE's Grade 9–10 portfolio component, which must "align with the philosophy of the Holistic Progress Card" ([CBSE 2026-27](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/CurriculumMain27/SecPart1/Curriculum_SecP1_2026-27.pdf)). And the HPC itself reports disaggregated, descriptive progress, compared "with the student's earlier report card and not with the performance of other students" ([NCF-SE 2023](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf)). Official documents still use three level vocabularies — Beginner/Progressive/Proficient in CBSE's guide, Beginner/Proficient/Advanced and Stream/Mountain/Sky in PARAKH's — so store evidence once and render the vocabulary the board requires ([CBSE HPC guide](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/Manuals/HPC_TeacherGuide.pdf); [PARAKH HPC](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/hpc)). Under CBSE, Grade 10 means 80 board and 20 internal marks per major subject, about half the board questions competency-focused, and two Class X sittings; in 2026, 59.95% of the 5.1 lakh students who took the second exam improved their result ([CBSE](https://www.cbse.gov.in/cbsenew/documents/Press_Release_18072026.pdf)). The board choice — CBSE, State Board or CISCE — sets the card format and the Grade 9–10 internal assessment, so it belongs before the schema hardens.

## Design the human check against automation bias; the law now expects it

### Neither checking everything nor checking only the doubtful works

People over-trust machines, and training does not cure it. Automation bias "occurs in both naive and expert participants, cannot be prevented by training or instructions" ([Parasuraman & Manzey](https://trid.trb.org/View/1085175)). In clinical decision support, **6–11% of correct decisions were switched to wrong ones** when the system erred, and wrong advice raised the risk of an incorrect decision by 26% ([Goddard et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3240751/)). Explanations made people more likely to accept an AI's recommendation "regardless of its correctness" ([Bansal et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14779)); making people decide before seeing the advice reduced over-reliance, and was the design they liked least ([Buçinca et al.](https://www.eecs.harvard.edu/~kgajos/papers/2021/bucinca2021trust.shtml)). Where alerts fire constantly, physicians override **90%** of them ([Felisberto et al.](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1177/14604582241263242?fields=title,abstract,year,venue,authors,citationCount)). A review of 41 policies requiring human oversight of government algorithms concluded that people often cannot perform the oversight asked of them, and that such policies "legitimize" faulty algorithms while providing "a false sense of security" ([Green](https://ailawblawg.com/2022/03/16/green-on-the-flaws-of-policies-requiring-human-oversight-of-government-algorithms/)).

Both obvious designs fail against this evidence. Asking a teacher to confirm every reading recreates alert fatigue: acceptance drifts toward 100%, the model's errors pass with a human signature, and the audit trail says "approved". Sending only doubtful answers to a person — the queue Cornerstone runs today — trusts the model's confidence, and the probabilities of RLHF-trained language models are "very poorly calibrated"; asking models to state their confidence often halves the calibration error on benchmark questions, which leaves the other half ([Tian et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14975)). School systems have paid for both mistakes. Wisconsin's dropout early-warning system labelled more than 32,000 students "high risk", was wrong nearly **three times in four**, produced false alarms 42 percentage points more often for Black than for white students, and had no measurable effect on graduation ([The Markup](https://themarkup.org/machine-learning/2023/04/27/false-alarm-how-wisconsin-uses-race-and-income-to-label-students-high-risk)). England's 2020 grading algorithm downgraded roughly 36–40% of A-level grades from teacher assessments and was abandoned four days after results day ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_Kingdom_school_exam_grading_controversy)).

Triage works when it is designed. In the MASAI randomised trial of more than 105,000 Swedish women, AI risk scores sent low-risk mammograms to one radiologist and high-risk ones to two; the AI arm cut screen reading by about 44%, detected more cancers and had 12% fewer interval cancers ([ecancer](https://ecancer.org/en/news/27721-ai-supported-mammography-screening-results-in-fewer-aggressive-and-advanced-breast-cancers-finds-full-results-from-first-randomised-controlled-trial)). A 2026 meta-analysis of 13 studies found that triage "consistently lower[s] radiologist workload and recalls without compromising sensitivity", while AI replacing a second reader raised arbitration cases by 270–305% ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12818982)). The conditions are what make it safe: thresholds validated in advance on the population served, the flagged set read in full by a person, and outcomes monitored. For Cornerstone that means checking by the kind of decision, not by one rule.

| Decision class | Examples at Cornerstone | Human check | Condition before relying on it |
|---|---|---|---|
| A: code-verifiable, reversible, low-stakes | A digit read from a cell whose correct answer code recomputes; a QR lookup | By exception, plus a random slice of confident reads checked blind every day | Calibration by confidence band measured on Cornerstone's own children, per grade, handwriting maturity and prompt version |
| B: judgement-laden or record-forming | A misconception tag; any change to a child's skill state; a transcribed observation; a report line | Every item, with the teacher entering or choosing rather than ticking; decide-then-compare on a fixed share | Crop or audio shown; the statement specific and observable |
| C: child- or parent-facing, hard to reverse | A report sent home; a change of group; a socio-emotional note | Two people: the class educator and the coordinator | Evidence trail attached; a teacher's name on every line |

Inside the queue, five mechanisms carry the evidence. Sort items by confidence, lowest first. Hide the model's answer until the teacher has entered their own on a small, visible share of items. Inject a random sample of confident items blind each day, so that drift the model is confidently wrong about still surfaces. Route every disagreement between the model's read and the code's recomputed answer — or between two independent reads — to a person, because code is a free second reader wherever an answer can be recomputed. And show information (the crop, the recomputed answer) rather than a green tick. Suspected hidden misconceptions go to a diagnostic follow-up item on a later worksheet rather than to the teacher, which is how Eedi's researchers propose to absorb the false alarms ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23205)). Then measure the check as hard as the model: record who confirmed, when, how long they looked and what they changed — the EU AI Act's logging template names "the natural persons involved in the verification" ([Article 12](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/12/)) — publish acceptance rates, and seed known-wrong readings to see how many pass, which is Green's "preliminary evaluation of whether people can effectively oversee the algorithm". An acceptance rate near 100% with sub-second dwell is a defect. No study has measured automation bias in teachers reviewing young children's work; Cornerstone would be the first to know its own rate.

### Transparency means evidence with a teacher's name on it

Regulators converge on a plain-language definition of an explanation. The UK ICO and the Alan Turing Institute list six parts: the rationale "in an accessible and non-technical way", who is responsible and "who to contact for a human review", which data were used, the steps taken for fairness, measured accuracy and reliability, and the impact on the person ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/artificial-intelligence/explaining-decisions-made-with-artificial-intelligence/part-1-the-basics-of-explaining-ai/what-goes-into-an-explanation/)); the OECD adds that those adversely affected must be able to "challenge its output" ([OECD](https://oecd.ai/en/dashboards/ai-principles/P7)). For each Cornerstone artefact — a marked cell, a misconception tag, a report line, a note — that means provenance (the capture, the model and prompt version with its evaluation score, the verifying code, the confirming person), one sentence a Grade 2 parent can read ("the answer 47 was read from the sheet; the correct answer is 45; the working shows the tens were not exchanged"), the evidence behind it, the step's measured error rate broken down where it varies, who to ask, and what the line changes. The UK Department for Education's rule is the right social contract: the quality of a document "remains the responsibility of the professional who produced it … regardless of the tools or resources used" ([DfE](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/generative-artificial-intelligence-in-education/generative-artificial-intelligence-ai-in-education)). No parent should read a model's sentence without a teacher's name beside it. Contestability should be a workflow with a clock: a dispute writes a superseding evidence row, re-derives the child's state and notifies anyone the data went to. Open learner models, which let learners inspect and challenge the system's picture of them, improved learners' self-assessment accuracy in small studies ([Bull et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6302918/)).

Show evidence, not verdicts. Grouping children by attainment yields no average progress and small losses for low attainers, through lower expectations, misallocation of disadvantaged pupils and rigid movement between groups ([EEF via QRF](https://qrf.org/en/teaching-and-learning-toolkit/setting-and-streaming)); expectancy effects in the original Pygmalion study were significant only in grades 1 and 2 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pygmalion_effect)) — the ages Cornerstone teaches. Derived states should be recomputable, should expire, and should appear with the sheets and notes behind them. No rank, set or risk score should reach a teacher or parent unless its positive predictive value on Cornerstone's own children has been measured and is shown beside it.

### DPDP is the floor from 13 May 2027; the EU AI Act is the bar

India's DPDP Act requires verifiable parental consent before processing any under-18's data (s.9(1)), forbids processing "likely to cause any detrimental effect on the well-being of a child" (s.9(2)), and forbids "tracking or behavioural monitoring of children" (s.9(3)) ([Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/98869575/)). The Rules notified in November 2025 exempt educational institutions from consent and from the tracking ban only where processing is "restricted to tracking and behavioural monitoring: for the educational activities of such institution; or in the interests of safety of children enrolled" ([Fourth Schedule](https://dpdpa.com/schedule/schedule4.html)), and the consent and children's provisions commence 18 months after notification, on **13 May 2027** ([Rule 1](https://dpdpa.com/dpdparules/rule1.html); [Consently](https://www.consently.in/blog/verifiable-parental-consent-dpdp-rules-2025-edtech-gaming)). Three consequences follow. A per-child learner model is behavioural monitoring in plain terms, lawful for a school without consent only as an educational activity; newsletter photographs, sharing with vendors, marketing and any training of models on pupil work need a Rule 10 consent flow, so build one and tag every data element with its lawful basis ([Rule 10](https://www.dpdpa.com/dpdparules/rule10.html)). Section 9(2) cannot be exempted, so a standing "low ability" flag or a class ranking shown to parents is a legal exposure as well as a pedagogical one. And the Act's general duties apply regardless: accuracy wherever data is "used to make a decision that affects" the child (s.8(3)) — which is exactly what the confirmation queue delivers — erasure when the purpose ends, correction with notice to everyone the data was shared with (s.12), processor contracts, and access logs kept for a year ([s.8](https://www.dpdpa.com/dpdpa2023/chapter-2/section8.html); [s.12](https://www.dpdpa.com/dpdpa2023/chapter-3/section12.html); [Rule 6](https://dpdpa.com/dpdparules/rule6.html)). Penalties for breaching children's obligations reach ₹200 crore ([PRS](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/digital-personal-data-protection-bill-2023)). The Rules were read on a mirror site because the ministry's own site was unreachable, so the text should be checked against the Gazette before a compliance document relies on it.

Courts are not waiting for 2027. In September 2026 the Supreme Court barred sharing APAAR student data with private entities except in accordance with law and applied DPDP provisions immediately ([Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-indias-apaar-student-parents/)), and NCERT has confirmed that APAAR is not required for admission ([Education for All in India](https://educationforallinindia.com/apaar-id-not-mandatory-for-school-admissions-ncert-clarification-parental-hardships-court-victory-revised-consent-form/)); hold the PEN and any APAAR ID as optional keys in the protected personal-data schema and never ingest from APAAR. No Ministry of Education or CBSE rule on a school's own use of AI for assessment or reporting was found: CBSE's April 2026 AI circular is curriculum ([CBSE](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/Circulars/2026/18_Circular_2026.pdf)), and MeitY's November 2025 guidelines are soft law asking for systems "Understandable by Design", human oversight in sensitive sectors and grievance redressal ([MeitY](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc2025115685601.pdf)). The policy risk for Cornerstone is reputational and data-protection, not board compliance.

The EU AI Act does not bind a Pune school, but it is the most detailed public statement of what oversight and transparency mean for education AI. Annex III makes high-risk any system "intended to be used to evaluate learning outcomes, including when those outcomes are used to steer the learning process" ([Annex III](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/annex/3/)) — an exact description of reading a worksheet, classifying an error and prescribing the next one. Its duties map directly: lifetime event logs (Article 12); documented accuracy including "performance regarding specific persons or groups" ([Article 13](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/13/)); oversight designed so people "remain aware of the possible tendency of automatically relying or over-relying on the output" and can override or stop it ([Article 14](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/14/)); overseers with "competence, training and authority", logs kept at least six months and affected people informed ([Article 26](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/26/)); and a right to "the main elements of the decision taken" ([Article 86](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/86/)). Emotion inference in education has been prohibited since 2 February 2025 ([Article 5](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/5/)), and the Digital Omnibus, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, moved the Annex III obligations to 2 December 2027 ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai)). Written into Cornerstone's own acceptance checks, these articles turn "not a black box" from a slogan into tests. The other bars point the same way: UNESCO set an age limit of 13 for AI tools in the classroom ([UNESCO](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-governments-must-quickly-regulate-generative-ai-schools)); England's product standards require a system to "only show a full solution after a genuine learner attempt" ([DfE](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/generative-ai-product-safety-standards/generative-ai-product-safety-standards)); and the US COPPA rule now counts voiceprints as personal information ([Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/ftc-publishes-updates-to-coppa-rule)). No Nursery–Grade 4 child should be an end-user of a generative model, and children's voices should be recorded only for a stated educational purpose with a retention limit.
