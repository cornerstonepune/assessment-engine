## 1. Policy: NEP 2020, NIPUN Bharat, NCF-FS 2022, NCF-SE 2023, PARAKH's Holistic Progress Card, CBSE circulars — and what is mandatory for a private school

### Takeaway
The national framework asks every school to move from marks to a competency-based, evidence-backed, 360-degree Holistic Progress Card (HPC) with portfolios, self/peer/parent input and a teacher narrative; for a CBSE school the NCF for the Foundational Stage is formally adopted (Circular Aff.08/2023) but the HPC itself is "adopt / adapt", not a fixed mandated format; the Maharashtra State Board is converging on the same design on a 2025–2029 timetable; the NIPUN Bharat 2026-27 target year has arrived with roughly a quarter to a third of rural Std III children at grade level; and CBSE's only AI-specific instruction so far is a Classes 3–8 "Computational Thinking and AI" curriculum from 2026-27, not a rule about schools' own use of AI.

### Cited Findings

**NEP 2020 (primary text, NCERT-hosted copy)**
- Structure: "The curricular and pedagogical structure … will therefore be guided by a 5+3+3+4 design, consisting of the Foundational Stage (in two parts, that is, 3 years of Anganwadi/pre-school + 2 years in primary school in Grades 1-2; both together covering ages 3-8), Preparatory Stage (Grades 3-5, covering ages 8-11), Middle Stage (Grades 6-8, covering ages 11-14), and Secondary Stage (Grades 9-12 …)" (para 4.1). Cornerstone's Nursery–Grade 4 therefore spans the Foundational and Preparatory stages. — [NEP 2020, para 4.1](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/nep/NEP_2020.pdf)
- FLN deadline: "every student will attain foundational literacy and numeracy by Grade 3 … The highest priority of the education system will be to achieve universal foundational literacy and numeracy in primary school by 2025" (para 2.2). — [NEP 2020, para 2.2](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/nep/NEP_2020.pdf)
- Assessment: "The aim of assessment … will shift from one that is summative and primarily tests rote memorization skills to one that is more regular and formative, is more competency-based … The primary purpose of assessment will indeed be for learning" (para 4.34). — [NEP 2020, para 4.34](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/nep/NEP_2020.pdf)
- The progress card: "The progress card of all students for school-based assessment … will be completely redesigned by States/UTs under guidance from the proposed National Assessment Centre, NCERT, and SCERTs. The progress card will be a holistic, 360-degree, multidimensional report … in the cognitive, affective, and psychomotor domains. It will include self-assessment and peer assessment, and progress of the child in project-based and inquiry-based learning, quizzes, role plays, group work, portfolios, etc., along with teacher assessment … accompanied by parent-teacher meetings … AI-based software could be developed and used by students to help track their growth through their school years based on learning data and interactive questionnaires for parents, students, and teachers" (para 4.35). — [NEP 2020, para 4.35](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/nep/NEP_2020.pdf)
- PARAKH: "a standard-setting body under MHRD that fulfils the basic objectives of setting norms, standards, and guidelines for student assessment and evaluation for all recognized school boards of India" (para 4.41). — [NEP 2020, para 4.41](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/nep/NEP_2020.pdf)
- Language: "there will be a greater flexibility in the three-language formula, and no language will be imposed on any State … so long as at least two of the three languages are native to India" (para 4.13). — [NEP 2020, para 4.13](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/nep/NEP_2020.pdf)

**NCF for the Foundational Stage 2022 (NCERT, 20 Oct 2022) — assessment, portfolios, HPC**
- Evidence and portfolios: "A portfolio is an intentional collection of significant work samples and records of children that allow for assessment by providing evidence of effort and accomplishment related to specific Learning Outcomes. The Teacher should analyse the portfolio of the child with regard to specific outcomes and mark the child's progress against competencies … Each child should have a dedicated folder to store their relevant artefacts." Work samples (artwork) and worksheets ("tasks that children perform and respond to in written form") are named evidence types. — [NCF-FS 2022, §6.2](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCF_for_Foundational_Stage_20_October_2022.pdf)
- Reporting cycle: "The school should maintain a folder for each child. The folder can contain all information about the child and the Teacher's narrative summary for each term/year. The summary of such an analysis can be captured into a Holistic Progress Card (HPC) and this can be used to communicate to the parents"; the narrative summary is "based on interpretation of multiple sources of information (e.g., anecdotal records, event samples, checklists, portfolios, worksheets)". — [NCF-FS 2022, §6.4.1–6.4.2](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCF_for_Foundational_Stage_20_October_2022.pdf)
- HPC content: "The HPC can contain not just the assessments done by the Teacher but can also include comments and observations by the parents and simple self-assessments by the children themselves … The HPC is an individualized and comprehensive reporting of a child's progress based on evidence gathered through classroom activities over a period of time … It is the primary source of information upon which parent-teacher meetings can be effectively conducted … Aggregated data from HPCs can be used to effectively understand the learning achievements of children at scale". — [NCF-FS 2022, §6.4.3](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCF_for_Foundational_Stage_20_October_2022.pdf)

**NCF for School Education 2023 (NCERT, Aug 2023) — Preparatory Stage assessment and HPC**
- HPC definition: "A Holistic Progress Card (HPCs) is an individualised and comprehensive reporting of a student's progress in all domains or areas of learning on the basis of Competencies and Learning Outcomes to be achieved … Unlike a single score or letter grade, an HPC provides disaggregated reporting, which does not represent performance in an entire subject area. It is based on evidence of student work beyond performances on tests (e.g., assessment of writing samples, projects, portfolios, artwork, participation in games) … The comparison will be with the student's earlier report card and not with the performance of other students in the class." — [NCF-SE 2023, §3.4.10](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf)
- Progression: "Students should not be detained from Grades 1 to 8." — [NCF-SE 2023, §3.4.11](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf)
- Preparatory Stage (Grades 3–5): assessment should "continue to be Competency based, covering all dimensions of learning"; "various formative assessment techniques, such as, projects, debates, presentations, experiments, investigations, role plays, journals and portfolios should be used"; "Regular assessments comprising MCQs and constructed responses (e.g., short answer, long answer), with the aim to test conceptual understanding and higher-order capacities rather than merely rote learning, may be used to track student progress". At the Middle Stage "there will be a comprehensive summative assessment of the student's readiness to enter the next grade". — [NCF-SE 2023, §3.4.9](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf)
- Portfolio definition: "a purposeful collection of student work … It could be a collection of the student's day-to-day work or a selection of the student's best pieces … Portfolios may include writing samples … worksheets, tests … teacher's qualitative comments … peer feedback, and the student's own reflections". — [NCF-SE 2023, §3.4.5](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf)

### Inferences
- For Cornerstone the binding layer is (a) its board's affiliation instructions, (b) Maharashtra statutes (Marathi Act; RTE norms), and (c) data law; the HPC is a strongly signalled expectation, not a fixed form — CBSE explicitly says "adopt / adapt", and the State Board is only now issuing its own card from Class 1 upward. A school that can emit a card structured like the PARAKH/CBSE prototype (competency-level descriptors, no ranks, evidence-backed narrative, self/peer/parent sections) will satisfy either board and the later schools children move to.
- The NCF-FS language ("folder for each child", "narrative summary for each term/year", worksheets and artwork as evidence, aggregated HPC data) is almost a specification for Cornerstone's per-child evidence record: the design is aligned with national policy rather than ahead of it.
- CBSE has no circular (found here) governing schools' own use of AI for assessment or reporting; the AI agenda is curricular (Classes 3–8 from 2026-27). The policy risk is therefore reputational and data-protection, not board compliance.
- Because Cornerstone will grow to Grade 10, the "two board exams a year" and "50% competency-based questions" agenda (CBSE via PARAKH; CISCE by 2027) will eventually shape what parents expect the skill graph to predict.

## 2. National digital infrastructure: NDEAR, DIKSHA, Vidya Samiksha Kendra, UDISE+, APAAR/DigiLocker, Bhashini and AI4Bharat — what a small private school can actually use

### Takeaway
The government stack a private school touches is UDISE+ (every recognised school reports; the student PEN lives there) and, through it, APAAR (voluntary, consent-based, generated inside UDISE+ and pushed to DigiLocker — now under court-ordered opt-out and Supreme Court limits on sharing with private entities). NDEAR/Sunbird and DIKSHA are open building blocks and content, usable but optional; Vidya Samiksha Kendras are government-school monitoring rooms; Bhashini's public APIs are proof-of-concept only; AI4Bharat's models are MIT-licensed and self-hostable.

### Cited Findings
- **NDEAR**: issued by the Ministry of Education in 2021 as "an architectural blueprint that aims to facilitate achieving the goals laid out in the National Education Policy, 2020 through a unified digital infrastructure", built of three kinds of building blocks — specifications and standards for interoperability; microservices and APIs deployed in a federated manner; and reference solutions usable "freely out of the box". It explicitly lets "public and private actors" build solutions; critics warn of a "Light but Tight Regulatory Framework" and weak consent. — [Internet Freedom Foundation explainer, 1 Dec 2021](https://internetfreedom.in/the-national-digital-education-architecture-an-explainer-2/). The official site (ndear.gov.in) returned HTTP 403 to this research, so its current building-block list could not be read.
- **Sunbird** (the open-source base of DIKSHA): "a digital infrastructure for learning" with 19 modular building blocks including Sunbird ED, Sunbird RC (Registry & Credentials), Sunbird inQuiry, cQube, Sunbird Knowlg and Sunbird Saral; DIKSHA is "India's flagship school education platform (Ministry of Education) deployed across 40 states, empowering 180 Mn+ and 7 Mn+ teachers". — [sunbird.org](https://www.sunbird.org/)
- **DIKSHA**: "a national platform for school education" run by NCERT under the Ministry of Education, launched in 2017, "adopted by almost all the States, Union Territories, central autonomous bodies/boards including CBSE", supporting "135 languages – 128 Indian languages and 7 foreign languages", "5 Cr+ App Downloads", and including "Sunbird Registry and Credential". The page does not say whether private schools or vendors may publish content. — [DIKSHA About](https://diksha.gov.in/about/)
- **Vidya Samiksha Kendra**: after Gujarat, 14 states and one UT — Chhattisgarh, Odisha, Meghalaya, Haryana, Assam, Arunachal Pradesh, Bihar, Mizoram, Uttar Pradesh, Jharkhand, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Uttarakhand, Andhra Pradesh, Andaman & Nicobar — were setting up VSKs with "Rs 2-5 crore of the total funds under the Samagra Shiksha Scheme" to track "attendance of teachers and students as well as undertake summative and periodic assessments of learning outcomes" in government schools; Maharashtra had "built the physical infrastructure for the control centre … a monitoring room along with computers and a video wall" and a two-district attendance pilot. — [Madhyamam, 20 May 2023](https://madhyamamonline.com/india/after-gujarat-14-states-1-ut-to-set-up-vidya-samiksha-kendras-to-monitor-govt-schools-1162055). An April 2026 report on Maharashtra's VSK (organiser.org) returned 403 and was not read.
- **UDISE+ 2024-25 (official booklet)**: India has 14,71,473 schools, 24,69,32,680 enrolments and 1,01,22,420 teachers; private unaided recognised schools number 3,39,583 with 9,58,56,710 enrolments and 39,62,114 teachers; national PTR is 20 at primary, 17 at upper primary, 15 at secondary, 23 at higher secondary. Maharashtra: 1,08,250 schools, 2,12,72,611 enrolments, 7,47,501 teachers; 18,746 private unaided recognised schools. — [UDISE+ 2024-25 booklet (PDF)](https://dashboard.udiseplus.gov.in/report2026/static/media/UDISE+2024_25_Booklet_existing.118ba29d4773e6372f72.pdf)
- **UDISE+ 2025-26** (released July 2026): total enrolment 24.72 crore; government schools fell from 12.75 crore to 11.89 crore students; private unaided rose from 9 crore to 9.89 crore; teachers 1,02,73,020; PTR improved from 25:1 to 24:1. — [Free Press Journal, 8 July 2026](https://www.freepressjournal.in/education/udise-2025-26-report-government-schools-lose-nearly-86-lakh-students-as-private-schools-gain-over-88-lakh-enrolments)
- **APAAR (official portal, viewed 26 Sept 2026)**: "Automated Permanent Academic Account Registry" under "One Nation, One Student ID"; counter shows 33.74 crore APAAR IDs registered; process: schools inform parents, who "fill up consent form voluntarily for APAAR ID creation"; demographic details are verified at school; "the UDISE+ system generates a 12-digit APAAR ID using the student's PEN (Permanent Enrollment Number)"; the ID "is pushed to the student's DigiLocker account"; the student's name must match Aadhaar records and a valid PEN is mandatory; APAAR stores "scorecards, marksheets, gradesheets, degrees, diplomas, certificates, co-curricular accomplishments". — [apaar.education.gov.in](https://apaar.education.gov.in/)
- **APAAR — courts and consent (Sept 2026)**: Odisha High Court in *Rohit Anand Das v. State of Odisha* (W.P.(C) 8285/2025), 12 Dec 2025, found the original form "offered no way to refuse" and directed the Ministry to revise it; the form now offers "two options — consent, or refusal" and states "Refusing APAAR ID will not affect my ward's admission, promotion, or access to government benefits". The Supreme Court in *Abhishek Baxi v. Union of India* (W.P.(C) 832/2026) directed that APAAR data "cannot be disclosed, shared, or otherwise made available to any private entity or third party except in accordance with law" and applied DPDP Act provisions immediately "rather than waiting for May 2027 commencement"; DPDPA Section 9 "bars tracking and behavioural monitoring of children". — [Medianama, 2 Sept 2026](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-indias-apaar-student-parents/)
- NCERT clarification, 19 April 2026: "A child can still get admission without an APAAR ID. It's not a requirement." — [Education for All in India, 20 April 2026](https://educationforallinindia.com/apaar-id-not-mandatory-for-school-admissions-ncert-clarification-parental-hardships-court-victory-revised-consent-form/)
- IFF (Dec 2024): APAAR follows a Ministry of Education directive of 11 October 2023; it collects "name, date of birth, gender, and contact information" plus "marksheets, transcripts, and certificates", requires Aadhaar linking and eKYC via DigiLocker; the consent form says already-processed information is unaffected by withdrawal; IFF reports schools pressuring parents despite voluntariness. — [IFF, 3 Dec 2024](https://internetfreedom.in/apaar-id-explained-what-every-parent-should-know/)
- **DPDP Act 2023**: a child is "a person below 18 years of age"; fiduciaries must "obtain verifiable consent from the legal guardian before processing the personal data of a child"; penalties up to Rs 200 crore for breaches of child-related obligations; the Centre may exempt classes of fiduciaries. — [PRS Legislative Research](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/digital-personal-data-protection-bill-2023). The MeitY data-protection page (Rules text and commencement schedule) returned 403; the "May 2027 commencement" is as reported by Medianama above.
- **Bhashini (government) APIs**: "Usage of these APIs shall be for the purposes of PoC only"; production or paid use requires reaching out to "the Bhashini team for the paid version of the APIs and exploring Pricing Plans"; tasks are ASR, NMT and TTS composed as pipelines (config call then compute call). — [Bhashini API docs (GitBook)](https://bhashini.gitbook.io/bhashini-apis). The bhashini.gov.in homepage rendered only a title (JavaScript app), so official usage statistics could not be read.
- **AI4Bharat IndicConformer**: "a suite of ASR models built to deliver accurate speech-to-text conversion in all 22 official Indian languages", MIT licence, one 600M-parameter multilingual model plus 22 monolingual models including Hindi and Marathi, downloadable from Hugging Face. — [GitHub AI4Bharat/IndicConformerASR](https://github.com/AI4Bharat/IndicConformerASR); the multilingual model card reports Hindi WER 13.2 on the ARTPARK-IISc Vaani benchmark. — [Hugging Face model card](https://huggingface.co/ai4bharat/indic-conformer-600m-multilingual)

### Inferences
- The one identifier Cornerstone must be able to carry is the UDISE+ PEN (and, when a parent consents, the 12-digit APAAR); everything the state later asks for (transfer certificates, DigiLocker credentials) hangs off those. Storing them as optional foreign keys in the `pii` schema, never as the child's primary key, satisfies both the "voluntary" ruling and the school's reporting duties.
- Because APAAR data may not flow to private entities except by law, Cornerstone should treat APAAR as a write-only destination (the school may issue records to it via the official channel) and never ingest or enrich from it.
- The DPDP Act's verifiable-parental-consent and Section 9 (no tracking or behavioural monitoring of children) apply squarely to a per-child evidence record and to any adaptive/"AI" feature; purpose limitation to teaching and reporting, no advertising, and a documented consent record are design requirements, not compliance afterthoughts.
- Nothing in NDEAR/DIKSHA obliges a private school; the practical value is (a) Sunbird RC as a reference for a credential/registry model and (b) DIKSHA's NCERT learning-outcome tags as the vocabulary future schools will recognise.
- Bhashini is not a production dependency for a school (PoC-only terms); AI4Bharat models are the only government-backed component that can be self-hosted under a permissive licence.

## 3. Findings on learning: ASER 2024, PARAKH Rashtriya Sarvekshan 2024, Maharashtra data, Teaching at the Right Level, Central Square Foundation

### Takeaway
Rural India's Std III children are at their best-ever but still low levels — about a quarter (government) to a third (all schools) read a Std II text and a third can subtract — and in rural Maharashtra private schools now do no better than government ones at Std III (37.5% vs 37.0%); the national PRS 2024 survey reports higher "proficiency" shares (57% language, 65% maths at Grade 3) on a different scale; the strongest causal evidence for what works is TaRL-style grouping by level (learning camps roughly tripled paragraph readers relative to controls) and personalised adaptive software (0.2–0.37 SD).

### Cited Findings

**ASER 2024 (rural household survey; Pratham)**
- Coverage: "reached 649,491 children in 17,997 villages across 605 rural districts". — [ASER 2024 national findings (PDF)](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/ASER-2024-National-findings.pdf)
- Std III reading (government schools): "20.9% in 2018 … 16.3% in 2022, and has increased to 23.4% in 2024 … the highest that they have been since the inception of the ASER survey … States with more than a 10 percentage point increase … include Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Kerala, Uttar Pradesh, Haryana, Odisha, and Maharashtra." — [ASER 2024 national findings](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/ASER-2024-National-findings.pdf)
- Std III arithmetic (all schools): "28.2% in 2018 and 25.9% in 2022 … increased to 33.7% in 2024. Among government school students … 20.9% in 2018 to 20.2% in 2022, increasing to 27.6% in 2024". Std V division: 27.9% (2018), 25.6% (2022), 30.7% (2024). Std V reading, private schools: 65.1% (2018) → 56.8% (2022) → 59.3% (2024). — [ASER 2024 national findings](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/ASER-2024-National-findings.pdf)
- Pre-primary and enrolment: 3-year-olds enrolled in some pre-primary institution 77.4% (2024); "Gujarat, Maharashtra, Odisha, and Telangana have achieved near-universal enrollment for this age group"; "Approximately one-third of all 5-year-olds attend a private school or pre-school in 2024"; government-school share of 6–14-year-olds 65.6% (2018) → 72.9% (2022) → 66.8% (2024); underage Std I entry down to 16.7%. — [ASER 2024 national findings](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/ASER-2024-National-findings.pdf)
- Digital: among 14–16-year-olds "Almost 90% of both girls and boys report having a smartphone at home"; 57% of users used it for an educational activity in the preceding week. — [ASER 2024 national findings](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/ASER-2024-National-findings.pdf)
- The ASER tools: reading is scored at letter / word / Std I text / Std II text; arithmetic at 1–9 / 11–99 / 2-digit subtraction with borrowing / 3-by-1-digit division, administered one-on-one, unchanged since 2006. — [ASER 2024 national findings](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/ASER-2024-National-findings.pdf)

**ASER 2024 — Maharashtra (rural, all 33 districts)**
- Std III reading levels 2024, all children: 4.2% not even letters, 13.4% letters, 18.1% words, 27.5% Std I text, 37.0% Std II text. By school type (Std III able to read Std II text): 2014 govt 33.1 / pvt 37.0; 2016 41.1 / 38.5; 2018 44.2 / 33.6; 2022 26.1 / 29.4; 2024 37.0 / 37.5 (combined 37.1). By sex 2024: boys 33.5%, girls 40.3%. — [ASER 2024 Maharashtra state page (PDF)](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/Maharashtra-1.pdf)
- Std V reading Std II text 2024: govt 57.9 / pvt 61.8 / combined 59.6; Std VIII: 70.9 / 75.7 / 74.3. — [ASER 2024 Maharashtra (PDF)](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/Maharashtra-1.pdf)
- Std III arithmetic 2024, all children: 3.6% cannot recognise 1–9; 19.3% recognise up to 9 only; 45.9% recognise up to 99 but cannot subtract; 25.5% subtract but cannot divide; 5.8% divide — i.e., 31.3% can at least do 2-digit subtraction with borrowing. — [ASER 2024 Maharashtra (PDF)](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/Maharashtra-1.pdf)
- The state index page also offers a Marathi translation of the state report and divisional and district estimates. — [ASER 2024 index](https://asercentre.org/aser-2024/)

**PARAKH Rashtriya Sarvekshan 2024 (school-based, all managements)**
- Planned design: 4 December 2024; "75,565 unique schools, reaching over 22,94,377 students in Grade 3, Grade 6, and Grade 9" across 36 States/UTs and 782 districts; Grade 3 tested on "foundational stage competencies" in a 90-minute sitting. — [PARAKH PRS page](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/prs)
- Results (released July 2025): 21.15 lakh students in 74,229 schools across 781 districts; Grade 3: 57% "proficiency or above" in language and 65% in mathematics, up from 39% and 42% in NAS 2021 and 47% and 53% in NAS 2017; girls 65% vs boys 63% in language; rural slightly ahead of urban; IRT-based scoring. — [Careers360, 8 July 2025](https://news.careers360.com/parakh-rashtriya-sarvekshan-2024-result-57-percent-class-3-students-language-proficiency-65-per-cent-mathematics-nas-ncert-nep/amp)
- Competency-level national figures quoted by a secondary source: Grade 3 — 55% could do mathematical sequencing, 67% language comprehension; Grade 6 — 38% practical maths problem-solving; Grade 9 — 31% on number systems; top performers Punjab, Himachal Pradesh, Kerala, Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu, Chandigarh. — [Rau's IAS summary, updated 10 July 2025](https://compass.rauias.com/current-affairs/parakh-rashtriya-sarvekshan-2024/)
- The survey covered "state government schools, government-aided schools, private unaided schools, and central government schools". — [Education for All in India](https://educationforallinindia.com/parakh-rashtriya-sarvekshan-2024/)

**Teaching at the Right Level (Pratham / J-PAL)**
- "TaRL has reached more than 80 million children in India and Africa" ("more than 76 million students" in India); the approach uses "a simple tool to group the children for part of the day based on their learning levels rather than age or grade"; among 27 costed programmes analysed by J-PAL, tailored instruction ranked among the most cost-effective, "often less than $10 per child per year". — [J-PAL evidence page](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evidence-effect/teaching-at-the-right-level)
- Learning Camps RCT, Uttar Pradesh (Sitapur and Unnao), 2013–2016, 17,266 students in grades 3–5, ~120 schools per arm; 10-day and 20-day camp arms taught Hindi and maths ~1.5 hours a day grouped by level; gains of 0.7–1.0 learning levels in both subjects; "Paragraph-level readers increased from 15% (baseline) to 49% (endline) in camp schools vs. 24% in controls"; a materials-only arm produced ~0.05 levels in maths and nothing in language. — [J-PAL evaluation summary](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evaluation/using-learning-camps-improve-basic-learning-outcomes-primary-school-children-india)
- Pratham's Grades 3–5 method is "Combined Activities for Maximised Learning (CAMaL)"; TaRL was rated a "Great Buy" by the Global Education Evidence Advisory Panel 2023. — [Pratham](https://www.pratham.org/guided-tour-of-teaching-at-the-right-level/)

**Personalised adaptive learning (Mindspark) — the Indian RCT evidence**
- Delhi, 2015–16: 619 students mostly grades 6–9, 4.5 months; treated students scored "0.37 standard deviations higher" in maths and "0.23 standard deviations higher" in Hindi; the platform "uses data to identify the learning level of every student, deliver customized content targeted at this level, and dynamically adjust"; cost about INR 1,000 per student per month. — [J-PAL](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evaluation/disrupting-education-evidence-technology-aided-instruction-india)
- Rajasthan government schools at scale (NBER WP 34205, Sept 2025, Muralidharan & Singh): a sample "over 20 times larger than the original study"; "After 18 months, treated students scored 0.22σ higher in Mathematics and 0.20σ higher in Hindi, a 50–66% productivity increase over the control group. Learning gains were proportional to student time on the platform, providing a simple, low-cost metric for monitoring implementation quality". — [NBER WP 34205 (PDF)](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w34205/revisions/w34205.rev0.pdf)

**Central Square Foundation**
- CSF "supports governments in achieving universal Foundational Literacy and Numeracy (FLN) by grade 3", runs District Project Management Units, works with Madhya Pradesh under Mission Ankur, and publishes the "Bharat Survey for EdTech (BaSE)". — [CSF homepage](https://www.centralsquarefoundation.org/). Its FLN and BaSE sub-pages returned 404 at the guessed URLs, so specific findings were not read.

### Inferences
- A Grade 3–4 classroom in Maharashtra should be expected to contain the full ASER spread — roughly a third of children below Std I-level reading and a third at grade level — so a skill graph that only records "the Grade 3 outcome met/not met" will mis-describe two-thirds of the class; the ladder needs rungs below grade level (letters, words, Std I text; 1–9, 11–99, subtraction) that map onto the ASER/TaRL tools parents and NGOs already recognise.
- The two robust effects in Indian trials — grouping by level and adaptive practice proportional to time-on-task — are both things a per-child evidence record makes possible without any model in the loop; the "time on platform predicts gains" finding is a candidate monitoring metric for Cornerstone's worksheets (sheets attempted per child per week).
- ASER (household, oral, one-on-one) and PRS (school, written, IRT) disagree by 20–30 points on "how many Grade 3 children are fine"; Cornerstone's parent reports should say which yardstick they use, and the ASER yardstick is the one that travels (19 languages, unchanged since 2006, Marathi translation available).
- In rural Maharashtra the private-school premium at Std III has vanished (37.5 vs 37.0); a Pune private school cannot assume enrolment in a private school signals grade-level reading.

### Gaps

- Urban Pune learning-level data — ASER is rural-only; no city-level survey was located.

### Inferences
- The market has no product that does what Cornerstone is building — a per-child evidence graph fed by paper worksheets and voice observations with a teacher-approved parent narrative. Toddle is closest on portfolios and AI-drafted reports but is IB/Cambridge-shaped and closed-priced; LEAD/Next Education sell the curriculum itself; ERPs (Entab, Fedena) hold the administrative record of truth (fees, attendance, transfer certificates) that Cornerstone's system should export to, not replace.
- Evidence culture is thin: apart from EI's RCTs, every number on these sites is a marketing claim. Cornerstone's promise-as-command discipline (goals with recomputed answers; `engine done`) is a differentiator parents and future schools can check, provided the school publishes the yardstick.
- Handwriting OCR vendors claim 95–98% without a method; Cornerstone's design principle that "a number is never a model's last word" is the correct response — every read cell is verified by code against the answer key and the three-signal rule, and confidence is recorded, rather than trusting a vendor accuracy figure.
- The Byju's lessons that matter for a school product: never sell to parental anxiety, never bundle loans or lock-ins, keep the parent channel free of upsell, and publish audited results — the same reasons NEP 4.36 condemns the "coaching culture".

## 5. The classroom reality in Pune private schools: class sizes and ratios, teacher qualifications and pay, multilingual classrooms, parents' expectations and channels, the coordinator

### Takeaway

A 2026 vendor blog asserting that most Indian schools run on WhatsApp gives no survey source; the "87% of parents" figure seen in search snippets is **unverified**. — [eduTinker, 3 April 2026](https://edutinker.com/parent-communication-in-indian-schools-why-whatsapp-alone-is-not-enough-in-2026/)

- **Detention**: exams in Classes 5 and 8 with re-examination and state-permitted detention (RTE Amendment Act 2019) — [PRS](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/the-right-of-children-to-free-and-compulsory-education-second-amendment-bill-2017); NCF-SE: "Students should not be detained from Grades 1 to 8" — [NCF-SE 2023](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf).

### Inferences
- Design for a teacher who is a graduate but not a specialist, paid well below government scale, possibly on no contract and therefore mobile: the system must make the teacher's evidence portable and her judgement visible (voice observations, approvals), so a teacher leaving mid-year does not take the class's memory with her.
- At pre-primary the workforce is 96% female and often part-time; voice capture should tolerate short, code-mixed Marathi/Hindi/English utterances recorded in a noisy room, not dictation.
- Marathi is not optional for Cornerstone under any board: report cards, parent summaries and the Marathi-language skills themselves must be first-class in the ladder and in output, with Hindi/other third language handled as a configurable column pending the Jadhav committee.
- Parents chose a private school for outcomes that, statewide, private schools do not reliably deliver at Std III; a report that shows a child's real reading level against the ASER/NIPUN yardstick will be more credible — and more uncomfortable — than a marks card. The report should therefore lead with progress against the child's own earlier card (NCF-SE §3.4.10), not with class position.
- WhatsApp will remain the delivery channel; the system should produce a short, approved, Marathi/English message per child per cycle rather than opening a two-way chat, avoiding the overload CBGA describes.
- The "coordinator" (a middle-management post between principal and class teachers, usually per stage or subject) is, in this research, unsourced; the design should nonetheless assume an approver above the class teacher because both CBSE's HPC guide and NCF-FS describe school-level aggregation and review of cards.

### Gaps
- Pune-specific section sizes and PTRs in private unaided schools (no source; CBSE Affiliation Bye-Laws page returned 403, so the per-section norm was not verified).
- Teacher pay in Pune private schools from a reliable survey (TISS PDFs 503; Indeed 403).
- Any survey on the coordinator role or Indian school hierarchies.
- Urban parent-expectation surveys (e.g., CSF's BaSE) — not reachable.

## 6. Language technology in 2026: Marathi and Hindi speech recognition and code-mixed (Hinglish) speech — Sarvam, AI4Bharat, Bhashini, Google

### Takeaway
On the only large independent real-world Indic benchmark opened (Voice of India, IIT Madras and Josh Talks, April–July 2026: 536 hours of unscripted phone speech from 36,691 adult speakers), Sarvam's models lead on Hindi (WER 5.0) and Marathi (9.4), Google's Gemini 3 Pro is close (6.0 / 10.7), AI4Bharat's open IndicConformer is a usable self-hosted third (8.2 / 13.1), and OpenAI's GPT-4o Transcribe is unusable for Marathi (55.6); most systems cross the 20% "practical usability" line on low-resource languages, degrade sharply with poor audio and short utterances, and none of the benchmarks include child speech.

### Cited Findings
- **Voice of India benchmark** (Bhogale, Javed, Khapra of IIT Madras with Josh Talks; arXiv 2604.19151, v1 21 April 2026, v4 3 July 2026): "a closed source benchmark built from unscripted telephonic conversations covering 15 major Indian languages across 139 regional clusters … 306230 utterances, totaling 536 hours of speech from 36691 speakers with transcripts accounting for spelling variations", motivated because "strict single reference WER penalizes natural spelling variation in Indian languages, including non standardized spellings of code-mixed English origin words". — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19151); [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.19151)
- WER on Voice of India, Hindi (hi) / Marathi (mr): Sarvam Audio 5.0 / 9.4; Saarika 2.5 (Sarvam) 6.2 / 10.0; Gemini 3 Pro 6.0 / 10.7; Amazon Transcribe 6.8 / 11.3; ElevenLabs Scribe v2 7.7 / 12.9; IndicConformer (AI4Bharat) 8.2 / 13.1; Gemini 3 Flash 8.3 / 16.0; Microsoft Speech-to-Text 11.4 / 31.9; Deepgram Nova 3 13.0 / 43.7; OmniASR LLM 7B 13.7 / 26.3; GPT-4o-mini Transcribe 19.6 / 30.7; GPT-4o Transcribe 33.9 / 55.6; AssemblyAI Universal 19.3 / 87.6 (Table 2a). — [Voice of India PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.19151)
- "most models exceed a WER of 20 …, a threshold often associated with practical usability, and no system meets this criterion consistently across all languages … SARVAMAUDIO achieves the lowest WER in 13 of 15 languages, followed by SAARIKA2.5 and GEMINI3 PRO. INDICCONFORMER and ELEVENLABS SCRIBE V2 show moderate performance"; the authors class Sarvam-Audio, Gemini-3-Pro and IndicConformer as "Tier I" (WER ≤ 20%). — [Voice of India PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.19151)
- Robustness: "Audio degradation raises WER monotonically; ElevenLabs Scribe rises from 15.31% to 25.20% and Gemini-3-Pro from 13.42% to 23.44% between the highest and lowest quality quartiles"; IndicConformer WER peaks at 27.57% (slow) and 27.53% (very fast) speech vs 24.75% at moderate pace; short utterances (<2 s) are worst (Amazon 10.45% → 18.74%); models perform 3.1–4.3 points better on female speech; the speaker age groups are 18–22 through 46+, i.e., adults only. — [Voice of India PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.19151)
- **Sarvam Saaras V3** (10 Feb 2026): "~19% WER" average on the IndicVoices benchmark (from ~22% for the prior version); compared against GPT-4o Transcribe, Gemini 3 Pro, Deepgram Nova 3 and Scribe v2; "handles code-mixed speech without distorting it. It preserves correct word boundaries and formatting, and avoids hallucinated insertions or dropped words"; no per-language or per-competitor numbers on the page. — [Sarvam blog](https://www.sarvam.ai/blogs/asr)
- **Sarvam Saaras V4** (24 Aug 2026): 22 Indian languages plus English; language-identification error "5.22% … across all 22 Indian languages, falling to 2.9% across the ten most widely spoken"; five output modes including one that "keeps native english words in english" (code-mixed); claims robustness to "compressed, clipped" field audio with wind and traffic noise; REST, batch and WebSocket streaming APIs; no Hindi/Marathi WER and no pricing on the page. — [Sarvam blog](https://www.sarvam.ai/blogs/introducing-saaras-v4)
- Sarvam's developer docs: Saaras v3 STT "supports 23 languages (22 Indian + English), with transcribe, translate, verbatim, translit, and codemix output modes"; a "saaras:v3-realtime" WebSocket API; Bulbul v3 TTS and Mayura translation for 11 languages; no unit pricing on the page. — [docs.sarvam.ai](https://docs.sarvam.ai/)
- **AI4Bharat IndicConformer**: MIT-licensed, 600M multilingual plus 22 monolingual (incl. Hindi and Marathi) hybrid CTC/RNN-T models — [GitHub](https://github.com/AI4Bharat/IndicConformerASR); Hindi WER 13.2 on Vaani per the model card — [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/ai4bharat/indic-conformer-600m-multilingual).
- **Bhashini** (government): API use "for the purposes of PoC only"; paid production access by arrangement. — [Bhashini API docs](https://bhashini.gitbook.io/bhashini-apis)
- **Google**: represented in Voice of India by Gemini 3 Pro/Flash (above); Gemini 3 Pro "performs best on dialectal varieties such as Bhojpuri and Chhattisgarhi". — [Voice of India PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.19151)

### Inferences
- For teacher voice notes in Marathi/Hindi/English, Sarvam (Saaras/Sarvam Audio) or Gemini 3 Pro are the accuracy leaders; the gap to self-hosted IndicConformer (about 3–4 WER points on Marathi) is the price of data sovereignty — a reasonable architecture is a hosted leader as the primary adapter with IndicConformer as an on-premise fallback, both behind Cornerstone's single `adapters/` boundary so the choice is one file.
- Every benchmark opened is adult speech; Cornerstone's voice capture is of educators (adults), so the numbers transfer, but any future capture of children reading aloud must be evaluated from scratch — no published Indic child-speech WER was found.
- Because WER roughly doubles between clean and poor audio and worsens for utterances under two seconds, the capture UI should encourage 5–20 second observations recorded close to the mouth, and the pipeline should keep the audio and show the transcript for the teacher's approval — consistent with "a model may generate what code then verifies" and with the teacher-approval step already in the design.
- Code-mixed output policy matters for the evidence record: Sarvam's "codemix" mode (English words kept in Latin script) is the right default for an evidence log that code later searches, and the record should store the raw transcript plus a normalised skill-tag, never only the model's paraphrase.
- Bhashini cannot be a production dependency (PoC terms); AI4Bharat models can.

### Gaps
- Per-language WER for Hindi and Marathi from Sarvam's own pages and any published Sarvam pricing (pricing page 404; docs show none).
- A dedicated Hinglish/code-mixed ASR benchmark for 2026 — not reachable after the search budget lapsed; Voice of India's lenient-WER treatment is the only code-mixing evidence opened.
- Child-speech ASR results in any Indian language.
- Marathi WER for IndicConformer from AI4Bharat's own evaluation (GitHub/HF pages carry none; AIKosh page did not render).
- Google Chirp/Cloud STT (non-Gemini) results on Indic speech.

## Council position

Each bullet: the position; confidence (High / Medium / Low); the evidence behind it.

1. **Comply with the board, not with a national mandate on the card.** For a CBSE school the NCF-FS is adopted for all affiliated schools (Circular Aff.08/2023) and the HPC is "adopt / adapt"; for a Maharashtra State Board school the SCF/HPC rollout is Class 1 in 2025-26 rising to Class 4 in 2026-27; for CISCE an HPC and competency-based shares are being phased in from 2025-26. Cornerstone must know which regime it is under and version its card format accordingly. Confidence: High for CBSE; Medium for State Board and CISCE (secondary sources). Evidence: [CBSE HPC guide](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/Manuals/HPC_TeacherGuide.pdf); [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/maharashtra-implement-cbse-curriculum-up-class-12-2028-confirms-education-minister-dada-bhuse); [ScooNews](https://scoonews.com/news/cisce-to-implement-major-academic-reforms-aligned-with-nep-2020-from-2025-26/).
2. **Emit an HPC-shaped report, not a marks sheet.** Structure the parent report as the national prototype does: domains (physical, socio-emotional, cognitive, language/literacy, aesthetic/cultural, learning habits), competency-level descriptors on a three-step scale (Beginner / Progressing / Proficient, or PARAKH's Stream / Mountain / Sky), a teacher narrative, and sections for the child's self-assessment, a peer comment and the parent's observation; compare the child with her own earlier card, never with the class. Confidence: High. Evidence: [NEP 4.35](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/nep/NEP_2020.pdf); [NCF-SE §3.4.10](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf); [PARAKH HPC](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/hpc); [CBSE HPC guide](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/Manuals/HPC_TeacherGuide.pdf).
3. **Make the per-child evidence record the NCF-FS "folder".** Every worksheet, artefact and observation is tagged to a Learning Outcome/competency and kept per child per term with a narrative summary; the card is a rendering of the folder, and aggregated folders feed the school's own review. This is exactly what NCF-FS §6.2–6.4 describes. Confidence: High. Evidence: [NCF-FS 2022](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCF_for_Foundational_Stage_20_October_2022.pdf).
4. **Carry the ASER/NIPUN yardstick below grade level.** The ladder must have rungs for letters/words/Std I text and 1–9/11–99/subtraction/division so that the two-thirds of Std III children who are below or above the grade mark are described truthfully; label reports with the yardstick used (household-oral ASER vs school-written PRS give 30-point-different pictures). Confidence: High. Evidence: [ASER 2024 Maharashtra](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/Maharashtra-1.pdf); [Careers360 on PRS 2024](https://news.careers360.com/parakh-rashtriya-sarvekshan-2024-result-57-percent-class-3-students-language-proficiency-65-per-cent-mathematics-nas-ncert-nep/amp).
5. **Design the graph to drive grouping by level and time-on-task, the two effects with Indian RCT evidence.** Expose per-child level groups to the teacher and track sheets attempted per week as the implementation-quality metric; treat model-generated "adaptive" recommendations as suggestions the teacher approves. Confidence: High for the evidence; Medium for transfer to a private Pune classroom. Evidence: [J-PAL Learning Camps](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evaluation/using-learning-camps-improve-basic-learning-outcomes-primary-school-children-india); [NBER WP 34205](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w34205/revisions/w34205.rev0.pdf).
6. **Treat children's data under the DPDP Act from day one.** Verifiable parental consent recorded per child; purpose limited to teaching and reporting; no behavioural profiling or advertising; names only in `pii`; images without names to any model; a documented withdrawal path. Confidence: High on the Act's requirements; Medium on the Rules' timing (May 2027 commencement as reported). Evidence: [PRS on DPDP](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/digital-personal-data-protection-bill-2023); [Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-indias-apaar-student-parents/).
7. **Hold the UDISE+ PEN and APAAR as optional foreign keys, never as identity.** APAAR is voluntary with a court-ordered refusal option, cannot be shared with private entities except by law, and is not required for admission; the school may issue records into DigiLocker via the official channel but must not ingest from it. Confidence: High. Evidence: [apaar.education.gov.in](https://apaar.education.gov.in/); [Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-indias-apaar-student-parents/); [EFA India on NCERT clarification](https://educationforallinindia.com/apaar-id-not-mandatory-for-school-admissions-ncert-clarification-parental-hardships-court-victory-revised-consent-form/).
8. **Marathi is first-class; the third language is a configurable column.** Marathi is compulsory and marked for every board in Maharashtra; Hindi's status in Classes 1–5 is unsettled pending the Jadhav committee. Reports, UI strings and the skill ladder need Marathi from the start; the parent message should be available in Marathi and English. Confidence: High. Evidence: [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/lawschool/articles/maharashtra-government-three-language-policy-critical-analysis-298829); [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/maharashtra-govt-scraps-three-langauge-policy-in-schools-announces-to-set-up-panel-cm-hindi-row-cm-devendra-fadnavis/amp).
9. **For voice, pick from the Tier I set and keep the choice in one adapter file.** Sarvam (Hindi 5.0 / Marathi 9.4 WER) or Gemini 3 Pro (6.0 / 10.7) as primary; AI4Bharat IndicConformer (8.2 / 13.1, MIT) as self-hosted fallback; avoid GPT-4o Transcribe (Marathi 55.6) and Bhashini's PoC-only API for production; use a code-mixed output mode, keep the audio, and require teacher approval of the transcript. Confidence: High on the ranking (one independent benchmark, adult speech); Medium on absolute error in a noisy classroom. Evidence: [Voice of India](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.19151); [Sarvam docs](https://docs.sarvam.ai/); [Bhashini API terms](https://bhashini.gitbook.io/bhashini-apis).
10. **Integrate with the school's ERP and the parents' WhatsApp by export, not by replacement.** The administrative record (fees, attendance, TCs) lives in an ERP such as Entab or Fedena (the latter advertises an API); Cornerstone's system should export the approved card and a short per-child message rather than run a chat, so it avoids the overload documented for class WhatsApp groups. Confidence: Medium. Evidence: [Fedena](https://fedena.com/); [Entab](https://www.entab.in/); [CBGA](https://www.cbgaindia.org/blog/whatsapp-school-opportunities-challenges/).
11. **Do not trust, or advertise, a handwriting-reading accuracy number.** Indian vendors claim 95–98% on handwritten sheets without published methods; Cornerstone should instead publish its own verified read-and-mark rate from `engine eval read_cells`, keep blank / wrong / wrong-with-working distinct, and let code verify every model read against the key. Confidence: High. Evidence: [Eklavvya](https://www.eklavvya.com/ai-answersheet-evaluation-school/); [ConveGenius](https://convegenius.com/plugging-learning-gaps-at-scale.html).
12. **Avoid the Byju's pattern.** No sale to parental anxiety, no rankings, no lock-in, no monetised parent channel; publish the yardstick and the audit, in line with NEP's own critique of the coaching culture. Confidence: High. Evidence: [Wikipedia: Byju's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byju%27s); [NEP 4.36](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/nep/NEP_2020.pdf).
