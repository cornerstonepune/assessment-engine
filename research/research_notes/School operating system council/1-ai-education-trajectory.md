## KQ1. What do frontier labs actually offer schools in 2026, and what independent evidence of learning effects exists?

### Takeaway
As of September 2026 the four big offers (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Khan Academy) are overwhelmingly *teacher-workspace and chatbot* products with compliance wrappers; none of the vendors publishes an independent, peer-reviewed K-12 learning-outcome study of its own product. The credible causal evidence on generative-AI tutoring comes from academic RCTs of purpose-built, guard-railed tutors (Harvard physics, Nigeria/World Bank, Tutor CoPilot, Medly), not from the vendors' school products.

### Cited Findings

- Anthropic's 9 July 2025 update describes Canvas LTI, Wiley and Panopto integrations, a free "AI Fluency" course and student ambassador/builder programmes; the announcement is exclusively higher-education (USF Law, Northumbria); "K-12 teachers" appears only as a footer category; no learning-outcome evidence is cited — [Anthropic, "Advancing Claude for Education", 2025-07-09](https://www.anthropic.com/news/advancing-claude-for-education)
- Anthropic's current API list prices (page undated, read 2026-09-26): Fable 5.1 $10 in / $50 out per MTok; Opus 5.5 $4 / $20; Sonnet 5 $2 / $10; Haiku 4.5 $1 / $5; batch processing "save 50%"; prompt-cache reads $0.10–$0.25 per MTok — [claude.com/pricing](https://claude.com/pricing)

OpenAI's own usage data (Jan–Jul 2026): report cards and lesson planning ≈1.7 M of 1.9 M "time-saving" messages — [WinsSolutions, 2026-08-26](https://www.winssolutions.org/openai-chatgpt-for-teachers-expansion/) (secondary source; OpenAI's own post returned 403)

**Google — Gemini for Education / LearnLM**

**Khan Academy — Khanmigo**
- Khan Academy's own November 2024 efficacy post reports *correlational* results for Khan Academy Districts/MAP Accelerator (~350k students, grades 3–8: 18+ hours/year associated with ~20% greater-than-expected gains, effect size 0.36) and explicitly reports **no Khanmigo efficacy results** — studies "underway" — [Khan Academy blog, Nov 2024](https://blog.khanacademy.org/khan-academy-efficacy-results-november-2024/)
- The J-PAL registered Khanmigo RCT (PI Philip Oreopoulos, Canada; randomises teacher coaching and proactive vs passive Khanmigo activation; outcome: standardized math scores) was at proposal stage in June 2023 and the page reports **no results** — [J-PAL project page](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/initiative-project/ai-powered-tutoring-unleashing-full-potential-personalized-learning-khanmigo)
- Khan's 6 May 2026 engineering post: 15 M+ tutoring threads analysed (Oct 2025–Apr 2026); "math error rates" tracked as a guardrail metric but **no error rate is disclosed**; passing student learning history improved "next-item correctness" by a cumulative 6.1%; no external learning-outcome measure — [Khan Academy blog, 2026-05-06](https://blog.khanacademy.org/how-khan-academy-is-building-a-better-ai-tutor-our-most-recent-learnings/)
- Only independent study found: 69 undergraduates (Eastern Kentucky University), Khanmigo vs Google vs paper on the Lunar Phases Concept Inventory — all groups gained, **no statistically significant between-group difference** (published 2025-10-22) — [Journal of Teaching and Learning](https://jtl.uwindsor.ca/index.php/jtl/article/view/10052)

**Independent causal evidence on generative-AI tutoring (not vendor products)**
- Harvard physics RCT (Kestin et al., *Scientific Reports*, published 2025-06-03; fielded fall 2023): 194 undergraduates, crossover design, custom "PS2 Pal" tutor vs in-class *active learning*; median post-test 4.5 vs 3.5, "learning gains more than double", quantile-regression effect sizes 0.73–1.3 SD; median AI time-on-task 49 min — [ETC Journal review, 2025-11-10](https://etcjournal.com/2025/11/10/review-of-kestin-et-al-s-june-2025-harvard-study-on-ai-tutoring/). The tutor was deliberately constrained: "only give away one step at a time", brief responses, students attempt before answers, answer keys supplied to prevent hallucination; caveats: elite sample, Zoom-proctored sessions — [Hechinger Report, 2024-09-16](https://hechingerreport.org/proof-points-ai-tutor-harvard-physics/)
- Turkey high-school maths RCT (Bastani et al., *PNAS*, 2025-06-25): ~1,000 students, grades 9–11, fall 2023; three arms. Practice performance: GPT Base +48%, GPT Tutor (hints not answers, teacher-designed safeguards) +127% vs control. Unassisted exam: **GPT Base −17% vs control; GPT Tutor statistically indistinguishable from control** — "unfettered access to GPT-4 can harm educational outcomes" — [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12232635/)
- Nigeria (Edo State) World Bank RCT, six-week after-school programme, twice weekly, Microsoft Copilot (GPT-4) with teacher-curated prompts and teacher supervision for hallucinations/over-reliance: ~0.3 SD overall, 0.24 SD English; "roughly 1.5 to 2 years of typical learning progress"; authors "cannot isolate the effect of the chatbot alone" — [World Bank blog, 2025-06-25](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/developmenttalk/addressing-the-learning-crisis-with-generative-ai--lessons-from-); working-paper abstract gives 0.31 SD overall / 0.23 SD English, largest effects for girls and higher-baseline students — [ERIC ED676624](https://eric.ed.gov/?q=source:%22World+Bank%22&id=ED676624); sample ≈800 students, mid-2024, power/connectivity problems, first weeks spent on digital literacy — [ICTworks, 2025-01-23](https://www.ictworks.org/genai-advance-learning-outcomes/)
- Tutor CoPilot (Wang, Ribeiro, Robinson, Loeb, Demszky; Stanford, 2025): RCT with 700+ human tutors and 1,000 elementary maths students; AI *coaching the tutor* raised topic mastery +4 pp overall (p<0.01) and **+9 pp for lower-rated tutors**; ~$20 per tutor per year; 350k messages show more probing questions, less generic praise — [Stanford NSSA](https://nssa.stanford.edu/studies/tutor-copilot-human-ai-approach-scaling-real-time-expertise)
- Medly (England, GCSE science), practitioner-led micro-RCTs, four weeks, 929 baseline / 644 post-test: Hedges' g = 0.33 (95% CI 0.18–0.48); attrition 30.7%; authors call results preliminary — [arXiv 2609.14789, 2026-09-13](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14789)
- (pre-2024, India) Mindspark adaptive-software RCT, Delhi, grades 6–9, 4.5 months: +0.36σ maths, +0.22σ Hindi; weaker students gained proportionally more — [NBER w22923, 2016/2017; AER 2019](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22923)

### Inferences
- The vendor K-12 products are productivity suites for adults plus guard-railed chat for students; their efficacy evidence is preference studies, usage telemetry and correlational dosage analyses. Nothing a vendor ships today comes with a causal K-12 learning claim a council could rely on.
- Where generative AI has demonstrably moved learning, the design pattern is constant: a *narrow, pedagogically constrained tutor* (hints not answers, one step at a time, answer key supplied, teacher-curated prompts, human supervision). Unconstrained chat produced *negative* transfer (Turkey). This is the single most transferable finding for a school building its own system.
- The strongest 2025–26 effect on *young* children's learning came from AI supporting the adult (Tutor CoPilot), not from AI facing the child.

### Gaps
- No independent K-12 learning-outcome evaluation of Claude for Education, ChatGPT for Teachers, Gemini for Education or Khanmigo was found; Khanmigo's registered RCT has published no results as of the page I opened.
- Provenance of Google's "5.5 pp vs human tutors" and "50th→64th percentile" claims could not be established (search budget exhausted); treat as unverified vendor claims.
- OpenAI's own posts (chatgpt-for-teachers, expansion) and CNBC returned 403; details above rest on two secondary trade sources.

## KQ2. What do the major evidence syntheses conclude (OECD, UNESCO, EEF, Brookings, RAND/Gallup, McKinsey)?

### Takeaway
The 2025–26 syntheses converge: generative AI improves *performance* readily and *learning* only when the design forces the learner to do the thinking; teachers' verified gains so far are modest workload savings (≈25 min/week in the one high-security RCT; ~6 h/week self-reported by weekly users), not instructional transformation. Brookings (Jan 2026) goes furthest: at this point the risks to children "overshadow" the benefits. McKinsey's 2020 "20–40% of hours automatable" is a consultancy estimate from a four-country teacher time-use survey, not an observed outcome.

### Cited Findings

**OECD Digital Education Outlook 2026 (published 2026-01-19; 247 pp.)**
- Official description: examines GenAI used "by students to learn subject knowledge, by students and teachers together as part of instruction, and by teachers alone", plus institutional efficiency — [ERIC ED681441](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED681441)
- Official summary text: "GenAI can support learning when guided by clear teaching principles. However, if designed or used without pedagogical guidance, outsourcing tasks to GenAI simply enhances performance with no real learning gains." Three roles: tutor, partner, assistant; much GenAI use "operates beyond institutional oversight" — [Youthrex record](https://youthrex.com/report/oecd-digital-education-outlook-2026-exploring-effective-uses-of-generative-ai-in-education/)
- "using GenAI with pedagogical intent can improve learning and foster skills like critical thinking, creativity and collaboration… education GenAI can moreover improve human teaching while preserving teacher's agency" — [Cedefop record](https://www.cedefop.europa.eu/en/tools/vet-toolkit-tackling-early-leaving/resources/oecd-digital-education-outlook-2026)
- From the launch conference (secondary account, 2026-03-24): Dragan Gašević's meta-analytic finding that "when we look only at performance metrics, AI interventions appear to have large positive effects. When we isolate genuine learning outcomes, the effects are small and sometimes negative" ("metacognitive laziness"); a US essay study in which "80% could not remember what they had written about afterwards"; Mutlu Cukurova's three human-AI teaming paradigms — replacement, complementarity, synergy — with most current practice being replacement and risking erosion of teacher competence; Andreas Schleicher: "Doing something with AI does not mean learning from it" — [Reda Sadki, 2026-03-24](https://redasadki.me/2026/03/24/oecd-digital-education-outlook-2026-how-can-ai-help-human-beings-learn-and-grow/) (the OECD's own pages returned 403 and the PDF exceeded the fetch size limit)

**UNESCO**
- *Guidance for generative AI in education and research*: first published 2023-09-07 (page updated 2026-01-16); recommends "mandating the protection of data privacy", "setting an age limit for the independent conversations with GenAI platforms", "a human-agent and age-appropriate approach to the ethical validation and pedagogical design processes"; notes institutions are "largely unprepared to validate the tools" and that model releases outpace regulation — [UNESCO](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/guidance-generative-ai-education-and-research). (The specific age figure — widely reported as 13 — is **not stated on the page I opened; unverified here**.)
- Digital Learning Week, Paris, 8–9 Sept 2025: two new reports — *AI and the future of education: disruptions, dilemmas and directions* and *AI and education: protecting the rights of learners*; UNESCO has supported 58 countries on AI/digital competency frameworks since 2024; 1 in 3 people worldwide still offline ("AI divide"); in higher education 9 of 10 respondents use AI professionally but only 50% feel confident about its technological and pedagogical foundations; 34% report positive experience of AI-assisted assessment; "protecting teachers' irreplaceable roles" named a priority — [UNESCO, Sept 2025](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-convenes-global-leaders-digital-learning-week-2025-shape-inclusive-human-centred-futures-ai)

**Education Endowment Foundation / NFER — the one high-security teacher-workload RCT**
- *ChatGPT in Lesson Preparation: A Teacher Choices Trial* (evaluation report December 2024; trial Dec 2023–Dec 2024; intervention summer term 2024): cluster-randomised, 34 schools (129 teachers) ChatGPT + web guide vs 34 schools (130 teachers) no-GenAI; Year 7–8 science. Primary outcome: ChatGPT group's preparation time was **69%** of control — 56.2 vs 81.5 min/week, **saving 25.3 min/week**; "high security rating"; blind expert panel found "no evidence… that the quality of the lesson resources… differed"; teachers used ChatGPT for one or two activities (questions/quizzes, activity ideas); 18.5% diary non-completion — [EEF/NFER report PDF (mirror)](http://www.mmiweb.org.uk/gaiandre/literature/EEF.pdf) (text extracted locally; EEF's own site returned 403)
- Transcript analysis in the same report: "use of additional prompts to refine the quality of outputs was rare" — teachers took the first output and edited by hand — [same PDF](http://www.mmiweb.org.uk/gaiandre/literature/EEF.pdf); corroborated with the 68-school/259-teacher figures by [Edtech Insiders, 2026-05-21](https://edtechinsiders.substack.com/p/how-ai-is-changing-teaching-workflows), which also cites (secondary, not opened by me) a Brazil essay-feedback study in 178 public schools (~19,000 seniors) where AI feedback freed teachers for 35% more one-to-one writing conversations

**Brookings Global Task Force on AI and Education (report 2026-01-14)**
- Method: yearlong "premortem"; 500+ students, teachers, parents, leaders and technologists across 50 countries; review of 400+ studies; Delphi panel. Headline: "at this point in its trajectory, the risks of utilizing generative AI in children's education overshadow its benefits" — risks that "undermine children's foundational development". Framework Prosper / Prepare / Protect; recommends co-creating tools with educators and preparing teachers to "teach with and through AI" rather than replacing them — [Brookings, 2026-01-14](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/a-new-direction-for-students-in-an-ai-world-prosper-prepare-protect/)

**RAND and Gallup (US adoption data)**
- RAND, *AI Use in Schools Is Quickly Increasing but Guidance Lags Behind* (2025-09-30; Doss, Bozick, Schwartz, Chu, Rainey, Woo, Reich, Dukes): "a rapid increase in AI use by students and teachers in the past one to two years on the order of 15 percentage points or more"; professional development, student training and district policy lag; students fear "false accusations of cheating"; stakeholders fear degraded critical thinking — [ERIC ED676922](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED676922) (RAND's own pages returned 403; the "53% of ELA/maths/science teachers used AI in 2025" and "45% of principals have policies" figures seen in search snippets are **unverified here**)
- Walton Family Foundation–Gallup, fielded 18 Mar–11 Apr 2025 on RAND's American Teacher Panel (n = 2,232 US public K-12 teachers): 6 in 10 used an AI tool for work; 3 in 10 weekly; weekly users save 5.9 h/week (≈6 weeks over a 37.4-week year); top uses: preparing to teach (37% at least monthly), making worksheets/activities (33%), modifying materials (28%); 57–74% say quality improves, ≤16% say it falls — [Gallup](https://news.gallup.com/poll/691967/three-teachers-weekly-saving-six-weeks-year.aspx)

**McKinsey 2020 "20–40% of teacher hours" (pre-2024)**
- The report (January 2020) estimated "20 to 40 percent of the tasks teachers spend time on… could be outsourced to technology", against a ~50-hour week, headlined as ~13 h/week; categories: grading, lesson planning, general administration. The EdWeek coverage notes the sophistication of grading tech was "unclear", bias and privacy risks, and that PISA/NAEP suggest classroom technology may harm learning — [EdWeek, 2020-01-29](https://www.edweek.org/technology/artificial-intelligence-could-free-up-13-hours-a-week-for-teachers-report-finds/2020/01)
- Activity breakdown as reported: lesson preparation ~11 → 6 h; grading 6 → 3 h; administrative paperwork −2 h. Hechinger's caveats: automatic grading "isn't very good yet" for writing and complex maths/science; teacher dashboards often "add to their workloads"; online resources reviewed were "mediocre"; automated grading may "break the feedback loop between teacher and student" — [Hechinger Report, 2020-01-27](https://hechingerreport.org/ai-in-education-reframing-ed-tech-to-save-teachers-time-and-reduce-workloads/)
- Basis and critique: the 50-hour week averages teachers in the US, Canada, UK and Singapore; the work was done with Microsoft; the figure was a projection of what "could be automated" with no demonstration — [Curmudgucation (Peter Greene), 2020-01-27](https://curmudgucation.blogspot.com/2020/01/mckinseys-new-baloney-sales-pitch-for.html). McKinsey's own article and PDF returned 403/503; the sample size of the underlying teacher survey is **unverified here**.

### Inferences
- The best-measured teacher time saving from generic chat (EEF: ~25 min/week on one subject's planning) is an order of magnitude below McKinsey's projection (13 h/week) and below self-reports (5.9 h/week). Verified savings come from narrow tasks — quiz/question generation, resource drafting — exactly the tasks that lend themselves to structured generation with code-side checking.
- Two independent synthesis bodies (OECD, Brookings) plus the two strongest RCTs (Turkey, Harvard) point the same way: design determines sign. A school that controls the design (guardrails, teacher approval, process-oriented use) sits on the right side of that line; one that buys open chat does not.
- Teachers do not iterate with chatbots (EEF transcripts). Any system that depends on teachers prompting skilfully will under-deliver; systems should present finished, checkable artefacts for approval instead.

## KQ3. Documented failures and backlashes — Summit Learning, AltSchool, LAUSD "Ed", Byju's — what went wrong and the transferable lesson

### Takeaway
Four different failure modes, one common root: the product led and the pedagogy, the evidence, the governance or the unit economics followed. Screen-first "personalised learning" for young children lost parents (Summit, AltSchool); a district bought an over-scoped chatbot from an unvetted startup and exposed student data (LAUSD); a consumer edtech scaled sales before proving learning (Byju's). None failed because the model was not smart enough.

### Cited Findings

**Summit Learning (Chan Zuckerberg-backed platform; 2015 onward) (pre-2024 events)**
- By December 2018 the platform was in "nearly 400 schools"; Brooklyn students walked out ("It's annoying to just sit there staring at one screen for so long"); parent revolts in Connecticut, Cincinnati, Milwaukee, Pennsylvania; concerns: screen time, reduced teacher interaction, data-mining, schools using it as "go learn"; **no outside research** on Summit's effectiveness existed; broader personalised-learning research showed "modest boosts" but students "less likely to say there was an adult at school who knew them well" — [Chalkbeat, 2018-12-19](https://www.chalkbeat.org/2018/12/19/21106372/could-the-forces-that-fought-the-common-core-bring-down-personalized-learning/)
- Parent Coalition for Student Privacy fact sheet (April 2019): suspensions/scale-backs in Connecticut, Indiana, Kansas (USD 418, McPherson), Wisconsin opt-outs; core concerns were screen time for young students, data collection and whether elementary-age children can run self-directed learning — [Parent Coalition fact sheet PDF](https://www.studentprivacymatters.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/Summit-fact-sheet-4.22.19-1.pdf)

**AltSchool (2013–2019) (pre-2024)**
- Founded 2013; raised >$175 M (Zuckerberg, Powell Jobs); 7 private micro-schools at $25k+/year used as testing grounds for its software; November 2017 it closed Palo Alto (~65 families) and consolidated to 4 sites to pivot to selling software — CEO Max Ventilla: "the path for the company to be sustainable and impactful is to provide the platform we're developing to schools we don't directly operate". Audrey Watters: "So much of what these companies do is really for their investors… you're not dealing with products, you're dealing with other people's children." — [EdWeek, 2017-11-03](https://www.edweek.org/education/citing-investor-priorities-altschool-closes-some-sites-pivots-to-software-sales/2017/11)

**LAUSD "Ed" chatbot / AllHere (2024)**
- Timeline: RFP Feb 2023 → launch 20 March 2024 → pilot of 1,000 students scaled to 55,000 in spring 2024 → shutdown 14 June 2024 when AllHere furloughed most staff; contract up to $6 M over five years — [EdWeek, 2024-07-08](https://www.edweek.org/technology/los-angeles-unifieds-ai-meltdown-5-ways-districts-can-avoid-the-same-mistakes/2024/07); [The 74, 2024-07-09](https://www.the74million.org/article/was-los-angeles-schools-6-million-ai-venture-a-disaster-waiting-to-happen/)
- Scope: one assistant for students, parents and staff across attendance, grades, transport, tutoring "in 100 languages"; Superintendent Carvalho promised "540,000 'schools of one'". Amanda Bickerstaff: "What they were trying to do is really not possible with where the technology is today." Richard Culatta: "Whenever a district says, 'Our strategy around AI is to buy a tool,' that's a problem." — [The 74, 2024-07-09](https://www.the74million.org/article/was-los-angeles-schools-6-million-ai-venture-a-disaster-waiting-to-happen/)
- Whistleblower (Chris Whiteley, ex-senior director of engineering): student PII was included in every prompt regardless of relevance; the data of *all students in a household* was processed to answer about one child; prompts processed on servers in Japan, Sweden, UK, France, Switzerland, Australia, Canada against district rules; "If the second-biggest district can't audit their PII on new… products… how are smaller districts going to do this?" — [The 74, 2024-07-10](https://www.the74million.org/article/chatbot-los-angeles-whistleblower-allhere-ai/)
- EdWeek's five lessons: define the problem before buying ("this is what happens when you let the technology lead you"); vet vendors (about a third of new AI edtech startups may not survive a year); start small (Georgia Tech's comparable chatbot had been in development since 2016); write data ownership/storage/penalties into contracts; do not abandon AI — [EdWeek, 2024-07-08](https://www.edweek.org/technology/los-angeles-unifieds-ai-meltdown-5-ways-districts-can-avoid-the-same-mistakes/2024/07)

**Byju's (India; peak 2022, collapse 2023–24)**
- $22 B peak valuation; 19 acquisitions for $3.63 B while the core product was "notoriously poorly received"; documented "predatory" sales practices toward parents; investors overlooked "completed audits and related party transactions"; US arm bankrupt, founder ousted February 2024, Enforcement Directorate lookout notice. Lesson drawn: move "from chasing unicorns to the slow, steady work of proving efficacy" — [Cornell SC Johnson, 2024-07-01](https://business.cornell.edu/hub/2024/07/01/what-investors-should-learn-from-fall-edtech-unicorn-byjus/)

### Inferences
- Transferable lessons, in order of relevance to Cornerstone: (1) young children on screens for long stretches is what parents revolt against — a paper-first design sidesteps the Summit/AltSchool failure mode entirely; (2) minimum-necessary data in every model call and no PII in prompts is not compliance theatre — it is the exact failure that ended LAUSD's project (Cornerstone's Rule 6 already encodes this); (3) scope creep to "one assistant for everything" fails; narrow, well-defined jobs succeed; (4) vendor solvency is a real risk for anything bought — a school that owns its data in its own Postgres is insulated from the AllHere/Byju's outcome.
- Every failure lacked independent efficacy evidence at the point of scale. The absence of outside evaluation is itself a warning sign, including for products the school might buy.

### Gaps
- Forbes' 2019 AltSchool post-mortem and the Seattle Times/AP Summit piece could not be opened; AltSchool's final outcome (sale of schools, Altitude Learning) is not documented from an opened source.
- No independent quantitative learning-outcome evaluation of Summit Learning was found (Chalkbeat states none existed in 2018).

## KQ4. Alpha School (Texas) "2 Hour Learning": what is claimed, what is independently verified, what is not

### Takeaway
Alpha's claims (top 1–2% nationally; "2.6× faster" MAP growth; two hours of academics with "guides" instead of teachers) rest entirely on internal analyses of NWEA MAP data; the only *public* state data on Alpha-model schools (Arizona charter; a Texas partnership) show results far below Alpha's predictions; five of six state charter boards reviewed by ProPublica rejected it as untested. It is a high-tuition, affluent-population model with unresolved conflicts of interest, now funded in Texas through a voucher route that bypassed pedagogical review.

### Cited Findings
- Model: founded 2014 (Austin); 13 campuses by 2026; tuition $10,000–$75,000; software-based adaptive learning (IXL/Khan-like) ~2 h/day; "guides" supervise rather than instruct; performance claims "rely on internal analyses of NWEA's Measures of Academic Progress (MAP) assessments, and the underlying data has not been independently reviewed" — [Wikipedia (raw wikitext read for citations)](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Alpha_School&action=raw); [Wikipedia article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_School)
- Operational detail (India-based GenWise, 2025-07-13): iXL and proprietary AlphaReads, eye-tracking and screen recording; academics 8:30–noon; guides paid $60k–$150k at ~5:1 ratios on the main campus; motivation via "Alpha bucks" (~10 cents each) and "Dojo Points"; the "2.6× faster MAP growth" claim compares Alpha students' improvement rate to national norms — [GenWise Substack](https://genwise.substack.com/p/the-alpha-school-educational-model)
- ProPublica/Texas Tribune investigation (2026-09-02): of six states reviewed (TX, AZ, AR, NC, PA, UT) **only Arizona approved** a charter; objections: replacing certified teachers, doubt that content is mastered in 2 h/day, model tested only on affluent students, unverified enrolment projections, and the Prices staying on the Arizona board while their companies were paid. **Public results:** the Arizona charter predicted 65% ELA proficiency and achieved 28%; predicted 60% maths and achieved 10%; a Texas Preparatory School partnership Alpha described as reaching "50–60% passing" moved the "approaches grade level" share only from 19% to 21%. Justin Reich (MIT): "I don't believe their claims that typical students will see rapid improvements." After the Texas charter rejection, Alpha became eligible for state voucher money through the comptroller's four-criterion check that "bypass[ed] curriculum and pedagogical review"; MacKenzie Price has given $1.5 M+ to Gov. Abbott and the Prices $2 M+ to voucher advocates — [ProPublica, 2026-09-02](https://www.propublica.org/article/alpha-school-texas-vouchers-ai)
- Pennsylvania rejected the Unbound Academic Institute application in 2025 as "untested" and misaligned with state standards — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_School)
- Former students interviewed by critic Benjamin Riley (2026-09-23) describe frequent software malfunction, computers "nuked" (locked to the app) when daily accuracy targets were missed, tracking of students into "Rocket Ship"/"Pirate Ship"/"Omega" groups, and guides with "very little power". **These are anecdotal accounts on a critic's blog, not verified data.** — [Cognitive Resonance Substack, 2026-09-23](https://buildcognitiveresonance.substack.com/p/fourth-report-former-alpha-school)

### Inferences
- Alpha demonstrates the *ceiling* of software-first schooling under the most favourable conditions (selected, affluent, incentivised students; 5:1 staffing; $1 B backing) and still cannot show public, independently verified outcomes. For a mixed-ability Pune primary school, "guides instead of teachers" has no evidentiary support; the Arizona and Texas public data argue against it.
- What Alpha does get right for Cornerstone's purposes is operational: mastery data per child per day, adults freed from whole-class lecture to work 1:1 — the same structure Cornerstone's evidence graph is meant to enable, with paper and human teachers rather than screens and guides.

### Gaps
- Search snippets referenced a WIRED investigation (IXL terminating Alpha's account, July 2025) and a 404 Media report (AI lesson plans flagged internally as "more harm than good"; scraping Khan Academy) — I could not open either (search budget exhausted; Wikipedia's raw text did not cite them). **Unverified.**
- No peer-reviewed or state-audited evaluation of any Alpha campus exists in what I could open.

## KQ5. Capability and cost trajectories that matter for a paper-first physical school

### Takeaway
Cost per unit of capability is collapsing (≈13×/year for fixed benchmark performance per Epoch, Sept 2026), making per-worksheet vision reads and per-child structured decisions effectively free at school scale; but *children's* handwriting remains the weakest documented capability — the two benchmarks that exist show frontier models degrade markedly on handwriting versus print and were "inadequate" zero-shot on children's maths handwriting. Indian-language ASR is now good on adult Hindi/Marathi speech (5–10% WER, best systems) but no benchmark covers child speech in noisy classrooms. On-device 2–3 B multimodal models are real in 2026, and structured-output/agentic tooling is mature.

### Cited Findings

**Multimodal reading of children's handwriting**
- FERMAT benchmark (Nath, Bathina, Khan, Khapra — IIT Madras; submitted 2025-01-13): 2,244 handwritten maths solutions, 609 problems, grades 7–12, 43 human transcribers; tasks = error detection / localisation / correction. Best: Gemini 1.5 Pro 0.63 balanced accuracy detection, 0.43 localisation, **0.76–0.77 correction**; GPT-4o 0.65 / 0.45 / 0.66; small open models near chance (Phi-3.5: 0.52 / 0.06 / 0.15). "Models demonstrated improved accuracy when handwritten inputs were replaced with printed text or images" — handwriting itself is the bottleneck — [arXiv 2501.07244 abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.07244); [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07244v1)
- Children's handwriting specifically (AIED 2024, July 2024; Pereira Júnior et al.): 251 images of children's handwritten mathematical expressions; GPT-4V, LLaVA 1.5, CogVLM; "performance without fine-tuning or zero-shot learning remains inadequate"; failure drivers: poor handwriting, partial erasures, GPT-4V safety refusals; conclusion: domain fine-tuning needed — [Springer, AIED 2024](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-64315-6_28?error=cookies_not_supported&code=af9a6fe6-5076-4bcb-877b-1614857174dc)
- VEHME (submitted 2025-10-26): a fine-tuned open VLM with an expression-aware visual prompting module for open-form handwritten maths, evaluated on AIHub and FERMAT; "state-of-the-art performance among open-source models and approaches the accuracy of proprietary systems" (no numbers in abstract) — [arXiv 2510.22798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22798)
- SMART-840 (NeurIPS 2024 Datasets & Benchmarks): 840 Mathematical Kangaroo problems, grades 1–12; frontier LVLMs do *relatively worse on younger children's puzzles* and "there is no significant correlation between the reasoning capabilities of AI models and that of young children" — [arXiv 2406.15736](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.15736)

**Indian-language speech recognition, real-world conditions**
- Voice of India benchmark (AI4Bharat/Khapra group; submitted 2026-04-21, final 2026-07-03): 306,230 utterances, 536 h, 36,691 speakers, 15 languages, 139 regional clusters, *unscripted telephonic* speech; 14 ASR systems evaluated; "most models exceed a WER of 20… a threshold often associated with practical usability". Best: Sarvam Audio (lowest WER in 13/15 languages) — **Hindi 5.0%, Marathi 9.4%**; Saarika 2.5 Hindi 6.2 / Marathi 10.0; Gemini 3 Pro Hindi 6.0 / Marathi 10.7; some systems fail outright (AssemblyAI >100 WER; GPT-4o Mini 295.9 on Gujarati). Noise matters: between best and worst audio-quality quartiles Gemini 3 Pro rises 13.42% → 23.44% WER, ElevenLabs Scribe 15.31% → 25.20%; district-level WER ranges ~4% (Nainital) to 44% (Mannarakkat) — [arXiv 2604.19151](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.19151)
- AI4Bharat (IIT Madras): IndicWav2Vec → IndicWhisper → IndicConformer covering all 22 scheduled languages; 300k h raw speech, 6k h transcribed, 6.4k h mined pairs from 400+ districts; benchmarks Vistaar, IndicSUPERB, Lahaja, Svarah; roadmap includes 8 kHz telephony and offline operation — [AI4Bharat ASR](https://ai4bharat.iitm.ac.in/areas/asr)
- SraVaani 1.0 (submitted 2026-08-08): FastConformer ASR for 65 Indian languages/dialects trained on 31,263 h labelled + 31,255 h unlabelled speech; "lowest WER on a large number of language-dataset pairs" on eight benchmarks — [arXiv 2608.08235](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08235)

**Small / on-device models**
- Gemma 4 released 2026-04-02 under Apache 2.0; the E2B variant is ~1.3 GB (Q4), ~2.3 B effective parameters, runs on 6 GB-RAM phones at 12–20 tokens/s on recent Snapdragon chips, with text + image input — [dev.to walkthrough, April 2026](https://dev.to/alichherawalla/how-to-run-gemma-4-on-your-android-phone-in-2026-locally-no-cloud-1e39) (community source)
- Pixel 10 (Tensor G5) runs Gemma 4 E2B fully offline for text, image and audio (transcription, object identification) with "internet-independent reasoning"; trade-off acknowledged: a 2.3 B model "cannot match the sheer analytical depth" of cloud models — [eWeek, 2026-07-17](https://www.eweek.com/news/google-pixel-10-offline-ai-gemma-model-2026/)

**Agentic workflows and structured "decision" outputs**
- Anthropic's engineering guidance (2024-12-19) distinguishes *workflows* ("LLMs and tools are orchestrated through predefined code paths") from *agents* (LLMs "dynamically direct their own processes"), recommends five composable patterns (prompt chaining, routing, parallelisation, orchestrator-workers, evaluator-optimiser) and "only increasing complexity when needed" — "optimizing single LLM calls with retrieval and in-context examples is usually enough" — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-effective-agents)
- Structured outputs (GA across Claude API, Bedrock, Vertex, Foundry) give schema-guaranteed JSON via constrained decoding; no numeric range or string-length constraints, no recursion — those checks must live in code — [Claude docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/structured-outputs)

**Cost per token / per unit capability, 2023–2026**
- Epoch AI (2025-03-12): price to reach a fixed benchmark level fell 9×–900× per year depending on task; GPT-4-level on GPQA Diamond fell from $37.50/MTok (Mar 2023) to $0.18 (Feb 2025), ~40×/year; general-knowledge (MMLU) prices fell faster than advanced maths; fastest drops were recent so persistence is uncertain — [Epoch AI data insight](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/llm-inference-price-trends)
- Epoch AI, *The plunging price of thought* (Emberson & Roodman, 2026-09-22): cost of a given level of AI performance "has fallen about 47% per quarter since 2023, or 13× per year" — vs 1.84×/yr for DNA sequencing, 1.51× compute, 1.16× lithium batteries; maths problems fall fastest (50–52%/quarter); example as read: OpenAI o3 at 75% on GPQA Diamond ≈ $0.30/question vs a later model at identical performance for $0.0004 — a 725× drop in under 18 months; caveats: "benchmaxxing", three-year window, real users do not continuously switch models — [Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/publications/the-plunging-price-of-thought)
- Du, *Tiered Super-Moore's Law* (submitted 2026-03-30; OpenRouter 318 models + Epoch 3,237 models): price half-life 1.10 years for economy-tier and 1.55 years for mid-tier models; ~600× cumulative decline since 2020; but flagship *reasoning* models carry a "reasoning premium averaging 31.5 times non-reasoning prices" with nearly flat decline; May 2024 marked the shift to competition-driven pricing; market concentration (HHI) fell 4,558 → 2,086 in three years — [arXiv 2603.28576](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28576)
- Gundlach, Lynch, Mertens, Thompson, *The Price of Progress* (submitted 2025-11-28, rev. 2026-03-23): frontier price-performance improves ~5–10×/year across knowledge, reasoning, maths and software benchmarks; algorithmic efficiency alone ~3×/year; counter-trend: frontier *operating* costs rising 3–18×/year due to larger models and reasoning; benchmark gains do not map 1:1 to real-world value per dollar — [arXiv 2511.23455](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.23455)
- Current list prices (read 2026-09-26): Claude Haiku 4.5 $1/$5, Sonnet 5 $2/$10, Opus 5.5 $4/$20, Fable 5.1 $10/$50 per MTok; batch −50% — [claude.com/pricing](https://claude.com/pricing); Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite $0.30/$2.50, Gemini 3.1 Pro $2/$12; batch −50%; Google has pre-announced increases from 1 Jan 2027 — [ai.google.dev](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)

### Inferences
- Back-of-envelope at 2026 prices: a worksheet photo plus structured read is ~1.5–3k tokens; at Sonnet-class batch prices (~$1/$5) that is well under $0.02 per sheet; 300 children × 5 sheets/week × 40 weeks ≈ 60k sheets/year ≈ on the order of $1,000/year at frontier-tier prices, and a fraction of that on economy tiers or after another year of 13×/yr decline. Compute is not the constraint for Cornerstone; reading accuracy on *young* children's writing and the cost of *human verification* are.
- Everything published on handwriting is grades 7–12 or unspecified "children"; nothing benchmarks Nursery–Grade 4 letter/number formation, reversals, erasures and crossings-out. Cornerstone's own captures-plus-teacher-corrections corpus is therefore a genuinely scarce asset — and the only route to a fine-tuned or few-shot reader for its population (the AIED 2024 authors' conclusion).
- The reasoning-tier price premium is not falling (Du); the economy and mid tiers are. Design for the mid tier: code does arithmetic, marking and lookup; the model reads and describes. That is already Cornerstone's stated principle, and the pricing data vindicates it.
- Indian-language ASR is ready for *adult* speech (educator dictation, parent voice notes) but unproven for children in noisy rooms; a 15–25% WER at low audio quality is unusable for assessment.

### Gaps
- No benchmark of VLM accuracy on Nursery–Grade 4 handwriting (digits, letters, number sentences) in any script was found; nothing on Devanagari/Marathi children's handwriting.
- No child-speech or classroom-noise ASR benchmark for Indian languages found; Voice of India is adult telephonic speech.
- OpenAI's pricing page returned 403; only Anthropic and Google price points were opened.
- Independent (non-vendor) measurements of structured-output error rates on real school documents were not found.

## KQ6. The teacher's role: what is plausibly automatable by 2028, what is not, the "orchestrator/learning designer" thesis and its critics, and implications for hiring, team structure and training

### Takeaway
By 2028 the automatable core is document work: drafting materials, first-pass marking of closed items, transcription, report drafting, data assembly — the tasks that already dominate teachers' actual AI use (report cards and lesson planning were ~90% of OpenAI's teacher "time-saving" messages). Diagnosis, relationship, motivation, safeguarding and the judgement calls about a particular child are not, and the evidence base argues they should be actively protected against "deskilling through disuse". The orchestrator thesis is real but conditional: teachers only orchestrate if the system leaves them the decisions.

### Cited Findings

**What teachers actually delegate today**
- US teachers' top AI uses: preparing to teach (37% monthly+), making worksheets/activities (33%), modifying materials (28%); weekly users report 5.9 h/week saved — [Gallup, 2025](https://news.gallup.com/poll/691967/three-teachers-weekly-saving-six-weeks-year.aspx)
- OpenAI's own telemetry Jan–Jul 2026: report cards and lesson planning ≈1.7 M of 1.9 M time-saving task messages — [WinsSolutions, 2026-08-26](https://www.winssolutions.org/openai-chatgpt-for-teachers-expansion/)
- In the EEF RCT teachers used ChatGPT for "one or two activities rather than across the lesson: most commonly for creating questions or quizzes or finding new ideas" and rarely refined outputs with follow-up prompts — [EEF/NFER, Dec 2024](http://www.mmiweb.org.uk/gaiandre/literature/EEF.pdf)
- Interviews with 22 teachers in an early-adopter US district (submitted 2025-10-21): adoption framed as *relief* from unsustainable workload versus fear of *displacement*; teachers "calibrated their use based on alignment with professional values", protecting relational and judgement work; district policy and professional norms shaped decisions more than tool features — [arXiv 2510.18296](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18296)

**What the causal evidence says AI does well *for* teachers/tutors**
- AI coaching the adult in real time (Tutor CoPilot) lifted elementary maths mastery +4 pp, +9 pp with weaker tutors, at ~$20/tutor/year, by increasing probing questions and cutting generic praise — [Stanford NSSA](https://nssa.stanford.edu/studies/tutor-copilot-human-ai-approach-scaling-real-time-expertise)
- Nigeria: gains occurred with *teachers* introducing topics, guiding the AI interaction and monitoring for hallucination; authors cannot separate the chatbot from the teacher-led programme — [World Bank, 2025-06-25](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/developmenttalk/addressing-the-learning-crisis-with-generative-ai--lessons-from-)

**The "orchestrator / learning designer" thesis**
- OECD 2026 frames the goal as GenAI that "can… improve human teaching while preserving teacher's agency" — [Cedefop record](https://www.cedefop.europa.eu/en/tools/vet-toolkit-tackling-early-leaving/resources/oecd-digital-education-outlook-2026); Cukurova's replacement / complementarity / synergy paradigms, with "synergy" ("teacher and AI together achieve more than either could alone") as the target and replacement as the default risk — [Sadki on the OECD launch, 2026-03-24](https://redasadki.me/2026/03/24/oecd-digital-education-outlook-2026-how-can-ai-help-human-beings-learn-and-grow/)
- Brookings: prepare teachers to "teach with and through AI"; co-create tools with educators — [Brookings, 2026-01-14](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/a-new-direction-for-students-in-an-ai-world-prosper-prepare-protect/)
- McKinsey 2020 (pre-2024): freed hours should go to "activities that lead to higher student outcomes" — the origin of the "teacher as facilitator" framing that Greene criticises as "repackaging failed personalized learning approaches" — [EdWeek 2020](https://www.edweek.org/technology/artificial-intelligence-could-free-up-13-hours-a-week-for-teachers-report-finds/2020/01); [Curmudgucation 2020](https://curmudgucation.blogspot.com/2020/01/mckinseys-new-baloney-sales-pitch-for.html)

**The critics: deskilling, agency, metacognition**
- Biström & Mollwing, *AI in education and the future of teachers' meaningful work* (Frontiers in Education, 2026-06-08): "pedagogical deskilling through disuse" — when teachers are less involved in core instructional decisions "the knowledge and judgment those practices sustain may gradually erode"; tasks at risk: diagnosis of understanding, sequencing, individual feedback, planning/assessment cycles; irreducibly human: relational care, contextual judgement, creative design, collegial mentorship, interpreting algorithmic recommendations against educational purposes. Conceptual paper — "does not draw on new empirical classroom data" — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2026.1844085/full)
- Gašević (OECD launch): performance gains without learning gains; "metacognitive laziness" when learners "think less about their own thinking" — applies to teachers as learners too — [Sadki, 2026-03-24](https://redasadki.me/2026/03/24/oecd-digital-education-outlook-2026-how-can-ai-help-human-beings-learn-and-grow/)
- Brcic & Frljic, *The Effortless Trap* (submitted 2026-06-24): the question is *placement* not permission — "if letting AI in makes the task feel effortless, it is in the wrong place"; secure the unaided first attempt and the final test, allow guarded scaffolding between — [arXiv 2606.26181](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26181)
- UNESCO (Sept 2025): "protecting teachers' irreplaceable roles" is a named priority; only half of higher-ed respondents feel confident about AI's pedagogical foundations — [UNESCO DLW 2025](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unesco-convenes-global-leaders-digital-learning-week-2025-shape-inclusive-human-centred-futures-ai)
- Brookings' bottom line: at present risks to children's foundational development "overshadow" benefits — [Brookings, 2026-01-14](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/a-new-direction-for-students-in-an-ai-world-prosper-prepare-protect/)

**Cautionary precedent for "guides instead of teachers"**
- Alpha's public results (AZ 28%/10% proficiency vs 65%/60% predicted; TX partnership 19%→21%) and regulators' rejection of the untested model — [ProPublica, 2026-09-02](https://www.propublica.org/article/alpha-school-texas-vouchers-ai); Summit-era research: personalised-learning students "less likely to say there was an adult at school who knew them well" — [Chalkbeat, 2018 (pre-2024)](https://www.chalkbeat.org/2018/12/19/21106372/could-the-forces-that-fought-the-common-core-bring-down-personalized-learning/)

### Inferences
- Plausibly automatable by 2028 (high confidence): reading and first-pass marking of closed-response paper work; generation of practice items and differentiated worksheets with code-side verification; drafting of parent notes, report cards and IEP-style summaries from structured evidence; transcription and translation of educator dictation; assembly of class-level dashboards. Each has 2024–26 evidence of acceptable quality *with a human approval step*.
- Not automatable by 2028 (high confidence): reading a child's affect and attention in the room; deciding what a particular child needs *today*; motivation and belonging; safeguarding judgements; parent relationships; the "is this evidence trustworthy?" call on ambiguous handwriting. The evidence for AI here is either absent or negative (Turkey; Summit; Alpha public data).
- The orchestrator thesis holds only under Cukurova's "synergy" condition. A system that presents *decisions to approve* keeps teachers exercising diagnosis (the skill most at risk of disuse); a system that *acts and reports* erodes it. Cornerstone's teacher-approval gates are therefore not merely a safety feature — they are the mechanism that keeps the teacher a teacher.
- Hiring implication: the scarce skill is *diagnostic judgement plus willingness to read evidence and correct the machine* — not prompt-writing (teachers do not iterate with chatbots and should not have to). Early-years and primary specialists with strong subject-pedagogical knowledge in maths and early literacy, comfortable with mastery data, matter more than "AI-savvy" generalists.
- Team-structure implication: the evidence supports a split between (a) qualified educators who diagnose, decide and relate, and (b) assistant/associate educators and one operations coordinator who run capture, approval queues, printing and data hygiene — analogous to Tutor CoPilot's finding that AI most lifts *less-experienced adults*, which is where an assistant-educator tier gains most. It does not support Alpha-style replacement of qualified teachers by guides.

### Gaps
- Selwyn's 2025 "Machine teaching?" (British Journal of Sociology of Education) and the MDPI "cognitive orchestrator" paper (Educ. Sci. 16(1):57) both returned 403; their arguments are known only from search listings and are not cited above.
- No study was found that measures teacher deskilling empirically over time; the deskilling literature is conceptual (Frontiers 2026 acknowledges this).
- No India-specific evidence on teacher AI adoption, CBSE/NCERT guidance or Maharashtra policy was opened (search budget exhausted).

## Council position — recommendations for Cornerstone (with confidence and evidence)

1. **Keep paper-first for children; put AI on the adults' side of the desk.** (Confidence: high.) Every documented parent/student backlash was against screen-first schooling for young children (Summit, AltSchool); Brookings 2026 judges GenAI risks to foundational development to outweigh benefits today; the strongest positive K-12 result for young children (Tutor CoPilot) had AI coaching the adult, not facing the child. Cornerstone's design already sits on the right side of this line — do not move it.

2. **Build the evidence engine and the approval gates; buy the models.** (Confidence: high.) Token cost for fixed capability is falling ~13×/year (Epoch, Sept 2026) and economy/mid-tier prices halve every 1.1–1.6 years (Du, 2026); there is no reason to train or host a frontier model. What no vendor sells is one Postgres of evidence per child with teacher approval on every consequential step and self-improvement from corrections — and LAUSD shows what buying that as a product looks like. The scarce asset is Cornerstone's own captures-plus-corrections corpus.

3. **Treat reading young children's handwriting as the open technical risk, and instrument it.** (Confidence: high that it is the risk; medium on how fast it closes.) FERMAT shows frontier models lose accuracy on handwriting versus print even for grades 7–12; the only children's-handwriting study (AIED 2024) called zero-shot performance "inadequate". Cornerstone should measure per-cell read precision/recall against teacher gold continuously (`engine eval read_cells` is the right shape), keep the three-signal distinction (blank / wrong / wrong-with-working) as a human-verified label, and plan for few-shot or fine-tuned readers on its own data by 2027–28 rather than waiting for vendors.

4. **Never let a model be the last word on a number, and never let a child's tutor give answers.** (Confidence: high.) Turkey RCT: unguarded GPT-4 cut unassisted exam scores 17%; the same model constrained to hints erased the harm; Harvard: the tutor that doubled learning gave "one step at a time" from a supplied answer key. If Cornerstone ever adds a child-facing component, it must be answer-key-grounded, hint-only, and placed after an unaided attempt (Brcic & Frljic's placement rule). Code computes; the model reads and describes — the existing constitution is correct and should be defended as evidence-based, not stylistic.

5. **Design for teachers who will not prompt.** (Confidence: high.) EEF/NFER transcripts: refinement prompts were "rare"; teachers take the first output and edit by hand. Every AI step should surface a finished, checkable artefact (a marked sheet, a proposed prescription, a draft note) with approve/correct controls — never a chat box. Corrections then feed the corpus (recommendation 2).

6. **Budget teacher time savings at ~30 minutes/week per automated task stream, not 13 hours.** (Confidence: medium-high.) The only high-security RCT found 25 min/week on one subject's planning; self-reports (Gallup 5.9 h/week among weekly users) and McKinsey's 2020 projection (13 h/week) are not causal. Expect real savings to accumulate across several streams (marking, differentiation, reports, parent notes) over 2026–28 rather than arrive at once; redeploy them explicitly into 1:1 diagnostic conversations, which is what teachers in the EEF trial did with saved time.

7. **Hire for diagnostic judgement and evidence literacy, not "AI skills".** (Confidence: medium-high.) The tasks AI cannot do by 2028 (reading the child, deciding today's next step, motivation, safeguarding) are the tasks the Frontiers 2026 analysis says are most at risk of atrophy if a system pre-decides them. Recruit primary/early-years teachers with strong maths and early-literacy pedagogical content knowledge who are willing to read a child's evidence graph and overrule the machine; screen for that in interviews with real (anonymised) sheets and prescriptions.

8. **Structure the team as qualified educators + assistant educators + one operations coordinator; reject the "guides instead of teachers" model.** (Confidence: medium-high.) Alpha School's public state data (AZ 28%/10% proficiency vs 65%/60% predicted; TX 19%→21%) and five of six state rejections show software-plus-guides has no independent support even with 5:1 staffing and affluent students. Tutor CoPilot shows AI lifts less-experienced adults most (+9 pp), so an assistant-educator tier running capture, approval queues, printing and 1:1 practice under a qualified teacher's decisions is where AI leverage is highest and safest. One coordinator owns data hygiene, PII discipline and vendor solvency risk (LAUSD/AllHere, Byju's).

9. **Make "no PII in any prompt or log" a hard, audited property, and keep data residency in the school's own store.** (Confidence: high.) The LAUSD chatbot failed on exactly this: PII in every prompt, whole-household records processed, offshore servers, no district audit capability. Cornerstone's Rule 6 and single-Postgres design are right; add a recurring automated check that model calls carry `child_id` and images only, and keep the checked output in `STATE.md`.

10. **Wait on Indian-language speech for children; adopt it now for adults.** (Confidence: medium.) Best systems reach ~5% (Hindi) / ~9% (Marathi) WER on adult telephonic speech, but WER roughly doubles at low audio quality and no child-speech classroom benchmark exists. Use ASR for educator dictation of observations and parent voice notes (with approval), revisit child oral-reading assessment in 2028 when Indian child-speech data may exist (AI4Bharat roadmap includes telephony and offline).

11. **Plan for on-device/offline reading by 2027–28 but do not depend on it in 2026.** (Confidence: medium.) 2–3 B multimodal open models now run offline on phones (Gemma 4 E2B, April 2026) with acknowledged depth trade-offs; for a paper-first school the value is resilience (capture and pre-read during outages) rather than cost. Keep adapters (Ring C) thin so a local model can be swapped in behind the same interface when its read accuracy on Cornerstone's gold set passes the bar.

12. **Publish Cornerstone's own evidence, on its own children, before making any efficacy claim — and demand the same of anything bought.** (Confidence: high.) Every failure studied (Summit, AltSchool, LAUSD, Byju's, Alpha) scaled without independent evidence; every vendor offer in 2026 lacks it. Cornerstone's Rule 8/12/14 ("every works is a command"; goals with 100% scenarios; `engine done`) already encodes the discipline; extend it to learning outcomes with a pre-registered internal comparison (e.g., mastery growth on the school's own rung ladder plus an external norm-referenced check) and an annual external review, so the school can say what worked with the same rigour it demands of others.
