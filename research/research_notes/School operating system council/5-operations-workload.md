Prepared 26 September 2026. Every URL below was opened during this research; where a primary source was unreachable (OECD.org, RAND.org, EEF web pages, ScienceDirect, SAGE, UNESCO returned 403/404) that is stated and the number is marked **unverified** or moved to Gaps. ## Key question 1 — Where teachers' time goes, and which of it is administrative

### Takeaway
Across England, the OECD and the US, full-time teachers report roughly 41–52 hours a week, of which only about 20–24 hours is teaching; general administration, data entry, marking and behaviour follow-up are the tasks teachers themselves most often call excessive (in England 2024: 74% say "too much" administration, 53% too much data work, 60% too much behaviour follow-up, 43% too much marking). Reliable Indian private-school time-use data does not exist; the one field study found low-fee private schools push non-teaching work into non-class hours rather than eliminating it.

### Cited Findings

**England — Working Lives of Teachers and Leaders (WLTL), wave 3 (fieldwork Jan–May 2024, n = 10,020; report June 2025)**
- Mean weekly hours (reference week), teachers: 48.7 (2022), 49.4 (2023), 48.1 (2024); full-time teachers 51.9 / 52.4 / 51.2. Leaders: 56.8 / 57.4 / 56.6 (full-time 57.5 / 58.2 / 57.6). — [DfE/IFF/UCL WLTL wave 3 research report, Figs 3.2–3.3](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/68f11c49e8e4040c38a3cfbf/Working_lives_of_teachers_and_leaders_wave_3_-_research_report.pdf)
- Full-time teachers taught 23.3 hours in the reference week (24.0 in 2023); 64% of teachers spend at least half their working time on non-teaching tasks (76% secondary, 54% primary, 53% special/AP). — [same](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/68f11c49e8e4040c38a3cfbf/Working_lives_of_teachers_and_leaders_wave_3_-_research_report.pdf)
- Share of classroom teachers and middle leaders saying they spend *too much* time on a task (2024, vs 2023): general administrative work 74% (75%) — defined as "communication, paperwork, work emails and other clerical duties" — with only 4% saying too little; following up behaviour incidents 60% (57%; 50% in 2022); recording/inputting/monitoring/analysing pupil data 53% (56%); individual planning 44% (48%); marking 43% (46%). — [same, Fig 3.8](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/68f11c49e8e4040c38a3cfbf/Working_lives_of_teachers_and_leaders_wave_3_-_research_report.pdf)
- Primary teachers are more likely than secondary to say too much time goes on lesson planning (53% vs 36%) and communication with parents/guardians (52% vs 47%); secondary more on marking (48% vs 40%) and data (55% vs 52%). Senior leaders: 53% too much time on in-school administration, 52% on responding to government policy changes. — [same](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/68f11c49e8e4040c38a3cfbf/Working_lives_of_teachers_and_leaders_wave_3_-_research_report.pdf)
- Only 22% agree they have an acceptable workload (17% in 2022 and 2023); 68% disagree; 34% feel they have sufficient control over workload (26% in 2022, 30% in 2023). — [same, Fig 3.10](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/68f11c49e8e4040c38a3cfbf/Working_lives_of_teachers_and_leaders_wave_3_-_research_report.pdf)

**England — Teacher Workload Survey 2019 (NFER for DfE, Oct 2019)**
- Teachers and middle leaders: 49.5 hours in the reference week, down 4.9 from 54.4 in 2016; primary 50.0 (55.5 in 2016), secondary 49.1 (53.5). — [TWS 2019 research brief](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5d9f383f40f0b6685970864a/Teacher_workload_survey_2019_brief.pdf)
- Reductions since 2016 (primary / secondary): marking −2.2 / −1.7 h; individual planning −1.3 / −1.1 h; pupil supervision −1.8 / −1.3 h; data recording/inputting/monitoring −0.5 / −0.6 h. Despite this, "most primary teachers and middle leaders said they still felt they spent too much time on planning, marking and data management, alongside general administrative work". — [same](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5d9f383f40f0b6685970864a/Teacher_workload_survey_2019_brief.pdf)
- 75%+ of respondents said their school had changed data tracking, behaviour, marking/feedback and appraisal approaches to cut workload, but typically only ~20% or fewer of those felt the change actually reduced workload, "with notable minorities reporting they had actually added to their workload"; the exception was primary marking/feedback policy changes, which 40% said reduced workload. — [same](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5d9f383f40f0b6685970864a/Teacher_workload_survey_2019_brief.pdf)
- Workload "a very serious problem": 21% primary, 37% secondary (49% and 56% in 2016). 70% of primary teachers and middle leaders strongly disagreed that they could complete their workload within contracted hours. — [same](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5d9f383f40f0b6685970864a/Teacher_workload_survey_2019_brief.pdf)

**OECD TALIS 2018 (England national brief, Jerrim & Sims for DfE, June 2019)**
- Full-time lower-secondary teachers in England: 49.3 h/week in 2018 (48.2 in 2013) vs OECD average ~41; primary 52.1 h, more than any participating country except Japan (56). — [DfE TALIS 2018 research brief](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/919065/TALIS_2018_research_brief.pdf)
- Primary teachers in England: 24 h teaching + ~31.9 h non-teaching tasks; lower-secondary 20.5 h teaching + 32.7 h non-teaching. OECD averages: ~21.5 h teaching, 26 h non-teaching. — [same](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/919065/TALIS_2018_research_brief.pdf)
- 53% of primary and 57% of lower-secondary teachers in England felt workload unmanageable (lower-secondary up from 51% in 2013); "more than half … reported that they spent too long upon marking and administrative work". Heads: ~57 h (primary), ~62 h (lower-secondary). — [same](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/919065/TALIS_2018_research_brief.pdf)

**OECD TALIS 2024 (results released Oct 2025) — primary chapter unreachable (oecd.org returned 403); figures below are from press coverage of the OECD release**
- OECD average total working hours 41 h/week; OECD average classroom teaching 23 h. Singapore 47 h total, 18 h teaching, 6.4 h marking, 4 h administrative work; 53% of Singapore teachers cite administrative work and 49% marking as sources of stress; 27% "a lot" of stress vs OECD 19%; 75% of Singapore teachers report using AI at work. — [Mothership, 7 Oct 2025](https://mothership.sg/2025/10/talis-2024-survey-teachers-work-hours-ai/)
- Alberta: 47 h/week vs OECD ~42; 52% cite too much marking, 51% responsibility for students' wellbeing, 48% discipline as stressors; 42% report high work-related stress vs 19% globally. — [Alberta Teachers' Association, 28 Oct 2025](https://teachers.ab.ca/news/talis-results-reveal-profession-under-pressure)

**United States — RAND State of the American Teacher**
- RAND's 2025 report "To Make Teaching Sustainable, Help Teachers Balance Work and Personal Demands" (published 14 Oct 2025) finds poor work–life balance correlates with lower wellbeing and that fewer than half of teachers say their school or district is making efforts to help them balance work and life. — [ERIC record ED676945](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED676945)
- The companion report "Teacher Well-Being, Pay, and Intentions to Leave in 2025" (24 June 2025) covers "sources of job-related stress, pay, hours worked, and teachers' intentions to leave". — [ERIC record ED677008](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED677008). The widely reported figure of ~49 hours/week, about 10 above contract, comes from RAND pages that returned 403 in this research and is therefore **unverified** here.

**India**
- RAISE study (Udaipur district, Rajasthan; 100+ teachers and principals across 40 government and low-fee private schools): ~75% of government teachers do non-teaching work *during class time* vs 13% in low-fee private schools; 83% of government principals vs 6% of private principals; 94% of private principals say they distribute non-teaching work to teachers in non-class hours or free periods. Government teachers taught roughly 2.5–3 hours a day, 50–60% of prescribed time. Tasks named: mid-day meal and milk supervision, election duty, child height/weight documentation, WhatsApp-based administrative orders, Shala Darpan portal data entry. — [Ideas for India, Indira Patil, 22 Oct 2020](https://www.ideasforindia.in/topics/human-development/teacher-accountability-non-teaching-work-over-classroom-engagement.html)
- Ahmedabad government schools: teachers pulled for census, election duty, RTE 12(1)(c) enrolment paperwork, dignitary visits and "WhatsApp-based administrative summons"; "often, the work they were called away for had nothing to do with education". No hours quantified; private-school comparison only anecdotal. — [The Wire, Vernekar & Singhal, 26 June 2018](https://m.thewire.in/article/education/non-teaching-duties-government-school-teachers-india)

### Inferences
- The stable pattern across three surveys and two decades is: teaching ≈ 20–24 h; everything else ≈ 25–32 h. The "administrative" bucket teachers resent most is not one task but the clerical residue of many: communication, paperwork, email, data entry. A school OS that removes double entry and outbound paperwork targets the single most-cited grievance (74%).
- Marking hours fell in England (−2 h) mainly through *policy* change (less written marking), not technology; teachers who saw real relief were those whose school changed the marking policy, not those given a tracking system.
- Whole-school "workload" initiatives frequently add work (TWS 2019: ~20% felt relief, a notable minority felt more load). Any new Cornerstone process must retire an old one.
- For a Nursery–Grade 4 school, WLTL's primary-phase profile is the relevant one: planning (53%) and parent communication (52%) are the "too much" items, ahead of marking (40%).
- The Indian private-school evidence suggests admin is displaced into unpaid time rather than removed; Cornerstone should expect hidden after-hours reporting/WhatsApp load unless it measures it.

### Gaps
- OECD TALIS 2024 full report (hours by task, OECD averages for admin/marking, change vs 2018): oecd.org blocked (403); only press coverage cited.
- RAND 2024/2025/2026 primary reports (hours worked, stress sources): rand.org blocked; ERIC abstracts carry no numbers.
- No Indian study quantifying private-school teachers' hours by task was found (UNESCO 2021 "No Teacher, No Class" returned 404; the World Bank time-on-task work could not be retrieved). Treat any Indian hour figure as unverified.
- No study of WhatsApp-group load on Indian private-school teachers was found.

## Key question 2 — The SIS / MIS / school-OS landscape: coverage, competency models, openness, pricing, criticisms

### Takeaway
Every mainstream platform is an operations record (roster, attendance, fees, gradebook, parent app); competency or evidence stores exist only as "standards tagging" (Toddle, Seesaw, Compass) or add-on reporting, not as a longitudinal per-child skill model. Pricing is almost universally undisclosed; API openness is claimed but rarely documented in public; the biggest incumbent (PowerSchool) suffered a 2024 breach through a single credential on a support portal without MFA.

### Cited Findings

**North America / global**
- PowerSchool claims "over 60 million students in 90 countries"; product families include SIS, Enrollment, SchoolMessenger communications, Schoology LMS, Performance Matters assessment, Naviance, ERP/HR, analytics and PowerBuddy AI, marketed as a "K-12 Connected Operating System". — [powerschool.com](https://www.powerschool.com/)
- Bain Capital announced a take-private of PowerSchool in June 2024, completed 1 Oct 2024. — [Wikipedia, Bain Capital (reached via the PowerSchool redirect)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PowerSchool)
- Breach: PowerSchool discovered on 28 Dec 2024 the "unauthorized exfiltration of personal information from certain PowerSchool SIS environments" via its PowerSource support portal using "a single compromised credential"; notified customers 7 Jan 2025; paid a ransom ("we made the decision to pay a ransom because we believed it to be in the best interest of our customers"); reported an extortion attempt against individual districts on 7 May 2025; "cannot confirm precise numbers". — [PowerSchool incident page](https://www.powerschool.com/security/sis-incident/)
- Reporting puts the breach at "more than 62 million students and 9.5 million teachers"; PowerSource "did not support multi-factor authentication at the time"; stolen data reportedly included grades, attendance, SSNs, medical alerts, and "information about parental access rights, restraining orders, medication schedules"; the company would not disclose the ransom or evidence of deletion. — [TechCrunch, 10 Mar 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/10/what-powerschool-isnt-saying-about-its-massive-student-data-breach/)
- Infinite Campus: privately held (Minnesota); "11 million students, 3,200 districts, 46 states, 10 state agencies"; add-ons for food service, messenger, online registration, payments, analytics, learning suite; no pricing or API standards on the homepage. — [infinitecampus.com](https://www.infinitecampus.com/)
- Veracross: independent/private-school platform ("one-person, one-record"), "3300+ schools" in 70+ countries, admissions–academics–billing–fundraising–portals on one database, "200+ integration partners" via its API; pricing by demo only. — [veracross.com](https://www.veracross.com/)

**UK**
- Arbor (part of The Key Group): "over 12,500 schools and trusts"; attendance, behaviour, assessment, comms, parent app, payments, HR/finance, and TimeTabler within its suite; vendor ROI claim of "117+ minutes saved per staff member per week"; pricing undisclosed. — [arbor-education.com](https://arbor-education.com/)
- SIMS: created 1982–84 by a teacher at Lea Manor High School; commercialised 1988; Capita from 1994; sold (as ESS) to Montagu in Dec 2020; the planned SIMS 8 was judged technically/commercially unfeasible in 2021 and ESS joined ParentPay Group, announcing SIMS Next Gen; "just over 50% market share" of UK primary and secondary schools, down from earlier dominance; a 2005 Becta report found maintenance costs 2.5–3× 1999 levels. — [Wikipedia, School Information Management System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_Information_Management_System)
- TimeTabler lists SIMS, Arbor, iSAMS and Bromcom among the MIS it links to (the only verified fact on Bromcom obtained; bromcom.com pages returned no readable content). — [timetabler.com](https://www.timetabler.com/)

**Australia**
- Compass (Melbourne, private): "5,000 schools globally"; attendance, curriculum/task management with "skill tracking" and semester reporting, wellbeing/behaviour incidents, parent portal and app, finance/billing, events; timetabling is *not* native — it integrates external systems such as Edval; cites "90% engagement through the parent portal"; recent acquisitions Clipboard, Inklass, Music Monitor. — [compass.education](https://www.compass.education/)

**India / India-origin**
- Toddle: founded by "five engineer-turned-educators" at an international school in India; "1,200+ schools across 100+ countries"; covers unit planning, portfolios, progress reports, gradebook, attendance, family communication; frameworks IB PYP/MYP/DP, Cambridge, UbD, US/UK/Australian/Canadian; evidence can be tagged to IB "standards and practices"; "open APIs for more real-time integrations"; ISO 27001, SOC 2 Type 2, COPPA, FERPA; per-user licensing (students, teachers, leaders), plans "Planning Pro", "360", custom. — [Toddle FAQ](https://www.toddleapp.com/faqs/)
- Teachmint: Capterra rating 4.8/5 on 74 reviews; "starting at $5 per user per year"; modules attendance, fees, report cards, LMS, parent app, admissions; reviewer cons include "flexibility of customisation has certain limitations" (May 2023), "there is no delete button in some areas" (Apr 2023), "integrating Teachmint with existing systems … can be complex" (Jan 2023). — [Capterra](https://capterra.com/p/163105/Teachmint/). Its current homepage leads with "Teachmint X" interactive-panel hardware and an "EduAI" assistant rather than the ERP. — [teachmint.com](https://www.teachmint.com/). (Reports of 2023 layoffs were seen only in search snippets — **unverified**.)
- LEAD Group: "9,000+" schools, "65,000+" teachers, "4.1 million+" students, 400+ cities; sells an integrated system — curriculum, Techbook digital content, student and teacher apps, "Nucleus" school ERP, teacher training, assessments (brands LEAD, PINNACLE+, PROPEL, Nursery–XII); outcome claims ("79% learning mastery", "2X better performance in board exams") carry no cited methodology; per-student fees not disclosed. — [leadgroup.co.in](https://leadgroup.co.in/)
- Entab CampusCare: fee/billing, attendance, examination, transport, library, admissions, inventory, mobile apps, AI dashboard/chatbot and an LMS ("Yooreka", CBSE-aligned, Classes 1–10); case studies include Aditya Birla Group schools (12 states, 45,000 students); no pricing or API documentation on the site. — [entab.in](https://www.entab.in/)
- Fedena (Foradian Technologies, Bengaluru): "40,000+" institutions in "200+" countries (vendor claim); admissions, attendance/timetable, fees, exams/gradebook, HR/payroll, online classes; API via an "API Playground"; pricing "depends on features and plugins". — [fedena.com](https://fedena.com/). The open-source edition (Ruby on Rails, Apache 2.0) has 547 stars, 558 forks, 22 open issues and is "now maintained by the open source community". — [github.com/projectfedena/fedena](https://github.com/projectfedena/fedena)
- Kriyo (Little Soldiers Solutions Pvt Ltd): preschool/daycare app — QR/geo-QR attendance and authorised pickup, daily reports, photos/videos, meal/sleep/diaper logs, "learning progress documentation", admissions pipeline, fee invoicing with automated reminders and receipts, live bus tracking, CCTV integration, multi-centre dashboard; App Store rating 4.8 (228 ratings); app free (school issues login); "5,700+ schools across 117 countries". — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/in/app/kriyo-preschool-daycare-app/id1384125107)
- Classplus: white-label apps for coaching institutes ("Aapki Coaching Aapki App"), content, tests, fee collection; "1 Lakh+ coaching institutes" in 1,100+ cities; investors include Sequoia, Tiger Global, GSV, Blume, Alpha Wave; not a school SIS. — [classplusapp.com/about-us](https://classplusapp.com/about-us)

### Inferences
- **No surveyed product holds a longitudinal, per-child skill/evidence model as its core record.** The closest are Toddle (evidence tagged to IB standards), Seesaw's standards-based reporting tier and Compass's "skill tracking"; all are reporting overlays on a marks-and-portfolio store. Cornerstone's Ring A (registry, ladder, evidence, results) is therefore genuinely new ground and the right thing to build rather than buy.
- Commodity operations (attendance, fees, transport, notices) are solved many times over; Kriyo already covers Cornerstone's early-years/primary needs. The buy/integrate question is about data portability, not features.
- Indian school-ERP vendors publish no APIs, no pricing and, in LEAD's case, unsourced outcome claims; Teachmint has pivoted to hardware. Lock-in and vendor-viability risk is high; export-ability should be a contract condition.
- The PowerSchool breach shows that a support/maintenance back door with one credential can expose every customer; for a self-built system the equivalent risks are shared admin accounts, no MFA, and over-collection of fields.

### Gaps
- Bromcom and ESS SIMS official pages returned no content / 403; no MIS market-share dataset for England could be opened (bringmoredata.co.uk did not resolve).
- Pricing: only Teachmint ($5/user/year, Capterra) and Toddle (per-user model, no numbers) disclose anything; all others are quote-only.
- API openness for Entab, LEAD, Kriyo and Classplus could not be verified from public documentation.
- Bain's purchase price for PowerSchool (reported ~$5.6bn) was not in any opened source — **unverified**.

## Key question 3 — Timetabling and staffing tools: what schools use and what fails

### Takeaway
Generation is a solved problem — FET (free, AGPL) and the commercial solvers all produce a conflict-free grid in minutes; the recurring pain is *daily operation*: cover for absent staff, room changes and publishing changes to parents, which is why every commercial product sells a substitution/cover module. Cornerstone's FET plan is sound if the constraint model is kept simple and a cover workflow is designed separately.

### Cited Findings
- FET: version 7.10.5 released 11 Sep 2026, GNU AGPL v3; "simple timetables" solve in under 5 minutes, typical complex ones in 5–20 minutes, "extremely difficult timetables … a matter of hours". — [lalescu.ro/liviu/fet](https://lalescu.ro/liviu/fet/)
- FET's algorithm since 2007 is "a new heuristic algorithm (based on recursive swapping of activities)" that replaced an earlier genetic algorithm inadequate for complex timetables. Constraints are split into time (teacher max/min days, max gaps per day/week, min days between activities, max hours continuously, preferred starting times, consecutive activities, same starting time) and space (home rooms, preferred rooms, room unavailable, building changes); a 100% weight is a hard constraint, lower weights are soft; a command-line build "fet-cl" exists; output is XHTML plus CSV import/export. Common causes of failed generation: impossible min-days constraints on split activities, too few periods, over-constrained 100%-filled grids, too-strict gap/day limits for heavily booked teachers. — [FET manual (timetabling.de mirror)](https://timetabling.de/manual/FET-manual.en.html)
- aSc Timetables (Bratislava): automatic generation ("over 5,000,000 possibilities"), online substitution/cover suggesting replacements and updating registers, notifications of daily changes to parents, class register, room booking, natural-language AI edits; claims "200,000 schools worldwide"; version 2027 released 10 Jun 2026; free unlimited trial, prices not listed. — [asctimetables.com](https://www.asctimetables.com/)
- Untis (Stockerau, Austria): timetabling, substitution planning, room booking and break supervision, digital class register, WebUntis and mobile app for parents/students; "30,000+" institutions, 35+ languages; quote-based pricing. — [untis.at](https://www.untis.at/en)
- TimeTabler (UK): TimeTabler + Options (elective blocks) + StaffCover; "2,200+" schools in 90+ countries, largest MAT installation "over 40 secondary schools"; links with SIMS, Arbor, iSAMS, Bromcom; flexible-working features "developed with England's Department of Education". — [timetabler.com](https://www.timetabler.com/)
- Edval now redirects to Tes Timetable, which claims to evaluate "up to 30000 alternate timetables … per second" and sells a "Choices" add-on for electives; no school counts or pricing. — [tes.com/for-schools/timetable](https://www.tes.com/for-schools/timetable). Compass integrates external timetablers such as Edval rather than generating natively. — [compass.education](https://www.compass.education/)

### Inferences
- For a Nursery–Grade 4 school with class-teacher model and few specialist rooms, FET's constraint palette is more than sufficient; the risk is over-specifying soft constraints (FET's own failure list) rather than solver capability.
- The features commercial vendors monetise beyond generation — cover, room booking, publishing changes — are exactly what n8n + the school's own tables can provide (a "cover" flow is trigger + sequence + notify, within Ring D's rule). FET's fet-cl and XML/CSV make it scriptable.

### Gaps
- No independent study of timetabling-tool failure rates or user satisfaction was found; the Cal Poly student review of FET (usability complaints) returned 503 and is **unverified**.
- Untis/aSc/TimeTabler/Tes pricing is quote-only.

## Key question 4 — Parent communication: platforms, what parents read, and evidence on outcomes

### Takeaway
The best causal evidence favours *few, specific, per-child messages* — a weekly one-sentence teacher note (Kraft & Rogers) or automated alerts on missed work/absences (Bergman & Chan) — over feeds and points; the commercial platforms optimise for reach and engagement metrics, not for outcomes. Cornerstone should route a small number of evidence-backed messages through the channel parents already have (Kriyo/WhatsApp) rather than add another app.

### Cited Findings

**Evidence**
- Kraft & Rogers (Economics of Education Review 47, 2015): weekly one-sentence individualised teacher-to-parent messages in a high-school credit-recovery programme cut the share failing to earn credit from 15.8% to 9.3% (a 41% reduction, +6.5 percentage points on an 84.2% control pass rate); the effect came mainly from preventing drop-out; messages about *what to improve* outperformed praise; teachers spent "less than thirty minutes each week to write two messages for approximately 15 students in each of their two classes"; cost per additional credit "less than one-tenth the typical cost per credit earned for the district". — [Kraft & Rogers 2015 (author PDF)](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6297c2b5c8bc35721cc7a65c/t/685d1b50a1fe313d1d091445/1750932305165/Kraft+Rogers+2015+The+underutilized+potential+of+teacher-to-parent+communication+EER.pdf)
- Bergman & Chan (CESifo WP 6493, 2017; RCT in 22 middle and high schools): automated text alerts to parents about missed assignments, grades and absences "reduces course failures by 38% and increases class attendance by 17%"; no effect on standardized tests; largest gains for below-average-GPA and high-school students; mechanism: alerts "change parents' beliefs about their child's performance and increases parent monitoring". — [RePEc abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/p/ces/ceswps/_6493.html)

**Platforms**
- ClassDojo: points/"Big Ideas" behaviour feedback, Class Story photo feed, portfolios, messaging with translation into 190+ languages, Dojo Islands, Dojo Tutor; "more than 45 million students and parents"; free for teachers, paid "ClassDojo Plus" for parents. — [classdojo.com](https://www.classdojo.com/)
- Seesaw: PreK–6 portfolios showing "year-over-year growth", activities, family messaging with translation, "standards-based reporting" only in the paid "Instruction and Insights" tier; free individual-teacher tier, school/district licences; integrations Google Classroom, Schoology, Canvas, Clever, ClassLink; SOC 2, GDPR, FERPA, COPPA. — [seesaw.com](https://seesaw.com/)
- ParentSquare: unified K-12 comms (posts, alerts, two-way messaging, translation 190+ languages, forms, sign-ups, attendance notices, payments, SIS sync); "22 million+ students" across "42,000+ schools"; claims "over 99% contactability" and that families need no app or login; pricing by package, undisclosed. — [parentsquare.com](https://www.parentsquare.com/)
- Remind: phone-based two-way teacher–family messaging with translation into 90+ languages; "nearly 30 million users in 80% of US schools", "over 2 million teachers"; "Remind is now part of the ParentSquare family" (date not stated); Remind Chat free for teachers. — [remind.com](https://www.remind.com/)
- Famly (Copenhagen; UK/US/Germany): nursery management — registers, daily logs (sleep, nappies, medication), observations and assessments against curricula, parent app and payments, staff rotas with automatic ratio calculation, occupancy/enquiries; "per child" pricing; "1 million+" users; marketed as removing "tedious admin tasks". — [famly.co](https://www.famly.co/)
- Kriyo (Cornerstone's incumbent) — see Q2: daily reports, photos, notices, fees with automated reminders, bus tracking; app free for parents. — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/in/app/kriyo-preschool-daycare-app/id1384125107)
- WhatsApp: the only opened evidence concerns Indian *government* schools, where WhatsApp is the channel for administrative orders and summons to teachers. — [Ideas for India](https://www.ideasforindia.in/topics/human-development/teacher-accountability-non-teaching-work-over-classroom-engagement.html); [The Wire](https://m.thewire.in/article/education/non-teaching-duties-government-school-teachers-india)

### Inferences
- What worked in trials is *content*, not channel: specific, actionable, per-child, improvement-focused, low frequency, cheap for the teacher (<30 min/week). A photo feed or points stream has no comparable causal evidence; it does generate the "share pressure" and after-hours messaging that WLTL respondents count as administration (52% of primary teachers already say too much time on parent communication).
- Cornerstone's engine can generate the Bergman-style alert ("this week: two blanks in exchange/regroup; here is what to try") from Ring A evidence, and the Kraft-style teacher sentence can be drafted by a model and approved by the educator — a message is judgment, so a model may draft it and a human sends it (constitution: model where judgment is needed, code where correctness is needed).
- Vendor "engagement" metrics (ParentSquare's contactability, Compass's 90% portal engagement) measure reach, not reading or action; no vendor publishes read rates.

### Gaps
- Kraft & Monti-Nussbaum (2017) summer texting (reported +0.21–0.29 SD reading comprehension for grades 3–4, none for grades 1–2) could not be opened (SAGE 403) — **unverified**.
- Bergman (2021) on how rarely parents log into portals and Kraft & Bolves (2022) on a communication-app RCT could not be located via RePEc search pages — not cited.

## Key question 5 — What has demonstrably reduced workload (and whether technology helps)

### Takeaway
The only interventions with documented hour reductions are *policy subtractions*: fewer/shorter written marking, fewer data-collection points and reporting cycles, planning taken over or bought in, fewer meetings and out-of-hours communication rules; the DfE's own evaluation puts the gain at about 1.7 "unnecessary hours" a week (8.75 → 7.07) and the national taskforce target at 5 hours in three years. Technology is repeatedly reported as adding load when layered on top of existing requirements.

### Cited Findings
- England's Workload Reduction Taskforce (initial recommendations 15 Jan 2024) set the ambition to "reduce teachers' and leaders' working hours by 5 hours per week within 3 years", covering data collection, marking, planning, technology, parent communication, email and out-of-hours work. — [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/workload-reduction-taskforce-initial-recommendations)
- Evaluation of the DfE workload reduction toolkit (Education Development Trust, July 2023; 1,002 survey responses, 80 schools recruited, 76 giving qualitative detail, 5 interviews): leaders and teachers estimated teachers worked 8.75 "unnecessary hours" (time on tasks not felt to benefit learners) per week in 2018 vs 7.07 at survey time. Reduction strategies: "reducing reporting cycles", "reducing the amount of data inputting required by teachers", "switching to in-class verbal feedback and either reducing or completely eliminating written marking", "purchasing plan schemes", "school leadership taking on the role of planning lessons", fewer/online parents' evenings, fewer meetings, policies on evening/weekend/holiday communications. Persistent problems: perceptions of Ofsted requirements, teachers playing multiple roles, referral waits. Only two schools completed small-scale trials; sample self-selected. — [DfE/EDT report](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/64b928e7ef5371000d7aef84/Workload_reduction_in_schools_in_England.pdf)
- Marking: the Independent Teacher Workload Review Group (26 Mar 2016) held that written marking "has become disproportionately valued by schools and has become unnecessarily burdensome for teachers", that effective marking is "meaningful, manageable and motivating", that feedback "can often be achieved without extensive written dialogue or comments", and that "deep marking" has "no broadly agreed definition … or any theoretical underpinning"; 53% of Workload Challenge respondents found marking's depth and frequency burdensome. — [gov.uk page](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/reducing-teacher-workload-marking-policy-review-group-report); [report PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/511256/Eliminating-unnecessary-workload-around-marking.pdf)
- EEF "A marked improvement?" (Oxford team for EEF, April 2016): "the quality of existing evidence focused specifically on written marking is low"; careless mistakes should be marked differently from errors of misunderstanding (hints/questions for the latter, mark-as-wrong without the answer for the former); grades on every piece may reduce impact; pupils need time to respond; "acknowledgement marking" ("tick and flick") is unlikely to enhance progress — "mark less … but mark better"; evidence on dialogic/triple marking rests almost entirely on surveys; marking was "the single biggest contributor to unsustainable workload" in the 2014 Workload Challenge (44,000+ responses); over 50% of surveyed teachers identify and correct errors on all or most pieces. — [EEF review PDF](https://www.angelsolutions.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2017/11/EEF_Marking_Review_April_2016.pdf)
- Data: "Making data work" (Teacher Workload Advisory Group, 5 Nov 2018) — government accepted all recommendations. — [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/teacher-workload-advisory-group-report-and-government-response). The response table records: DfE "should not request regular attainment data from schools unless they meet a trigger for intervention", should automate the MIS→DfE data flow "reducing the amount people need to do themselves", Ofsted should ask "whether schools' attainment data collections are proportionate … and are sustainable for staff", and performance management "should not be based on teacher generated data and predictions, or solely on the assessment data for a single group of pupils". — [response table PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/753496/Workload_Advisory_Group_response_table_final.pdf)
- Technology as added load: a 2022 EdWeek survey found nearly two-thirds of teachers, principals and district leaders "exhausted by the volume of tools" and 79% overwhelmed by tech adoption over the prior two years; a 2021 University of Seville review of 15 years of LMS research found tech-related stress rising "exponentially"; 2025 reporting found LMS platforms were "simply another thing on teachers' plates", with schools requiring both digital uploads and paper lesson plans. — [Edutopia, Andrew Simmons, 6 Feb 2026](https://www.edutopia.org/article/technology-integration/)
- The DfE has run an EdTech Survey 2020–21 (24 Jun 2021) and a Technology in Schools Survey 2022–23 (28 Nov 2023; 156 pp) on technology decision-making, use and barriers; only the landing pages were readable here, so their percentages are not cited. — [EdTech survey](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/education-technology-edtech-survey-2020-to-2021); [Technology in schools 2022–23](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/technology-in-schools-survey-report-2022-to-2023)

### Inferences
- The engine's design already embodies the two best-evidenced marking moves: distinguish mistake from error (three signals: blank / wrong / wrong-with-working) and replace acknowledgement marking of computable answers with code. The time saved should be claimed only after measurement (rule 8).
- The "Making data work" principles translate directly for Cornerstone: collect a datum once, where it is created; never ask an educator to re-key; publish the purpose of every collection; never use pupil data in educator appraisal.
- Technology reduces workload only when it *retires* a task (paper register, hand-written report, WhatsApp broadcast). If Kriyo, WhatsApp, the engine and a printed report all survive, load rises.

### Gaps
- The EEF ChatGPT lesson-preparation trial (reported ~31% planning-time saving) could not be opened (EEF 403) — **unverified**.
- No RCT of an SIS/MIS or parent-app on teacher hours was found.
- DfE EdTech/Technology-in-schools survey percentages on workload not extracted (PDFs not opened).

## Key question 6 — Adoption failures and their lessons

### Takeaway
The graveyard has three recurring causes: (1) data collected without visible consent and purpose (inBloom, Summit, PowerSchool); (2) the platform replacing rather than serving teachers and children (Summit, Bridge's scripts, AltSchool's playlists); (3) buying before the product or the operating model existed (LAUSD's unfinished Pearson curriculum, AltSchool's economics, Byju's sales-led growth). None failed for lack of features.

### Cited Findings
- **inBloom (2014)**: $100m from Gates and Carnegie to "store, clean, and aggregate a wide range of student information" and expose it to district-approved vendors; Louisiana (May 2013) and Jefferson County, Colorado (Nov 2013 — despite an independent security review) withdrew after parent protest; New York's legislature (April 2014) barred the education department from contracting outside companies to store student data; CEO cited "generalized public concerns about data misuse" and that "building public acceptance … will require more time and resources than anyone could have anticipated"; Leonie Haimson: designed to share "very sensitive information with data-mining vendors, with no attention paid to parental notification or consent". Announced shutdown 21 Apr 2014. — [EdWeek](https://www.edweek.org/technology/inbloom-to-shut-down-amid-growing-data-privacy-concerns/2014/04)
- **AltSchool (2013–2021)**: founded 2013 by Max Ventilla; $33m (2014) then $100m (2015) raised; six schools by 2016; teachers and students built individual "playlists" and "students' progress was streamed to parents using a portal app"; stopped operating schools in 2019 and rebranded as Altitude Learning; per Wikipedia, ceased operations in 2025. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AltSchool). $174m of venture capital in total; schools handed to Higher Ground Education (2019) and the software sold to Higher Ground on 15 Jan 2021, leaving Altitude a "technology-enabled service organization". — [EdSurge, 15 Jan 2021](https://www.edsurge.com/news/2021-01-15-the-edtech-company-formerly-known-as-altschool-sold-its-tech-so-what-s-left). (Snippet claims of $150/student/year pricing and $7m revenue were not opened — **unverified**.)
- **Summit Learning (2018)**: nearly 100 students walked out of the Secondary School for Journalism, Brooklyn, on 5 Nov 2018; platform built by Summit Public Schools with Facebook engineers and CZI support, used by "about 380 schools nationwide"; complaints: hours "staring at one screen", having to teach themselves, easy cheating, glitches (report-card and scheduling errors), and data collected — "names, student ID numbers, email addresses, our attendance, disability, suspension and expulsion records, our race, gender, ethnicity and socio-economic status"; the school dropped the programme for grades 11–12 citing lack of teacher professional development; a Connecticut district had suspended it in 2017. — [EdSurge, 15 Nov 2018](https://www.edsurge.com/news/2018-11-15-dear-mr-zuckerberg-students-take-summit-learning-protests-directly-to-facebook-chief)
- **Bridge International Academies / NewGlobe**: founded 2008 (Nairobi); ~750,000 students across India, Kenya, Nigeria, Uganda, Liberia (Mar 2023); one administrator per school on a smartphone app, teachers reading scripted lessons, ~$6/month fees; funders include Gates Foundation, IFC, CZI, Khosla, CDC; Uganda's High Court (Nov 2016) allowed closure of 63 unlicensed schools; Kenya's High Court dismissed Bridge's defamation case against the teachers' union (Feb 2018); a 2023 Intercept report documented child sexual-abuse allegations and the World Bank's CAO opened an investigation in Dec 2020. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridge_International_Academies). UK MPs on the International Development Committee (Mar 2017) said teachers were "disempowered" by tablet scripts and questioned sustainability; Bridge's co-founder defended the "scientifically driven" model whose guides adapt to assessment data. — [Tes, 28 Mar 2017](https://www.tes.com/news/teachers-disempowered-highly-scripted-lessons-delivered-tablets). The lottery-based RCT (Gray-Lobe, Keats, Kremer, Mbiti, Ozier, June 2022) found two years at Bridge added 0.89 equivalent years of schooling for primary and 1.48 for pre-primary pupils — "among the largest observed in the international education literature" — but Bridge teachers earned about one-fifth of public-school pay, worked longer hours and were less experienced; classrooms had a full-length open window so an observer could see whether a teacher was teaching; NewGlobe later shut most of its Kenyan private schools to sell services to governments. — [Education Next, 8 Jun 2022](https://www.educationnext.org/dramatic-learning-gains-for-mcdonalds-of-african-private-schools-study-by-nobel-economist-finds/)
- **LAUSD iPads (2013–2015)**: plan for 650,000 iPads at "nearly $1.3 billion" ($500m devices + $800m connectivity); only 43,261 bought initially; bid specifications favoured Apple (FBI and SEC inquiries); Pearson curriculum chosen from samples; students bypassed security filters; spotty Wi-Fi; $768 iPads without keyboards vs ~$200 Chromebooks; Superintendent Deasy resigned Oct 2014. — [Governing, 11 May 2015](https://www.governing.com/archive/gov-tablets-los-angeles-ipad-apple-schools.html). Settlements: $6.4m from Pearson and $4.2m from Apple; Pearson "provided only sample units of curriculum, rather than a finished product" against a $200-per-device curriculum charge. — [Gizmodo, 29 Sep 2015](https://gizmodo.com/the-la-school-systems-1-3-billion-ipad-fiasco-comes-to-1733569377)
- **Byju's (2022–2026)**: peak valuation $22bn (Mar 2022); India's Department of Consumer Affairs raised concerns about "aggressive sales practices and deceptive marketing" (24 Jun 2022); WhiteHat Jr ads withdrawn after ASCI rulings; FY22 revenue ₹5,298 cr against a ₹8,245 cr loss, filed 22 months late; Deloitte resigned as auditor (2023); ~$2.8bn spent on about a dozen acquisitions; default on a $1.2bn Term Loan B and US Chapter 11 for the US unit (Feb 2024); NCLT insolvency (2024) over ₹158 cr BCCI dues, upheld by the Supreme Court on 23 Oct 2024; US courts found founders complicit in hiding $533m; app delisted from Google Play in May 2025 for unpaid AWS bills. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byju%27s); Inc42's year-end review adds 120+ tuition centres locked for unpaid rent, ~5,000 staff terminated and months of unpaid salaries, and lists "aggressive sales practices and mis-selling to parents" and CFO absence (2021–23) among root causes. — [Inc42, 16 Dec 2024](https://inc42.com/features/byjus-2024-review-collapse-edtech-giant-byju-raveendran/)
- **PowerSchool breach (2024–25)** — see Q2: a single compromised support-portal credential, no MFA on that portal, ransom paid, ~62m student records reported exposed. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/10/what-powerschool-isnt-saying-about-its-massive-student-data-breach/); [PowerSchool](https://www.powerschool.com/security/sis-incident/)

### Inferences
- Consent and legibility beat security certificates: Jefferson County left inBloom *after* a clean third-party security review. Parents object to opaque aggregation and third-party access, not to encryption levels. Cornerstone's parent-facing data notice — what is stored, who sees it, why, for how long — is as load-bearing as RLS.
- A platform that mediates *instruction* (Summit, Bridge scripts, AltSchool playlists) invites revolt from students and teachers even when test scores rise; a platform that mediates *evidence and administration* and leaves teaching to the educator does not carry that risk. Cornerstone's constitution ("a number is never a model's last word"; educators exercise judgment) is aligned, and should be stated publicly to parents and staff.
- Bridge's gains came with one-fifth pay, longer hours and surveillance architecture; the lesson for a "teacher's team" is that standardisation of *materials and evidence* can help, but standardisation of *the teacher* is what the profession rejects.
- Procurement before product (LAUSD) and sales before pedagogy (Byju's) both punish schools; Cornerstone should not sign multi-year Indian ERP contracts on demo promises, and should demand data export from day one.

### Gaps
- Chalkbeat/Hechinger case studies on Summit's later exits (e.g., Kansas, Cheshire) were not opened; only EdSurge's 2018 account is cited.
- AltSchool per-pupil economics, tuition and losses not verified.
- The CAO's final findings on Bridge (2024) were not opened.

## Key question 7 — The "teacher's team": restructured roles and evidence on assistants

### Takeaway
The evidence supports assistants and teams when they *supplement* the teacher and take structured or supportive tasks (EEF), and shows team-based staffing improves retention with modest, uneven learning effects (CRPE/ASU); the workload evaluations show that having someone formally responsible for workload, and shifting planning/administration to others, are the levers schools actually use.

### Cited Findings
- EEF "Deployment of Teaching Assistants" guidance (2025; systematic review of 52 studies): five recommendations — (1) deploy TAs so all pupils access high-quality teaching, (2) deploy TAs to scaffold learning and develop independence, (3) deliver "well-chosen, evidence based, structured interventions where appropriate", (4) prepare and train staff, (5) engage all staff in implementation. Core principle: TAs "supplement – not replace – the teacher", so "pupils who struggle most should spend at least as much time with the teacher as other pupils, if not more"; TAs "undertaking supportive tasks inside or outside lessons … should free up teachers to prepare or deliver high-quality teaching to those who need additional support"; interventions chosen on vendor or colleague say-so "may have no—or even negative—impacts". — [EEF guidance PDF](https://d2tic4wvo1iusb.cloudfront.net/production/eef-guidance-reports/teaching-assistants/deployment_of_teaching_assistants_-_guidance_report_v1.1.0.pdf); [ERIC record ED673291](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED673291)
- ASU Next Education Workforce team-based staffing (2–6 educators sharing one larger roster): early evidence from Mesa Public Schools (May 2024) reports higher retention especially for early-career teachers, longer career plans, greater satisfaction and higher evaluation ratings than non-teamed peers; explicitly preliminary. — [CRPE, May 2024](https://crpe.org/early-evidence-of-improved-educator-outcomes-in-next-education-workforcetm-models/)
- Laski, Clifton & Goldhaber (Aug 2026; one large Arizona district, five years, grades 3–8, difference-in-differences): "on average, we could not measure an effect of teaming on student test scores"; middle-school maths +0.08 SD (~2–3 months); teamed teachers ~7 percentage points less likely to leave their school (baseline turnover 27%) and received significantly higher evaluation ratings; implementation quality unobserved. — [CRPE, Aug 2026](https://crpe.org/team-based-teaching-improve-learning-arizona/)
- Schools in the DfE workload evaluation reduced load by "school leadership taking on the role of planning lessons", giving TAs additional duties, and by naming someone responsible for workload and wellbeing (analysed as a predictor of reduction). — [DfE/EDT 2023](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/64b928e7ef5371000d7aef84/Workload_reduction_in_schools_in_England.pdf)
- Bridge's model (one non-teaching "academy manager" per school running admissions, fees and attendance on a smartphone, teachers teaching from scripts) is the extreme case of separating operations from teaching. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridge_International_Academies)

### Inferences
- The EEF's condition — TAs free teachers *for* teaching, never replace them — is the design rule for Cornerstone's assistant-educator role: assistants run attendance, captures/scans, materials, notices and fee follow-up; the educator keeps every judgment about a child.
- Team-based staffing's clearest payoff is retention, which in a growing private school in Pune (hiring one new grade a year) is worth more than a small test-score effect.
- A named operations-and-data coordinator (the DfE lever) is the natural owner of the school OS and of the annual time-use measurement.

### Gaps
- The EEF headline "+3–4 months for structured TA interventions" appeared only in search snippets, not in the opened PDF text — **unverified**.
- Opportunity Culture / multi-classroom-leader evaluations (CALDER) could not be opened.
- No Indian example of a formal assistant-educator/coordinator restructuring with evidence was found.

## Council position (seat 5: operations and teacher workload)

1. **Build the per-child evidence and skill model; do not look for it in a product.** No surveyed SIS/LMS (PowerSchool, Infinite Campus, Veracross, Arbor, SIMS, Compass, Toddle, Teachmint, LEAD, Entab, Fedena, Kriyo) carries a longitudinal skill state as its core record; the nearest are standards tags on portfolios. *Confidence: high* (vendor documentation, Q2).
2. **Buy/keep commodity operations, on condition of export.** Kriyo already covers attendance, daily reports, fees, transport and notices for the current age range; retain it while it exports child-level data, and make an API or scheduled export a renewal condition. Replace it only if it cannot feed the one Postgres. *Confidence: medium* (Kriyo features verified; its API openness unverified).
3. **One child record, entered once.** Every tool must read from or write to Ring A; no educator re-keys a datum that exists elsewhere. This attacks the top grievance in every survey (74% "too much" general administration; 53% too much data work). *Confidence: high* (WLTL 2024, TWS 2019, "Making data work").
4. **Retire before you add.** For each engine/n8n flow that goes live, name the paper or WhatsApp task it removes and remove it. Technology layered on unchanged requirements is documented to raise load (EdWeek 2022 two-thirds "exhausted by tools"; LMS "another thing on teachers' plates"). *Confidence: high*.
5. **Parent messages: few, specific, per child, improvement-focused, educator-approved.** Generate Bergman-style alerts and Kraft-style one-sentence notes from evidence; send through the channel parents already use; cap frequency; no points, streaks or public feeds. *Confidence: medium-high for message design (two RCTs), medium for channel choice (no Indian read-rate data)*.
6. **Mark less, mark better — and let code do the acknowledgement.** Computable answers are marked by the engine; educators write feedback only where judgment is needed; mistakes are distinguished from errors (already the three-signal rule). Publish the marking policy so no one triple-marks to look thorough. *Confidence: high on the principle (EEF 2016, Review Group 2016), medium on hours saved (no trial)*.
7. **Adopt "Making data work" as school policy.** Two or three attainment snapshots a year at most for reporting, no collection without a stated use, automated flows from the record to any report, and pupil data never used in educator appraisal. *Confidence: high* (DfE accepted all recommendations; TWS shows marking/data policy is where relief actually came).
8. **FET is enough; design the cover workflow.** Keep constraints minimal (FET's own failure list is over-specification), script it via fet-cl/XML, and build absence-cover and change-publishing as n8n flows — the part commercial vendors charge for. *Confidence: medium* (FET docs; no independent failure data).
9. **Consent and legibility are survival conditions.** Publish a plain-language notice of what is stored, who sees it and why; MFA and per-person admin accounts; no names in prompts or logs (rule 6); minimal fields. inBloom died after a clean security audit; PowerSchool fell to one credential. *Confidence: high*.
10. **Never let the system script the educator.** The OS delivers evidence, drafts and materials; it never delivers the lesson or the verdict. Summit's walkout and Bridge's teacher backlash (despite large test gains) show the line. *Confidence: medium-high*.
11. **Roles: a teacher's team of three kinds.** Class educator (all judgments about children, feedback, conferences); assistant educator per class pair (attendance, captures/scans, materials, notices, structured practice under the educator's plan — EEF's "supplement, not replace"); one operations-and-data coordinator for the school (owns Kriyo/engine/FET, fees follow-up, annual time-use survey, workload accountability). Team-based staffing is associated with ~7 pp lower turnover. *Confidence: medium* (EEF 2025; CRPE 2024/2026; DfE 2023 lever).
12. **Measure the hours or the claim is void.** Run a WLTL/TWS-style reference-week time-use survey before launch and annually, with the DfE's "unnecessary hours" item, and set an explicit target (England's taskforce chose 5 hours/week in three years). *Confidence: high* (rule 8; DfE 2023 measured 8.75 → 7.07).
13. **Avoid**: multi-year contracts with Indian all-in-one ERPs that publish no API or pricing and whose businesses are unstable (Teachmint's pivot to hardware; Byju's collapse; LEAD's unsourced outcome claims); any parent-facing feature whose success metric is "engagement". *Confidence: medium*.
