# Curriculum and pedagogy systems: what Cornerstone's curriculum engine should borrow and avoid

Seat: curriculum and pedagogy, Cornerstone design council. Written 2026-09-26. Every URL below was opened by the researcher (WebFetch or curl) on that date; where a document could only be seen as a search-result snippet, or a number could not be confirmed on an opened page, it is marked "unverified". Cloudflare-blocked sources (ibo.org, tewhariki.tahurangi.education.govt.nz, journals.sagepub.com, educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk, support.khanacademy.org, pubpub.org, wiley.com, mitpress.mit.edu, learningtrajectories.org) are listed under Gaps and were replaced with open mirrors (ERIC full texts, DERA archive, NCERT PDFs) wherever one existed.

Scope convention: "Nursery–Grade 5" is the focus (India: Foundational Stage ages 3–8 and Preparatory Stage ages 8–11); each section ends with what changes for Grades 6–10.

## Key question 1 — Frameworks: what is each one's unit of curriculum, and how fine-grained is it?

### Takeaway
The frameworks split into two families: (a) outcome-coded systems whose unit is a coded, stage-specific statement (Cambridge learning objective, NCF competency, EYFS early learning goal, Singapore syllabus outcome) and (b) principle-and-process systems whose unit is a strand, phase or observation rather than a testable statement (IB PYP conceptual understandings in phases, Te Whāriki strands with 20 outcomes, Reggio documentation, Montessori lesson/material). India's NCF gives Cornerstone a ready three-tier hierarchy (Curricular Goal → Competency → Learning Outcome) with stage-specific codes, but at ~68 competencies for the whole Foundational Stage it is far too coarse to be the diagnostic grain; Cambridge (roughly 50–110 objectives per stage in maths) and Montessori tools (individual lesson/material with measurable "elements") show what a working grain looks like.

### Cited Findings

**IB Primary Years Programme (PYP)**
- The PYP serves ages 3–12; its subject areas are language, social studies, mathematics, science, technology, arts, and personal/social/physical education, organised through six transdisciplinary themes (Who we are; Where we are in place and time; How we express ourselves; How the world works; How we organize ourselves; Sharing the planet) and a ten-attribute learner profile — [Wikipedia: IB Primary Years Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IB_Primary_Years_Programme) (the IBO's own curriculum page returned HTTP 403; see Gaps).
- The PYP mathematics continuum is organised by five strands (Data handling; Measurement; Number; Pattern and function; Shape and space), each with "Conceptual understandings" and "Learning outcomes" stated per Phase 1–4 — not per age or year; a publisher alignment document tabulates every strand's conceptual understandings and learning outcomes under Phases 1–4 (no Phase 5 appears for maths) and maps them onto its PYP 1–5 books — [Haese Mathematics, "IB PYP Mathematics Scope and Sequence", last updated 26 May 2025](https://www.haesemathematics.com/media/W1siZiIsIjIwMjUvMDUvMjcvOWE1dnMzdG16bF9JQl9QWVBfc2NvcGVfYW5kX3NlcXVlbmNlXzIwMjUucGRmIl1d/IB%20PYP%20scope%20and%20sequence%202025.pdf?sha=c308474a2451de32).
- In 2025 the IB replaced the 2009 subject "scope and sequence" documents with "subject continuums" (phases, conceptual understandings, learning outcomes per strand); a PYP coordinator's blog compares "the 2009 scope and sequence" with "the 2025 subject continuum: PSPE" — [Inquiry into Learning blog, 24 May 2025](https://inquiryintolearningblog.wordpress.com/2025/05/24/ready-to-r-u-n-unpacking-the-ib-pyp-subject-continuums-updates-with-curiosity-collaboration-and-joy/).
- Schools have "until September 2027" to transition to the updated strands/scope and sequence; ManageBac notes most schools "work in Phases (as per IB recommendations)" while some use "Year Levels", and that "schools are encouraged to adapt the framework to their own context" — [ManageBac blog, 19 Sept 2025](https://www.managebac.com/blog/ib-pyp-updates-strands-scope-sequence).
- Toddle, the IB-licensed planning platform, auto-generates the programme of inquiry from unit planners (central idea, lines of inquiry, key concepts, ATLs, learner profile) and reports "coverage of ATLs, Specified Concepts, Learner Profile Attributes", i.e., coverage is tracked at the level of framework elements, not of learner mastery — [Toddle IB PYP page](https://www.toddleapp.com/ib-pyp/).

**Cambridge Primary and Cambridge Early Years**
- Cambridge Primary covers ages 5–11 in Stages 1–6, offers more than ten subjects "in any combination and adapted to suit your context", each with a curriculum framework of learning objectives, schemes of work, Progression Tests and Checkpoint — [Cambridge International: Cambridge Primary curriculum](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/programmes-and-qualifications/cambridge-primary/curriculum/).
- The current maths curriculum (0096, first teaching 2020/2021) has three strands — Number; Geometry and Measure; Statistics and Probability — plus "Thinking and Working Mathematically" characteristics, continuing into Cambridge Lower Secondary — [Cambridge International: Primary Mathematics](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/programmes-and-qualifications/cambridge-primary/curriculum/mathematics/).
- Granularity (previous 0845 framework, the only version I could open): "Each learning objective has a unique curriculum framework code, e.g. 1Nn1", sub-strands carry reporting codes used in Checkpoint feedback, Stages 1–2 are not externally assessed, and Problem solving "is not assessed separately"; Stage 1 Number objectives read like small steps ("1Nn3 Count objects up to 20, recognising conservation of number", "1Nn6 Begin partitioning two-digit numbers into tens and ones and reverse"). My regex count over the opened PDF found roughly 490 coded objectives across Stages 1–6 (about 55 in Stage 1, about 111 in Stage 6) — [Cambridge Primary Mathematics Curriculum Framework (with codes), school-hosted copy](https://chartwell.edu.rs/images/Primary_Maths_Curriculum_Framework.pdf). Counts are mine and approximate; the 0096 PDF was not retrievable (404).
- Cambridge Early Years serves ages 3–6 in three stages EY1–EY3 as a "holistic, play-based programme" with learning statements organised by areas of learning, feeding into Cambridge Primary — [Cambridge International: Cambridge Early Years](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/programmes-and-qualifications/cambridge-early-years/).

**Montessori and its record-keeping tools**
- Transparent Classroom ships lesson sets for "AMI Toddler, Primary, and Elementary and AMS Elementary", organised as "Areas, Groups, and Materials", records lesson presentations and observations with planning notes, offers standards alignment (CCSS, TEKS, state standards), tracks "concentration and build Montessori work curves", flags "gaps in lesson areas", is "used by over 3000 Montessori schools", and retains a child's records after they leave — [Transparent Classroom](https://www.transparentclassroom.com/). The commonly cited progress vocabulary (presented / practising / mastered) could not be confirmed on an opened page — unverified.
- Montessori Compass (launched 2012; "Montessori Compass 2.0" rebuilt in 2025) offers a "customizable Montessori Scope & Sequence, supporting programs from infant through age 12" — [Montessori Compass: About](https://www.montessoricompass.com/about/). Its curriculum is a four-level hierarchy — Category (e.g., Sensorial, Mathematics) → Sub-Category (e.g., "Visual Discrimination – Size and Dimension") → Lesson/Material (e.g., Pink Tower) → Element, a measurable outcome (e.g., "Uses smallest cube to indicate unit of difference") — across ten areas from Infant (birth–18 months) to age 12, mapped to US Common Core maths and ELA for K–6 — [Montessori Compass: Language Arts](https://www.montessoricompass.com/language-arts/). The "3,000+ lessons" figure appeared only in a search snippet — unverified.

**Reggio Emilia documentation**
- Pedagogical documentation "is a form of recording children's actions and words … in order to listen to and come to better know the child … and to co-construct curricular experiences with children"; it "aims to make children's learning, skills, strategies, processes and understandings visible". The same review states: "There is little empirical research directly measuring the impact of the Reggio Emilia approach for children's learning" — [The Education Hub (NZ), "The Reggio Emilia approach", 2019](https://theeducationhub.org.nz/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/The-Reggio-Emilia-approach.pdf).
- Carlina Rinaldi (Reggio pedagogista): documentation "does not mean to collect documents after the conclusion of experiences with children but during the course of these experiences"; "assessment is also an integral part of the learning and teaching process"; she warns of "the risk of considering testing as a tool of assessment" — [Rinaldi, "The Relationship between Documentation and Assessment", Innovations in Early Education vol. 11 no. 1, Winter 2004](https://reggioaustralia.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Reading-2-The-relationships-between-Documentation-and-Assessment-Rinaldi.pdf).

**New Zealand Te Whāriki and Learning Stories**
- Te Whāriki ("woven mat") was published in 1996 and revised in 2017; it is "a framework … rather than prescriptive methodology", with five strands (Wellbeing/Mana atua; Belonging/Mana whenua; Contribution/Mana tangata; Communication/Mana reo; Exploration/Mana aotūroa); the 2017 revision drew criticism for "lack of consultation" and earlier critiques "questioned insufficient research backing" — [Wikipedia: Te Whāriki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Te_Wh%C4%81riki) (official site returned 403).
- The 2017 revision cut learning outcomes "from 118 to 20", and defines assessment as making "valued learning visible"; the same article catalogues Learning Stories' documented weaknesses: inadequate observation guidance (Perkins 2013), a "celebratory" focus on strengths only, "snapshots of events, rather than demonstrating continuity in children's learning" (Stuart et al. 2008), and Blaiklock's critique of assessing dispositions "without attention to domain-specific knowledge" — [McLachlan, He Kupu, May 2018](https://www.hekupu.ac.nz/article/te-whariki-revisited-how-approaches-assessment-can-make-valued-learning-visible).
- Wanoa & Johnston (Victoria University of Wellington): "concerns have been raised about both the validity of learning stories … and also their reliability – the degree of consistency with which teachers construct learning stories from observational evidence (Blaiklock, 2008, 2010)"; they propose that "describing their challenges in learning stories could contribute to planning" and add peer review and multiple perspectives to the notice–recognise–respond cycle — [Wanoa & Johnston, NZ Annual Review of Education 24 (2019): 90–104](https://ojs.victoria.ac.nz/nzaroe/article/download/6333/5496/8635).
- The Education Hub's assessment guide: Learning Stories are "the most established form of assessment for ECE, backed by the Ministry of Education, ERO and academic" research, but "have also been criticised for a lack of validity, for focusing on one teacher's observations", and "other assessment strategies may also be required" for children needing intervention — [The Education Hub, "Introduction to Assessment in ECE"](https://theeducationhub.org.nz/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/Introduction-to-Assessment-in-ECE.pdf).

**UK EYFS and Development Matters**
- The statutory framework (version effective 1 September 2026): "There are seven areas of learning and development" — three prime (Communication and language; Physical development; Personal, social and emotional development) and four specific (Literacy; Mathematics; Understanding the world; Expressive arts and design). Early learning goals define "the level of development children should be expected to have reached by the end of the EYFS" but "should not be used as a curriculum"; they "support practitioners to make a holistic, best-fit judgement"; "Assessment should not involve … excessive paperwork … Practitioners are not required to prove this through collection of any physical evidence" — [DfE, EYFS statutory framework for group and school-based providers, from September 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a5f8e4fb00f3323bf1a23a1/EYFS_group_and_school_based_from_September_2026.pdf); listing page — [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/early-years-foundation-stage-framework--2).
- Development Matters (non-statutory; last updated 4 September 2023) is organised in three age bands — birth to three, 3- and 4-year-olds, reception — [GOV.UK: Development Matters](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/development-matters--2). The document itself: "It is not designed to be used as a tick list for generating lots of data"; it "guides, but does not replace, your professional judgement"; it carries "observation checkpoints" (e.g., "Around 12 months, can the baby choose between 2 objects"; "Towards their second birthday, can the child use up to 50 words?") that "can help you to notice whether a child is at risk of falling behind" — [DfE, Development Matters, Sept 2023 PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/64e6002a20ae890014f26cbc/DfE_Development_Matters_Report_Sep2023.pdf). The 2021 revision made "No major changes … to the curriculum guidance under the seven areas of learning" — [Foundation Years, July 2021](https://www.foundationyears.org.uk/2021/07/an-update-to-development-matters/).

**Singapore mathematics and the Concrete–Pictorial–Abstract (CPA) approach**
- Singapore's syllabus spans 12 years, is built around a five-component framework (concepts, skills, processes, metacognition, attitudes) centred on problem solving, is spiral ("concepts and skills in each content strand … are revisited and built upon at each level"), and uses the C-P-A approach in which teachers build "abstract mathematical concepts from everyday experiences and meaningful contexts, using concrete and pictorial representations" — [TIMSS 2015 Encyclopedia: Singapore](https://timssandpirls.bc.edu/timss2015/encyclopedia/countries/singapore/the-mathematics-curriculum-in-primary-and-lower-secondary-grades/). The MOE 2021 syllabus PDF returned 404 (see Gaps).
- NIE researchers: "The Concrete-Pictorial-Abstract (C-P-A) sequence is the key instructional strategy for the development of primary mathematics concepts in Singapore (MOE, 2007, 2012)", rooted in Bruner's enactive–iconic–symbolic modes; but "the way to go about teaching and learning with the C-P-A sequence is unclear", "The sequence of occurrence of the various interactions is not linear and highly fluid", concrete and abstract representations may be "presented simultaneously", and "an array of combinations of C-P-A sufficed; C-A, A-C, P-A, A-P, C-P, P-C" — [Chang, Lee & Koay, The Mathematics Educator 17(1&2), 2017, pp. 1–28](https://math.nie.edu.sg/ame/matheduc/tme/tmeV17_1/paper1.pdf).

**India: NCF for the Foundational Stage 2022 (NCF-FS)**
- NCF-FS is "the first ever integrated Curriculum Framework for children between ages 3-8 in India", the first stage of the 5+3+3+4 structure. Its Learning Standards are four tiers: Aims; Curricular Goals ("statements that give directions to curriculum development … specific to a Stage"); Competencies ("learning achievements that are observable and can be assessed systematically … expected to be attained by the end of a Stage"; curriculum developers "can adapt and modify"); Learning Outcomes ("granular milestones of learning and usually progress in a sequence leading to attainment of a Competency"; teachers "should have the autonomy to define Learning Outcomes") — [NCERT, NCF for Foundational Stage, 20 Oct 2022 (PDF)](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCF_for_Foundational_Stage_20_October_2022.pdf); the chapter map (Sections 2.1–2.5, Annexure 1 for the full illustrative outcomes) — [NCERT flipbook p. 49](https://ncert.nic.in/flipbook/NCF/National_Curriculum_Framework_for_Foundational_Stage_2022/files/basic-html/page49.html).
- Counts (from the opened PDF): 13 Curricular Goals (CG-1 … CG-13) across five developmental domains — Physical; Socio-emotional and Ethical; Cognitive; Language and Literacy; Aesthetic and Cultural — plus "Positive Learning Habits" (CG-13); my scan found 68 distinct competency codes (C-1.1 … C-13.4). Mathematics is one goal, CG-8 ("develop mathematical understanding and abilities to recognize the world through quantities, shapes, and measures"), with 13 competencies C-8.1–C-8.13, e.g., "C-8.3 Counts up to 99 both forwards and backwards and in groups", "C-8.6 Performs addition and subtraction of 2-digit numbers fluently", "C-8.11 Performs simple transactions using money up to INR 100" — same PDF.
- Learning outcomes are laid out per competency as a row of five ordered columns (A–E) spanning "Ages 3 - 8", e.g., for "Converses fluently…": "Listens attentively and speaks in short conversations with familiar people around" → "Initiates conversations in daily life with peers and teachers" → "Engages in conversations based on events, stories, or their needs and asks questions" → "Engages in conversations, waits for their turn to speak" → "Maintains the thread of the conversation across multiple exchanges" (Table 2.1A) — same PDF.
- Stage design: in the Foundational Stage "Assessments are conducted largely in the form of qualitative observations by teachers … Explicit tests and examinations are deemed to be inappropriate for this stage"; content for the first three years is "predominantly concrete play materials"; "Textbooks, playbooks, workbooks are recommended only from Grade 1" — [NCERT, NCF for School Education 2023, §1.4.1 (PDF, 47 MB)](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf).

**India: NCF for School Education 2023 (NCF-SE)**
- Four stages: Foundational (3–8; standards set "based on domains of development"), Preparatory (8–11; standards for R1, R2, Mathematics, Art Education, Physical Education and "The World Around Us" as an interdisciplinary area), Middle (11–14; adds R3, separate Science and Social Science, Vocational Education), Secondary (14–18). "From the Preparatory Stage onwards, the Curricular Goals are defined for specific Curricular Areas." Preparatory-stage assessment: "Short formal written assessments are appropriate … Summative assessments at the end of this stage should be based on the competencies defined in the Learning Standards" — [NCF-SE 2023 §1.4, §3](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf).
- Granularity of the Preparatory-stage mathematics standards (§3.4.1.1): five Curricular Goals and, by my count, 19 competencies — CG-1 numbers/place value/operations/patterns (C-1.1–C-1.4, e.g., "knows addition and multiplication tables at least up to 10 × 10 (Pahade)"), CG-2 shapes/space/symmetry (C-2.1–C-2.4), CG-3 measurement (C-3.1–C-3.7, e.g., "Deduces that shapes having equal areas can have different perimetres"), CG-4 problem solving/computational thinking (C-4.1–C-4.3), CG-5 history of the Indian place-value system (C-5.1) — same PDF. Middle-stage maths lists 10 CGs and Secondary 11 CGs by my line scan (approximate).

### Inferences
- Every framework that is used for reporting or external assessment (Cambridge, EYFS ELGs, NCF competencies, Singapore syllabus) has a coded, stage-bounded statement as its unit; every framework that resists coding (Reggio, Te Whāriki, PYP phases) has been forced by practice to bolt on a coding layer later (Te Whāriki's 20 outcomes; PYP subject continuums; Montessori tools mapping lessons to CCSS). Cornerstone should start coded.
- The NCF hierarchy is the natural spine for an Indian school: CG → Competency (stage-level, ~70 per stage across all domains, ~13–19 per subject) → Learning Outcome (school-defined, sequenced). Nothing in NCF-FS prevents Cornerstone from defining hundreds of sequenced outcomes under each competency; NCF explicitly gives that autonomy.
- Cambridge's Stage 1 objectives (~55) versus NCF-FS's 13 maths competencies for five years show the grain gap: a diagnostic engine needs the Cambridge/White-Rose grain (one idea per node), with NCF competencies as grouping nodes and reporting labels.
- Montessori Compass's "Element" (a measurable outcome attached to a lesson/material) is the closest existing analogue to "a skill node with an observable check", and Transparent Classroom's practice of never deleting a child's record matches the append-only evidence rule.

## Key question 2 — Products and open curricula: how do they encode prerequisites and progressions in data?

### Takeaway
Almost no product encodes prerequisites as an explicit, inspectable graph. Oak's Open API is the exception in exposing per-lesson "prior knowledge" and "misconceptions" fields plus cross-year "threads"; Achieve the Core's Coherence Map (question 3) is the only fully public standard-to-standard edge list, and it is expert-derived. White Rose and Eureka encode progression as a fixed order of blocks/modules and small steps; Khan Academy encodes mastery per skill with four levels but not (in any opened source) a prerequisite structure; Montessori tools encode a lesson sequence; TaRL encodes a five-rung level ladder and regroups children on it.

### Cited Findings

**Oak National Academy (UK) — open curriculum plus Aila**
- Oak's primary maths curriculum runs Years 1–6 with 18–25 units per year and typically 5–15 lessons per unit, described as "sequenced, flexible, adaptable", "rooted in mastery approaches" and "informed by NCETM guidance"; cross-year "threads" link units (e.g., Number: Place Value; Number: Addition and Subtraction; Number: Multiplication and Division; Number: Fractions; Geometry and Measure; Algebra; Ratio and Proportion; Statistics) — [Oak: maths primary units](https://www.thenational.academy/teachers/curriculum/maths-primary/units).
- The Oak Open API exposes key stages 1–4, subjects, units and unit sequences, and per-lesson quiz questions/answers, keywords, misconceptions, "prior knowledge requirements", transcripts, slides and video, under the Open Government Licence — [Oak Open API](https://open-api.thenational.academy/).
- Scale (impact report, 1 Dec 2025): 196,143 teachers used Oak Jan–Jul 2025; 10,941 lessons published (~80% of plan); 81% rated curriculum structure and sequencing "high"/"very high"; Aila had 42,610 users by Sept 2025 and 191,765 lesson resources co-created since launch; evaluator ImpactEd — [Oak blog, 24/25 impact report](https://www.thenational.academy/blog/five-key-insights-from-our-2425-impact-report).
- Aila's generation is anchored to Oak's corpus: it "will first scan a high-quality, representative dataset to see whether it can build exclusively from our quality assured content which is aligned to the national curriculum in England" before widening; Oak was funded by the UK Department for Education; stated limitation: resources "align to our national curriculum in England" — [New America, 4 Dec 2025](https://www.newamerica.org/insights/how-oak-national-academy-is-transforming-classrooms-with-ai-and-oer/). The code is MIT-licensed, built on Next.js, PostgreSQL with pgvector and Prisma — [GitHub: oaknational/oak-ai-lesson-assistant](https://github.com/oaknational/oak-ai-lesson-assistant).
- Aila's fixed lesson schema: learning outcome → learning cycles (explanation, check for understanding, practice) → key vocabulary → misconceptions → quizzes (multiple-choice only); it runs on "Chat GPT4" with "a 9,000-word prompt that provides very specific guidance on what excellence looks like for each section of a lesson"; teachers "must verify outputs for accuracy"; weaker "in STEM and modern languages"; no image generation — [Oak Help Centre: Aila FAQs](https://support.thenational.academy/aila-faqs).

**White Rose Maths (UK) — small steps**
- Year 1 is organised into termly blocks (Autumn: place value within 10, addition and subtraction within 10, shape; Spring: place value within 20/50, addition and subtraction within 20, length and height, mass and volume; Summer: multiplication and division, fractions, position and direction, place value within 100, money, time), "follows the national curriculum with a mastery approach", uses concrete–pictorial–abstract representations, and "it is not necessarily intended for each small step to take one lesson" — [Third Space Learning: White Rose Maths Year 1](https://thirdspacelearning.com/blog/white-rose-maths-year-1/).
- Schemes have "12-14 blocks per year group", each block a sequence of small steps "focused on one key mathematical idea", with "flashback" retrieval practice and end-of-block assessments; the scheme of learning is free while slides/assessments are paid; the review reports "an NFER evaluation found one extra month of maths progress on average, but the result was not statistically significant" and lists criticisms (pacing, over-reliance on the prescribed sequence, weak differentiation for SEND) — [Structural Learning, White Rose Maths teacher's guide (2026)](https://www.structural-learning.com/post/white-rose-maths-a-teachers-guide) (secondary source; the NFER report itself was not opened — unverified).
- White Rose's own help centre confirms schemes are filtered by "year group" and organised into "blocks of learning" with a separate "Nursery" section, but gives no step counts or prerequisite links — [White Rose Education help centre](https://help.whiteroseeducation.com/knowledge/primary-schemes-of-learning).

**Khan Academy — mastery**
- Skills move through four mastery levels — "attempted" → "familiar" → "proficient" → "mastered" — on the basis of exercises, quizzes, unit tests and course challenges; "in a typical 30-minute session, a learner can work on two exercises and level up to proficient on two skills"; Khan reports that the proportion of skills proficient/mastered correlates with MAP Growth mathematics scores ("convergent validity") and cites Block & Burns (1976) on mastery learning — [Khan Academy blog, "skills to proficient", 25 Sept 2023 / updated 21 Mar 2025](https://blog.khanacademy.org/why-khan-academy-will-be-using-skills-to-proficient-to-measure-learning-outcomes/). Khan's help-centre articles on how levels rise and fall returned 403; no opened source documents a prerequisite graph between skills (see Gaps).

**Eureka Math / Great Minds — "A Story of Units"**
- Progression is encoded as module order: "skipping or altering the order of the modules can snowball into other areas"; Grade 4 Module 1 (place value to 1 million) underpins Module 3 (multi-digit multiplication/division), Module 2's measurement conversions are needed mid-Module 3, Modules 5–6 build fractions on the "units" idea, and "fluencies build from one module to the next" — [Great Minds blog, "The Progression of Grade 4 Modules", 14 Feb 2017](https://greatminds.org/math/blog/eureka/the-progression-of-grade-4-modules).

**Amplify CKLA (US) — knowledge-building ELA**
- K–2 runs two strands — explicit, systematic foundational skills (phonics, decoding, fluency) in parallel with domain-based knowledge units in science, social studies, literature and the arts — which merge into one strand in Grades 3–5; Amplify asserts CKLA "Meets ESSA Tier I criteria" but the page cites no specific study; it is aligned to the Core Knowledge Sequence and rated on EdReports — [Amplify CKLA](https://amplify.com/programs/amplify-core-knowledge-language-arts/). WestEd's evaluation of CKLA 3rd edition is a quasi-experimental K–5 study in California, baseline 2025–26, final report "Fall 2026/Winter 2027" — no results yet — [WestEd](https://www.wested.org/event/amplify-core-knowledge-language-arts-evaluation-study/).

**Core Knowledge Sequence (US)**
- A content blueprint "grade by grade from preschool through grade eight" (Preschool ages 3–5; K–8 in language arts, history, geography, mathematics, science, visual arts, music), current editions 2023, distinguished by naming specific works and periods (e.g., Grade 5: the Renaissance, "A Midsummer Night's Dream") on the principle that "we learn new knowledge by building on what we already know"; it specifies content domains rather than skill trajectories for maths — [Core Knowledge Foundation](https://www.coreknowledge.org/core-knowledge-sequence/).

**Toddle (India-origin IB platform)**
- Built by "five engineer-turned-educators" at an international school in India; "1,200+ schools" in 100+ countries, "30,000+ PYP educators across 1,000+ PYP schools" (the FAQ dates its MYP figure to Sept 2022; the other figures are undated), "officially licensed by the IB"; ISO 27001/27017/27018/27701, SOC2 Type 2, COPPA, FERPA; for Toddle AI: "We never share your data with Large Language Model providers and do not use your data to train LLMs", using self-hosted and third-party LLMs with pseudonymised interactions — [Toddle FAQs](https://www.toddleapp.com/faqs/). The PYP page claims "50%+ PYP schools globally run on Toddle" and 50,000 PYP educators, and that "Toddle AI takes the first pass at grading student work, strictly based on your rubric", with no stated verification protocol for AI output — [Toddle IB PYP](https://www.toddleapp.com/ib-pyp/). (The two pages' figures differ; the FAQ is dated 2022.)

**Pratham — Teaching at the Right Level (TaRL)**
- The ladder: reading levels Beginner → Letter → Word → Paragraph → Story; arithmetic levels Beginner → 1-digit → 2-digit → Subtraction → Division; children "are grouped according to their current learning levels either across grades or within the same class"; "Regular, time-bound assessments are conducted … children are re-grouped as they advance"; target is children aged 8+ (typically Grades 3–5); two delivery models — Learning Camps (30–50 days, 3–5 hours daily) and government partnerships (4–6 months, 1–2 hours daily) — [Pratham: Teaching at the Right Level](https://www.pratham.org/about/teaching-at-the-right-level/).
- Evidence: six randomised evaluations across seven Indian states since 2001 (Banerjee, Duflo et al.), "some of the largest effect sizes rigorously measured in the education literature"; programmes have reached "over 60 million students" in India and Africa — [J-PAL case study](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/case-study/teaching-right-level-improve-learning). Scaled models: Haryana government-teacher model with resource-person mentoring in dedicated periods gave "gains in language of 0.15 standard deviation"; Uttar Pradesh 40-day in-school camps run by Pratham volunteers (plus 10-day summer camps) gave "0.70 standard deviations"; in Bihar and Uttarakhand "the methodology was not adopted by government schoolteachers, despite well-received training sessions and Pratham support" — [Banerjee et al., NBER Working Paper 22746](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22746).

### Inferences
- Where a product's progression lives only in the order of a printed scheme (White Rose, Eureka), a school cannot compute "what is missing" for a child; where it lives in per-node fields (Oak's prior knowledge/misconceptions; Montessori Compass elements), it can. Cornerstone's "precedes" edges should be first-class rows, not implied by ordering.
- Oak demonstrates the workable pattern for a school-scale corpus: fixed schema per lesson, a curated corpus that generation must draw from first, open API over the same data, and threads to name the vertical strands — this is the model closest to Cornerstone's "generate → approve → lock → serve".
- TaRL is the only system in this set with a causal effect measured in Indian government schools; its design lesson is that a five-rung ladder plus disciplined regrouping beats a rich curriculum that teachers do not implement. Cornerstone's ladder (R1–R14) should keep TaRL-style coarse rungs for grouping and reporting even if the underlying graph is fine.
- Khan-style mastery levels depend on volume of digital responses; Cornerstone's evidence is largely paper-based, so mastery states must be derivable from a small number of marked items plus teacher confirmation rather than from dozens of attempts.

## Key question 3 — Research-based learning progressions: how reliable are published prerequisite graphs, and how were they validated?

### Takeaway
Three kinds of validation exist and they are not equivalent: (1) experimental tests of ordering — Clements & Sarama showed that teaching the same activities in the trajectory's order beats reverse order (kindergarten length, n=189) and that following levels beats jumping to the target (preschool shape composition, n=145, g≈0.55); (2) psychometric calibration — NZ's PaCT fits teacher judgments across aspects to a Rasch scale (0–1300) and maps it to curriculum levels by expert alignment; ACARA's numeracy progression builds "as many levels within each sub-element as can be supported by evidence", tells teachers students "may straddle two or more levels", and was trialled in a national formative-assessment alpha; (3) expert derivation — the Common Core Coherence Map's edges come from the standards writers' "wiring diagram" and Progressions documents, not from data. Published graphs are therefore reliable as within-strand orderings and as hypotheses about cross-strand dependencies, not as validated cross-strand prerequisite facts.

### Cited Findings

**Clements & Sarama learning trajectories (Building Blocks, TRIAD, LT2)**
- Definition: learning trajectories consist of "a learning goal, a developmental path along which children develop to reach that goal, and a set of activities matched to each level in that learning path"; Clements' RCTs "shown it to have a positive impact on children's learning" and influenced EEF and What Works Clearinghouse guidance — [Wikipedia: Douglas Clements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Clements).
- Ordering test (kindergarten length measurement): "Participating kindergartners (n = 189) were assigned to one of three conditions: LT, reverse-order (REV), or business-as-usual (BAU). LT and REV students received one-on-one instruction using the same activities from a length LT, while the REV condition reversed the LT order. At posttest, LT and REV children exhibited significantly greater learning relative to BAU peers. But importantly, LT children outperformed their REV peers" (LT–REV contrast .32, 95% credible interval [0.57, 0.07]); the authors also note REV still beat BAU, so the activities work even out of order — [Sarama, Clements, Baroody, Kutaka, Chernyavskiy, Shi & Cong, AERA Open 7(1), 2021 (ERIC full text)](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1323924.pdf).
- Skip-levels test (preschool shape composition): children "at least two levels below the target instructional LT level" taught level-by-level (n=82) versus an equal amount of instruction "focused only on the target level" (skip-levels, n=63); "children in the LT condition exhibited significantly greater learning … mainly on near-transfer items" (g = .55); the paper also recalls that the Building Blocks curriculum beat business-as-usual with effect size 1.07 and a topic-based curriculum with 0.47 (Clements & Sarama 2008), and cites Frye et al. (2013) that interventions with LTs are more efficacious for numeracy — [Clements, Sarama, Baroody, Joswick & Wolfe, AERJ 56(6), 2019 (ERIC full text)](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1234710.pdf).
- LT2 (the public Learning and Teaching with Learning Trajectories tool) could not be read: the site is a JavaScript application that returned an empty shell to both fetch methods — [learningtrajectories.org](https://www.learningtrajectories.org/) (content unverified).

**Australia — National Numeracy Learning Progression v3 (ACARA) and Australian Curriculum v9**
- Structure: three elements (Number sense and algebra; Measurement and geometry; Statistics and probability) and 14 sub-elements; "Within each sub-element, indicators are grouped together to form developmental levels … There are as many levels within each sub-element as can be supported by evidence. The listing of indicators within a level is non-hierarchical"; levels are coded by sub-element abbreviation and number (NPV1–NPV10 for Number and place value, CPr1–CPr8 counting processes, AdS1–AdS10 additive strategies, MuS1–MuS10 multiplicative strategies, …); "The progression has not been designed as a checklist and does not replace the Australian Curriculum: Mathematics"; "Individual students may not neatly fit within a particular level … and may straddle two or more levels"; "not all students will progress through every level in a uniform manner"; teaching decisions "can be based on judgements about student capability that relate to a single indicator rather than all indicators at a level"; CC BY 4.0 — [ACARA, National Numeracy Learning Progression v3, March 2020 (PDF)](https://education.nsw.gov.au/content/dam/main-education/en/home/teaching-and-learning/curriculum/literacy-and-numeracy/resources-for-schools/lnlp/national-numeracy-learning-progression-v3-for-publication.pdf).
- Provenance: v3 was produced in the "discovery phase of the Learning Progressions and Online Formative Assessment national initiative"; ACER "undertook a mapping and analysis of Version 2 … to ACER's learning progression in Mathematics", which "contributed to informing the refinement" to v3 — same PDF. It "was trialed through the Online Formative Assessment Initiative in 2020" and Australian Curriculum v9 (endorsed 2022) presents Literacy and Numeracy "as learning progressions, which were based on the Version 3 NLNLPs" — [ACARA v8 site: Version 3 of the progressions](https://v8.australiancurriculum.edu.au/resources/national-literacy-and-numeracy-learning-progressions/version-3-of-national-literacy-and-numeracy-learning-progressions/).
- In v9 the Numeracy general capability keeps 3 elements and 14 sub-elements (8 + 4 + 2); "Number and place value has 10 levels while Positioning and locating has 5"; "progression levels may span across year levels of the curriculum" — [Australian Curriculum v9: Understand this general capability — Numeracy](https://www.australiancurriculum.edu.au/teacher-resources/understand-this-general-capability/numeracy).
- NSW tags its digital K–10 English and Mathematics syllabuses with progression indicators and provides PLAN2 software for planning and monitoring; "The amount of time it takes a student to progress through each level is not specified and will vary" — [NSW Department of Education: learning progressions](https://education.nsw.gov.au/teaching-and-learning/curriculum/literacy-and-numeracy/resources-for-schools/learning-progressions).

**New Zealand — Progress and Consistency Tool (PaCT)**
- PaCT "uses teachers' judgments to determine the best estimate of student achievement" in reading, writing and mathematics; each Learning Progression Framework "is divided into several aspects", teachers pick "the set of illustrations [signposts] that best fits" per aspect, PaCT "combines them into a total score" and "uses a mathematical model called the Rasch model to convert the total score to a range on a measurement scale" numbered 0–1300; results are shown "as a range on the PaCT scale rather than a fixed point" to expose measurement error; curriculum levels come from "a curriculum alignment exercise" in which subject-matter experts set the scale scores for NZC levels 1–5; the frameworks "were developed through a rigorous process involving several rounds of trials and statistical analysis". The paper also names the judgment biases the tool is designed against (stereotypes about gender and ethnicity, confirmation, surface features) — [Ministry of Education NZ, "About the Progress and Consistency Tool" (PDF)](https://assets.curriculumprogresstools.education.govt.nz/public/Uploads/info_about_PaCT.pdf); overview — [Curriculum Progress Tools: Learn about PaCT](https://curriculumprogresstools.education.govt.nz/pact/learn-about-pact/). NZCER "was responsible for facilitating the psychometric work associated with the tool development" — [NZCER: PaCT](https://www.nzcer.org.nz/research/progress-and-consistency-tool-pact).

**US — Common Core Coherence Map (Achieve the Core)**
- The map shows how "standards relate to one another, both within and across grades" for K–High School, derived from Jason Zimba's wiring diagram and the Progressions documents by the CCSS-M lead writers (Zimba, McCallum, Daro); the connections are expert-derived rather than empirically validated; uses include tracing "a standard back through its logical pre-requisites" — [Achieve the Core: Learn more about the Coherence Map](https://achievethecore.org/page/2801/learn-more-about-the-coherence-map). The visualiser's code is public domain (CC0) and reads standards and their relations as graph nodes from a standards JSON endpoint — [GitHub: achievethecore/atc-coherence-map](https://github.com/achievethecore/atc-coherence-map).

**Convergent validity of platform mastery data**
- Khan Academy reports that the share of skills at proficient/mastered correlates with MAP Growth scores ("convergent validity") — [Khan Academy blog](https://blog.khanacademy.org/why-khan-academy-will-be-using-skills-to-proficient-to-measure-learning-outcomes/).

**Mastery learning as the pedagogy behind small-step progressions (EEF)**
- Mastery learning: "Moderate impact for very low cost, based on moderate evidence" — "+5" months on average; "Learners work through each block of content in a series of sequential steps and must demonstrate a high level of success on tests, typically about 80%, before progressing"; "much of [the research] is relatively dated and findings are not consistent"; the EEF's own two-RCT evaluation of the Mathematics Mastery programme (Feb 2015) found "one additional month's progress", possibly because "classes did not delay starting new topics until a high level of proficiency had been reached by all pupils" — [EEF Teaching and Learning Toolkit, October 2018 edition (DERA archive PDF)](https://dera.ioe.ac.uk/id/eprint/33734/1/EEF-Teaching-Learning-Toolkit-October-2018.pdf) (the live EEF site returned 403; the toolkit has since been updated — figures here are the 2018 edition's).

### Inferences
- The strongest evidence for "prerequisite" edges is within a single strand (length; shape composition; ACARA sub-elements), where ordering was tested or calibrated. Cross-strand edges (e.g., "place value precedes column addition") remain expert assertions everywhere I could check, including the Coherence Map. Cornerstone should store an edge's provenance (expert | trialled | validated) and treat expert edges as defeasible.
- Two design facts recur: levels are decoupled from grades (ACARA, PaCT, LT2-style trajectories, PYP phases), and a child's position is a range or a straddle, not a point. Cornerstone's child_skill_state should carry uncertainty and allow "between rungs".
- PaCT shows how to make teacher judgment measurable without tests: fixed aspects, anchored exemplars, Rasch-scaled totals, ranges. For Nursery–Grade 2 (where NCF forbids tests) this is the model for turning the reader's notebook into a defensible scale — at the cost of a calibration study Cornerstone does not yet have.
- The mastery-learning evidence warns that a beautifully sequenced scheme yields little unless the "don't move on until ~80%" rule and the extra support time are actually enforced — i.e., the operational half of mastery, not the curriculum half, carries the effect.

## Key question 4 — Misconception taxonomies in early maths, and how Eedi tags them

### Takeaway
Two catalogues exist at different grains: the classical "bug" literature (Brown & Burton 1978; Brown & VanLehn 1980; VanLehn's Mind Bugs) for multi-digit subtraction, whose bug names (smaller-from-larger, borrow-no-decrement) are still the working taxonomy in clinical studies; and Eedi's "Misconception Graph" of 8,000+ maths misconceptions inferred from 200 million responses, released CC BY 4.0. Eedi's operational method — one correct answer and three distractors each tied to a named misconception, tagged by humans with model suggestions — is the state of the art, but two limits are documented: manual tagging was "Painful. Slow. Inconsistent. Not scalable.", and correct answers can hide misconceptions that classifiers catch only 57% of the time (reasoning models 84%, at ~8 false alarms per true detection).

### Cited Findings
- The foundational catalogues: "Diagnostic Models for Procedural Bugs in Basic Mathematical Skills" (Brown & Burton, Cognitive Science, 1978; 1,327 citations) and "Repair Theory: A Generative Theory of Bugs in Procedural Skills" (Brown & VanLehn, Cognitive Science, 1980; 627 citations) — bibliographic records via [Semantic Scholar API (Brown & Burton 1978)](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1207/s15516709cog0202_4?fields=title,authors,year,venue,abstract,citationCount) and [Semantic Scholar API (Brown & VanLehn 1980)](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1207/s15516709cog0404_3?fields=title,authors,year,venue,abstract,citationCount). The full texts and VanLehn's Mind Bugs page were blocked, so bug counts are not reported here.
- A clinical taxonomy still in use: Raghubar et al. (2009) coded 291 third- and fourth-graders' multi-digit addition/subtraction errors into five types — math fact errors, procedural bugs ("smaller-from-larger", "borrow no decrement"), procedural slips, visual-spatial errors (misalignment, misreading), and operation-switch errors; at least one error of each type appeared in 44%, 45.7%, 49.5%, 13.1% and 22.3% of children respectively; "procedural bugs distinguished all math-difficulty groups from controls", fact errors marked severe maths disability, and visual-spatial errors tracked reading rather than maths difficulty — [Raghubar, Cirino, Barnes, Ewing-Cobbs, Fletcher & Fuchs, 2009 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2788949/).
- Eedi's numbers: a "Misconception Graph" — "a mapping of more than 8,000 common maths misconceptions, informed by 200 million real student responses" — released open-source through Learning Commons under CC BY 4.0; a two-year RCT with effects "d = 0.46" at 18 months and "d = 0.30" at 24 months (EduEvidence gold certificate) — [Eedi](https://www.eedi.com/). (The EEF's independent trial page for Eedi/Diagnostic Questions returned 403 — unverified here.)
- Question format and teacher view: each diagnostic question has "one correct answer, three distractors each tied to a misconception"; results mark each pupil as correct/no misconception, "misconception … found and resolved after receiving feedback", or "found and remains unresolved"; a "Fix" page surfaces quizzes with unresolved misconceptions — [Eedi Help Centre: Understanding student misconceptions](https://help.eedi.co.uk/en/articles/6212339-understanding-student-misconceptions).
- Tagging: Eedi "didn't have labels linking distractors to the misconceptions they revealed"; manual tagging was "Painful. Slow. Inconsistent. Not scalable."; it ran the Kaggle competition "Eedi - Mining Misconceptions in Mathematics" to build models that "suggest candidate misconceptions for distractors, making it easier for expert human teachers to tag" (winners Raja Biswas; Kazuhito Yonekawa et al.; Team Waseda Pochi) — [Eedi news](https://www.eedi.com/news/from-wrong-answers-to-real-insights-how-we-used-a-kaggle-challenge-to-map-student-misconceptions). The Kaggle page itself could not be read; the number of misconceptions in the competition mapping is unverified.
- Data scale behind the taxonomy: the NeurIPS 2020 Education Challenge released "over 20 million examples of students' answers to mathematics questions" from Eedi, noting that wrong answers "reveal key information about the specific nature of misconceptions" — [Wang et al., arXiv:2007.12061](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.12061).
- The limit of answer-based tagging: on 20,964 real Eedi responses, "students reach the correct answer through flawed reasoning" and automated feedback "instead reinforce them"; fine-tuned classifiers detected 57% of such hidden misconceptions, open-weight reasoning models 84% but with "approximately 8 false alarms per genuine detection at realistic prevalence rates"; the authors propose a rubric that "separates answer correctness from method validity" and a detect-verify-escalate pipeline routing uncertain cases to diagnostic follow-up questions — [Imran & Bulathwela, "The Correct Answer Trap", arXiv:2606.23205, June 2026 (AIED PEAF 2026 workshop)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23205).

### Inferences
- For Cornerstone's Grades 1–5 arithmetic, the bug taxonomy is small, stable and code-checkable: a marker can test a wrong answer against a finite set of generative bugs (smaller-from-larger, borrow-no-decrement, fact error, alignment) and only fall back to a model when no bug explains the answer. This is exactly the "code where correctness is needed" rule.
- Eedi's evidence supports tagging at the distractor level (each pre-written wrong answer or each observed wrong-answer pattern → one misconception code), with human confirmation and model-suggested candidates — not free-text model labels.
- The Correct Answer Trap result is a direct argument for Cornerstone's "three signals, never two" rule and for capturing working: a right answer with wrong working must be its own signal, and any detector will produce many false alarms, so its output should route to a follow-up item, not to a verdict.
- Eedi's open CC BY 4.0 graph is a candidate seed for Cornerstone's misconception table (with `external_ref` to Eedi ids), subject to checking its coverage of Foundational/Preparatory topics and Indian notation (e.g., Indian place-value naming, "exchange/regroup").

### Gaps
- The full texts of Brown & Burton (1978), Brown & VanLehn (1980) and VanLehn's Mind Bugs were blocked; the number of catalogued subtraction bugs is not reported.
- No opened source gives a published catalogue of early place-value or counting misconceptions for ages 3–8 (the search budget ran out before this could be pursued).
- The Kaggle competition page was unreadable; the size of Eedi's competition misconception mapping is unverified.
- The Learning Commons release page for the Misconception Graph was not opened; licence terms are as stated on eedi.com.

## Key question 5 — Generated pedagogy at scale: who is doing it, how they QA it, and what problems are documented

### Takeaway
Oak National Academy is the only operator that publishes its quality machinery: a fixed lesson schema, a 9,000-word prompt, retrieval from its own approved corpus, an auto-evaluator with 24 benchmarks whose agreement with expert teachers rose from QWK 0.17 to 0.32 after prompt fixes (still weak), a four-layer safety stack with an independent moderation agent and external red-teaming, and mandatory teacher review. Independent assessment of the US market (Common Sense Media, Aug 2025: Khanmigo, MagicSchool, Curipod, Gemini in Classroom) rated the category "Moderate" risk and documented racially biased behaviour-intervention plans, high-risk IEP generators built on insufficient data, "professional-looking output [that] may mask errors", and "push AI-generated material directly to classrooms without review". Diffit's simplifications introduce factual imprecision at the lowest reading levels; Brisk-style feedback tools decide silently what merits feedback.

### Cited Findings

**Oak National Academy — Aila**
- Quality method (21 Jan 2025): an auto-evaluation tool scoring lessons on 24 quality benchmarks analysed 4,985 AI-generated lessons across English, Maths, Science, Geography and History; experienced teachers rated selected multiple-choice questions so the tool could be compared with humans; after codifying findings about weak distractors ("opposite sentiments", "obvious choices") into the prompt, mean squared error between auto- and human ratings fell from 3.83 to 2.95 and agreement rose from 0.17 to 0.32 (quadratic weighted kappa) — [Oak blog: How do we ensure our AI-generated resources are high-quality?](https://www.thenational.academy/blog/how-do-we-ensure-our-ai-generated-resources-are-high-quality). (Oak's fuller paper, "Auto-Evaluation: A Critical Measure…", is hosted on PubPub, which returned 403 — not opened.)
- Safety method (30 July 2025): four stages — prompt engineering for UK-school content; input threat detection; an "Independent Asynchronous Content Moderation Agent (IACMA)" classifying output as Safe / Content guidance / Highly sensitive / Toxic; human-in-the-loop teacher review before pupils see anything; "External safety specialists conduct red-teaming exercises"; analysis of 10,000+ post-launch lessons found difficulty with recent events outside training data and with topics such as specific educational needs or outdoor learning; Aila is text-only, so images would need new guardrails — [Oak blog: Building AI that's safe for the classroom](https://www.thenational.academy/blog/building-ai-that-s-safe-for-the-classroom-what-we-have-learned-with-aila).
- Grounding and schema: Aila first tries to build "exclusively from our quality assured content"; it uses GPT-4 and a 9,000-word prompt; output follows learning outcome → learning cycles → checks for understanding → vocabulary → misconceptions → multiple-choice quizzes; teachers "must verify outputs for accuracy"; weaker in STEM and modern languages — [New America, Dec 2025](https://www.newamerica.org/insights/how-oak-national-academy-is-transforming-classrooms-with-ai-and-oer/); [Aila FAQs](https://support.thenational.academy/aila-faqs).
- Teacher-practice study (13 Feb 2025; 72 surveyed, 8 interviewed; "preliminary"): nearly two-thirds reported reduced workload (planning from scratch falling from ~30 minutes to 5–10); "over half of teachers reported thinking differently about lesson planning — incorporating checks for understanding and misconceptions"; 85% rated plan structure/content quality "fairly high" or "very high"; few quality complaints, mainly missing images — [Oak blog: How is Aila impacting teacher lesson planning practices…](https://www.thenational.academy/blog/how-is-aila-impacting-teacher-lesson-planning-practices-workload-and-expertise-early-insights).

**Khanmigo (Khan Academy)**
- Teacher tools: standards-aligned lesson plans, rubrics, quiz questions and exit tickets, student groupings/differentiation, learning objectives; free for teachers in 44+ countries; "GPT-4-powered"; the page carries no statement about mistakes, accuracy or required teacher review — [Khanmigo](https://www.khanmigo.ai/). Microsoft's quick guide describes it as "powered by Azure OpenAI Service" and "available for free" — [Khanmigo for Teachers Quick Guide (Microsoft PDF)](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/education/msdownloads/Khanmigo-for-Teachers-Quick-Guide.pdf). Common Sense Media notes "Khanmigo requires educators to upload context before generating lesson plans, which leads to higher-quality outputs than tools that start from general prompts" — [Common Sense Media risk assessment PDF](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/sites/default/files/featured-content/files/csm-ai-risk-assessment-ai-teacher-assistants-final.pdf).

**Common Sense Media risk assessment of AI teacher assistants (Khanmigo, MagicSchool, Curipod, Gemini in Google Classroom)**
- "Overall risk level: Moderate" (last updated 14 Aug 2025; sub-ratings: Keep Kids & Teens Safe — Low; Be Effective — Moderate; Prioritize Fairness — Moderate; Put People First — Moderate; Support Human Connection — Minimal). Key takeaways: tools "can act as 'invisible influencers' in students' learning, shaping what they learn through content that may contain biases, inaccuracies, or one-sided perspectives"; "Professional-looking output may mask errors"; "Risk of 'outsourced thinking' for important decisions"; high-stakes documents (IEPs, 504 plans, behaviour plans) are "Highest Risk: Based on insufficient student data"; retrieval grounding "can help reduce inaccuracies … though it doesn't eliminate the fundamental limitations of generative AI"; "Novice teachers may lack the experience needed to use these tools" — [Common Sense Media, AI Risk Assessment: AI Teacher Assistants (PDF)](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/sites/default/files/featured-content/files/csm-ai-risk-assessment-ai-teacher-assistants-final.pdf). Press release (6 Aug 2025): platforms let educators "push AI-generated material directly to classrooms without review"; recommendations — build on established curricula, mandatory review for accuracy/bias/appropriateness, professional development, avoid AI for critical documents like IEPs, clear district policies — [Common Sense Media press release](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/press-releases/ai-teacher-assistants-need-better-safety-measures-common-sense-media-report-finds).
- Bias test: identical behaviour-intervention prompts run 100 times (50 Black-coded, 50 white-coded names, split by gender) produced more punitive plans for Black-coded students (e.g., Gemini gave "Annie" "consistent positive reinforcement" and "Lakeesha" "immediate" responses); MagicSchool and Gemini showed the largest gaps; Google disabled its behaviour-intervention shortcut pending testing; MagicSchool said it could not reproduce the findings but would improve bias detection — [Chalkbeat, 6 Aug 2025](https://www.chalkbeat.org/2025/08/06/ai-teacher-assistants-promote-racial-bias-study-finds/).

**Brisk and Diffit**
- Chen (2025) analyses AI lesson-planning and feedback tools (Brisk Teaching as the case) as "epistemic infrastructures rather than as neutral tools", finding "epistemic substitution": they perform teachers' cognitive work "without sustaining skilled epistemic actions, epistemic sensitivity, or virtuous habit formation" — e.g., deciding what in a student's writing merits feedback, opaquely — [Chen, "Beyond Tools: Generative AI as Epistemic Infrastructure in Education", arXiv:2504.06928 (rev. Dec 2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.06928).
- A teacher-PD site's comparison (30 May 2026): "Diffit's AI-generated science and history passages occasionally simplify in ways that introduce factual imprecision", worst "at the lowest reading levels" (a Grade-2 photosynthesis passage "may omit detail that matters for the curriculum concept"); advice: "review before distributing" — [tpd.edu.au](https://tpd.edu.au/diffit-vs-brisk-teaching-vs-schoolai-comparison/) (commercial secondary source; low weight).

### Inferences
- Oak's numbers set realistic expectations for Cornerstone's own generate-then-approve loop: even a purpose-built evaluator agrees only weakly with expert teachers (QWK 0.32), so the curriculum team's approve-and-lock step is the quality mechanism, and the auto-evaluator is a triage filter, not a judge. Every Cornerstone prompt version therefore needs a human-rated gold set (Rule 7 "engine eval") with agreement reported, exactly as Oak does.
- The documented failure modes are content-type specific: pedagogy-generation risks are bias (student-specific plans), plausibility masking errors, and out-of-corpus topics; the remedy Oak and Common Sense Media converge on is the same — generate only from an approved corpus, never generate child-specific high-stakes documents, and require review before anything reaches children. Cornerstone's plan to generate pedagogy per learning outcome and level (not per child) sits on the safer side of this line, provided the per-child layer (prescriptions, notes to parents) is computed or reviewed rather than free-generated.
- Aila's 9,000-word prompt and fixed schema show that "prompt as a versioned artefact with a schema" (Cornerstone Rule 2) is what makes evaluation possible at all.

### Gaps
- Oak's Auto-Evaluation paper (PubPub) was blocked; details of the 24 benchmarks and the evaluator's per-criterion validity are not reported here.
- MagicSchool's, Brisk's and Diffit's own QA documentation was not opened; Khanmigo's documented arithmetic-error episodes (2023 press) could not be verified without search budget — unverified.
- No independent study of Toddle AI's output quality was found.
- No published evaluation of AI-generated pedagogy for ages 3–6 (Nursery–KG) was found; all documented deployments target school-age lessons.

## Key question 6 — Cross-subject and cross-grade: how good systems span Nursery to Grade 10 across domains without one scale

### Takeaway
Every credible system keeps separate structures per domain and changes the structure at stage boundaries rather than stretching one scale: EYFS uses seven areas with best-fit goals and no numeric tracking; Te Whāriki uses five strands and 20 outcomes; NCF-FS sets goals by developmental domain and NCF-SE switches to curricular areas from Grade 3, adds Science and Social Science as separate standards from Grade 6, and replaces observation with written assessment as children age; ACARA keeps 14 numeracy sub-elements each with its own level count and lets levels span year levels; PaCT builds one Rasch scale per subject (reading, writing, maths) and maps each to curriculum levels by expert alignment. Nobody scores socio-emotional, physical or arts development on the same scale as maths.

### Cited Findings
- NCF-SE stage design: Foundational standards "based on domains of development" (Physical; Socio-emotional and Ethical; Cognitive; Aesthetic and Cultural; Language and Literacy; plus positive learning habits); Preparatory standards for R1, R2, Mathematics, Art Education, Physical Education and "The World Around Us"; Middle stage adds R3, separate Science and Social Science standards and Vocational Education; assessment moves from "qualitative observations" (Foundational) to "Short formal written assessments" with competency-based summative assessment at the end of the Preparatory Stage — [NCF-SE 2023 §1.4](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf).
- NCF-FS keeps one goal for maths (CG-8, 13 competencies) alongside goals for health/safety, sensorial perception, fitness, emotional intelligence, positive attitude to work, regard for nature, sense-making of the world, two languages, arts and learning habits — all coded the same way (CG-n / C-n.m) but never summed — [NCF-FS 2022](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCF_for_Foundational_Stage_20_October_2022.pdf).
- EYFS: seven areas, each an "educational programme"; the ELGs support "a holistic, best-fit judgement about a child's development at the end of the EYFS, and their readiness for year 1"; no physical evidence collection required — [EYFS framework 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a5f8e4fb00f3323bf1a23a1/EYFS_group_and_school_based_from_September_2026.pdf); Development Matters gives age-band checkpoints and is "not … a tick list" — [Development Matters 2023](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/64e6002a20ae890014f26cbc/DfE_Development_Matters_Report_Sep2023.pdf).
- Te Whāriki: five strands, 20 learning outcomes after 2017; the revision aimed to make "connections between early childhood and school curricula more explicit" — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Te_Wh%C4%81riki); [McLachlan 2018](https://www.hekupu.ac.nz/article/te-whariki-revisited-how-approaches-assessment-can-make-valued-learning-visible).
- ACARA: three elements, 14 sub-elements with different level counts (NPV 10, Positioning 5), "progression levels may span across year levels", and the numeracy progression "is designed to assist schools and teachers in all learning areas" with advice on the numeracy demands of each subject — [ACARA v3 PDF](https://education.nsw.gov.au/content/dam/main-education/en/home/teaching-and-learning/curriculum/literacy-and-numeracy/resources-for-schools/lnlp/national-numeracy-learning-progression-v3-for-publication.pdf); [AC v9 numeracy](https://www.australiancurriculum.edu.au/teacher-resources/understand-this-general-capability/numeracy).
- PaCT: separate frameworks for reading, writing and mathematics, each with its own aspects and its own 0–1300 Rasch scale, from school entry to Year 10; curriculum-level cut scores set by expert alignment — [About PaCT](https://assets.curriculumprogresstools.education.govt.nz/public/Uploads/info_about_PaCT.pdf).
- IB PYP: six transdisciplinary themes carry the units of inquiry across ages 3–12 while each subject keeps its own continuum of phases — [Wikipedia: PYP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IB_Primary_Years_Programme); [Haese S&S 2025](https://www.haesemathematics.com/media/W1siZiIsIjIwMjUvMDUvMjcvOWE1dnMzdG16bF9JQl9QWVBfc2NvcGVfYW5kX3NlcXVlbmNlXzIwMjUucGRmIl1d/IB%20PYP%20scope%20and%20sequence%202025.pdf?sha=c308474a2451de32).
- Cambridge: subjects "can be offered in any combination", each with its own framework and stage codes; Early Years (EY1–EY3) → Primary (Stages 1–6) → Lower Secondary — [Cambridge Primary](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/programmes-and-qualifications/cambridge-primary/curriculum/); [Cambridge Early Years](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/programmes-and-qualifications/cambridge-early-years/).
- Montessori tools: Montessori Compass keeps ten areas (Practical Life, Sensorial, Mathematics, Language Arts, Science, Geography, History, Cosmic Studies, Art/Music/Movement, Infant) each with its own lesson hierarchy — [Montessori Compass](https://www.montessoricompass.com/language-arts/).
- Oak: primary maths threads (place value, addition/subtraction, multiplication/division, fractions, geometry and measure, algebra, ratio and proportion, statistics) run Years 1–6 and continue into KS3–4 content exposed by the same API — [Oak maths primary units](https://www.thenational.academy/teachers/curriculum/maths-primary/units); [Oak Open API](https://open-api.thenational.academy/).

**What changes for Grades 6–10 (from the opened sources)**
- NCF-SE adds separate Science and Social Science standards, a third language and vocational education in the Middle Stage (11–14); maths Curricular Goals grow from 5 (Preparatory) to about 10 (Middle) and 11 (Secondary) by my scan — [NCF-SE 2023](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf).
- ACARA's progression and PaCT both run to Year 10, so the same sub-element/aspect structures persist but with more levels at the top — [ACARA v3](https://education.nsw.gov.au/content/dam/main-education/en/home/teaching-and-learning/curriculum/literacy-and-numeracy/resources-for-schools/lnlp/national-numeracy-learning-progression-v3-for-publication.pdf); [About PaCT](https://assets.curriculumprogresstools.education.govt.nz/public/Uploads/info_about_PaCT.pdf).
- Oak's Aila and Open API already cover KS3–4 (ages 11–16); Aila's weakest subjects are "STEM and modern languages", which dominate secondary timetables — [Aila FAQs](https://support.thenational.academy/aila-faqs); [Oak Open API](https://open-api.thenational.academy/).
- Eedi's diagnostic-question model and misconception graph are the mature tool for secondary maths misconception tagging — [Eedi](https://www.eedi.com/).

### Inferences
- Cornerstone's graph should be a forest, not a tree: one strand hierarchy per domain (NCF domains for Foundational; NCF curricular areas from Grade 3), each with its own rung ladder and its own evidence types; "bands G1–G4" and "rungs R1–R14" can be shared identifiers only if a rung's meaning is defined per strand.
- The stage boundary at Grade 3 (Foundational → Preparatory) is where the system must change mode — from observation-based, no-test evidence to written, competency-based summative evidence — and where the graph re-parents nodes from developmental domains to curricular areas. Design the schema for that migration now (a node can belong to a domain in one stage and an area in the next) rather than forcing one taxonomy across ages 3–16.
- Non-academic domains (socio-emotional, physical, arts) should follow EYFS/Te Whāriki: coded outcomes with observation checkpoints and best-fit judgments, no numeric mastery percentage, and reports written in words.

### Gaps
- No opened source documents a single system that runs one data model unchanged from age 3 to 16; the closest (Cambridge Pathway, NCF) change unit types at stage boundaries, which is itself the finding.
- Australian Curriculum's F–10 structure page (learning areas, general capabilities, content-description codes) returned 404 at both URLs tried; the numeracy-capability page was used instead.
- NCF-SE Middle/Secondary competency counts for maths were not extracted precisely.

## Council position

Confidence: High = multiple opened primary sources agree; Medium = one primary source or consistent secondary sources; Low = plausible but not verified here.

1. **Borrow India's NCF hierarchy as the spine, unchanged in vocabulary.** Node types: Curricular Goal → Competency (stage-level, coded CG-n / C-n.m, ~70 for the Foundational Stage, 19 for Preparatory maths) → Learning Outcome (school-defined, sequenced). NCF explicitly grants schools autonomy over Learning Outcomes, and NCF-SE requires end-of-stage summative assessment "based on the competencies". Confidence: High — [NCF-FS 2022](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCF_for_Foundational_Stage_20_October_2022.pdf); [NCF-SE 2023](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf).
2. **Set the diagnostic grain at the small-step/indicator level, grouped under competencies — hundreds of nodes per subject per stage, not tens.** Cambridge's ~55 Stage-1 maths objectives and White Rose's one-idea-per-step blocks show the working grain; ACARA's indicators and Montessori Compass's lesson "elements" confirm it. NCF competencies alone (13 for five years of maths) cannot locate a child. Confidence: Medium-High — [Cambridge framework with codes](https://chartwell.edu.rs/images/Primary_Maths_Curriculum_Framework.pdf); [Structural Learning on White Rose](https://www.structural-learning.com/post/white-rose-maths-a-teachers-guide); [ACARA v3](https://education.nsw.gov.au/content/dam/main-education/en/home/teaching-and-learning/curriculum/literacy-and-numeracy/resources-for-schools/lnlp/national-numeracy-learning-progression-v3-for-publication.pdf); [Montessori Compass](https://www.montessoricompass.com/language-arts/).
3. **Borrow ACARA's "levels per sub-element, decoupled from grade" design for the ladder; avoid one global scale.** Each strand gets as many rungs "as can be supported by evidence", a child may straddle rungs, and rungs are not a checklist. Confidence: High — [ACARA v3](https://education.nsw.gov.au/content/dam/main-education/en/home/teaching-and-learning/curriculum/literacy-and-numeracy/resources-for-schools/lnlp/national-numeracy-learning-progression-v3-for-publication.pdf); [AC v9 numeracy](https://www.australiancurriculum.edu.au/teacher-resources/understand-this-general-capability/numeracy).
4. **Store every "precedes" edge with its provenance (expert / trialled / validated) and treat expert edges as hypotheses, as Clements & Sarama did.** Within-strand ordering has experimental support (LT beat reverse-order, n=189; level-by-level beat skip-to-target, g≈0.55); cross-strand edges are expert-derived everywhere, including the Common Core Coherence Map. Plan a validation loop on Cornerstone's own data. Confidence: High on the evidence, Medium on the mechanism — [Sarama et al. 2021](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1323924.pdf); [Clements et al. 2019](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1234710.pdf); [Coherence Map](https://achievethecore.org/page/2801/learn-more-about-the-coherence-map).
5. **Borrow PaCT's method for turning teacher judgment into a scale for reading, writing and maths, and report ranges, not points.** Fixed aspects, anchored exemplars ("signposts"), Rasch-scaled totals, expert alignment to curriculum levels, explicit measurement error. Do not apply it to socio-emotional, physical or arts domains. Confidence: Medium (method documented; Cornerstone would need its own calibration) — [About PaCT](https://assets.curriculumprogresstools.education.govt.nz/public/Uploads/info_about_PaCT.pdf).
6. **For Nursery–Grade 2, borrow EYFS/NCF's no-test, observation-led stance, but fix Learning Stories' documented flaws.** Record challenges as well as strengths, tie every observation to a coded outcome, and show continuity over time; NCF-FS bans tests at this stage and EYFS forbids evidence-collection burdens. Confidence: High — [NCF-SE §1.4.1](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf); [EYFS 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a5f8e4fb00f3323bf1a23a1/EYFS_group_and_school_based_from_September_2026.pdf); [Wanoa & Johnston 2019](https://ojs.victoria.ac.nz/nzaroe/article/download/6333/5496/8635); [McLachlan 2018](https://www.hekupu.ac.nz/article/te-whariki-revisited-how-approaches-assessment-can-make-valued-learning-visible).
7. **Borrow Eedi's distractor-level misconception tagging with a curated catalogue seeded from open sources, and keep right-answer-wrong-method as its own signal.** Manual tagging does not scale without model-suggested candidates plus human confirmation; hidden misconceptions behind correct answers are caught by classifiers only 57% of the time with heavy false alarms, so detectors must route to follow-up items, never to verdicts. Use the classical bug taxonomy (smaller-from-larger, borrow-no-decrement, fact, alignment) as code-checkable rules for Grades 1–5 arithmetic. Confidence: High — [Eedi](https://www.eedi.com/); [Eedi Kaggle blog](https://www.eedi.com/news/from-wrong-answers-to-real-insights-how-we-used-a-kaggle-challenge-to-map-student-misconceptions); [Correct Answer Trap](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23205); [Raghubar et al. 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2788949/).
8. **Copy Oak's generation discipline: fixed lesson schema, versioned prompt, retrieval from the locked corpus first, auto-evaluation validated against human raters, moderation layer, human approval before anything reaches a child.** Expect weak human–machine agreement at first (QWK 0.17→0.32 after fixes) and treat the evaluator as triage, not judge; pedagogy per outcome-and-level (Cornerstone's plan) is the safer product class. Confidence: High — [Oak quality blog](https://www.thenational.academy/blog/how-do-we-ensure-our-ai-generated-resources-are-high-quality); [Oak safety blog](https://www.thenational.academy/blog/building-ai-that-s-safe-for-the-classroom-what-we-have-learned-with-aila); [Aila FAQs](https://support.thenational.academy/aila-faqs).
9. **Avoid any AI-generated child-specific high-stakes document (IEP-equivalents, behaviour plans, parent-facing judgments) and any path that bypasses review.** Independent testing found racially biased intervention plans across four leading tools and "professional-looking output [that] may mask errors". Parent notes and prescriptions should be computed from the graph or reviewed, never free-generated. Confidence: High — [Common Sense Media 2025](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/sites/default/files/featured-content/files/csm-ai-risk-assessment-ai-teacher-assistants-final.pdf); [Chalkbeat 2025](https://www.chalkbeat.org/2025/08/06/ai-teacher-assistants-promote-racial-bias-study-finds/).
10. **Borrow TaRL's coarse level ladder and time-bound regrouping for foundational literacy and numeracy, and budget for implementation support, not just design.** The Indian RCT record shows 0.15–0.70 SD gains when dedicated time and mentoring exist, and zero adoption when teachers were merely trained. Cornerstone's rungs should collapse to a handful of grouping levels for classroom use. Confidence: High — [NBER w22746](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22746); [J-PAL](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/case-study/teaching-right-level-improve-learning); [Pratham](https://www.pratham.org/about/teaching-at-the-right-level/).
11. **Adopt mastery learning's high bar (~80%) and "outcome constant, time varies", but do not expect the scheme alone to move outcomes.** Meta-analytic +5 months shrank to +1 month when a mastery scheme was adopted without holding topics until proficiency; White Rose's own evaluation (+1 month, not significant) tells the same story. Confidence: Medium — [EEF Toolkit 2018 (DERA)](https://dera.ioe.ac.uk/id/eprint/33734/1/EEF-Teaching-Learning-Toolkit-October-2018.pdf); [Structural Learning](https://www.structural-learning.com/post/white-rose-maths-a-teachers-guide).
12. **Avoid importing Reggio's emergent "progettazione" or PYP's phase-only continuums as the structural layer; borrow only their documentation practice and CPA as a representation toolkit, not a rigid three-step sequence.** Reggio has "little empirical research directly measuring the impact"; PYP leaves sequencing to each school; Singapore's own researchers call CPA's ordering "not linear and highly fluid". Keep the graph coded and let inquiry/documentation live in the reader's notebook. Confidence: Medium-High — [Education Hub on Reggio](https://theeducationhub.org.nz/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/The-Reggio-Emilia-approach.pdf); [ManageBac on PYP](https://www.managebac.com/blog/ib-pyp-updates-strands-scope-sequence); [Chang, Lee & Koay 2017](https://math.nie.edu.sg/ame/matheduc/tme/tmeV17_1/paper1.pdf).
