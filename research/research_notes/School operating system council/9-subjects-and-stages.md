## KQ1. Early literacy and reading: what counts as evidence, how it is captured, and how accurate machine listening is on young children (especially Indian languages and accents)

### Takeaway
Reading evidence at Foundational and Preparatory stages is overwhelmingly *oral, one-to-one and short* (letter-sound, word, passage reading, retell/comprehension), and the strongest human instruments (ASER levels, England's Phonics Screening Check, running records) are simple, levelled and teacher-administered. Machine listening is good enough at the **aggregate** (automated WCPM correlates ~0.96 with expert scoring in an accented-English classroom study) but weak at the **word/miscue** level (precision ~0.5 in a Dutch primary study); no Indian vendor (Wadhwani AI, IIT Bombay's TARA, Google Read Along) publishes child word-level accuracy for Marathi or Hindi. Audio is therefore worth capturing as evidence, with a model pre-scoring and a teacher confirming anything consequential.

### Cited Findings

**What Indian frameworks count as reading/language evidence**
- NCF-SE: language assessment must cover "fluency and proficiency in the language(s) taught, their ability to effectively communicate ... and their skill of reading and writing in various forms, such as, picture descriptions, simple stories, complex essays..."; also "answering questions based on familiar/seen, as well as unseen passages", and "oral communication in class discussions, debates, role-plays" — [NCF-SE 2023, Part C, "Assessment in Languages"](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf)
- NCF-FS offers a *sample checklist for observation of skills for language and literacy* (Table 6.2A) but warns: "Teachers should use checklists and questionnaires for the purpose of improving and not as a 'report card' of children's achievement"; a "mix and match" of checklist plus observation record is preferred — [NCF-FS 2022, §6.3, pp. 179–180](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/focus-group/NCF-FS_2022EN.pdf)
- CBSE's new third language (R3) from Class IX 2026-27: "The focus remains on oral/reading fluency, not heavy grammar or writing"; R3 is a "mandatory qualifying subject" shown as "Qualified/Not Qualified", with internal grades A–E and qualitative HPC feedback encouraged — [CBSE Secondary Curriculum 2026-27, Part 1, pp. 8–9](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/CurriculumMain27/SecPart1/Curriculum_SecP1_2026-27.pdf)

**Human-administered reading instruments that work at scale**
- ASER reading tool: four tasks — letters; words ("2 letters and 1 or 2 matras/syllables"); Std I text (4 linked sentences, ≤6 words each); Std II text (a 7–10 sentence story); administered one-on-one in the household to every sampled child aged 5–16, in 19 languages; the child is marked at the highest level reached "comfortably"; method unchanged since 2006 — [ASER 2024 report (published Jan 2025; file dated 13 Nov 2025)](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/ASER_2024_Final-Report_13_2_24-1.pdf)
- ASER 2024 (rural India): Std III children able to read a Std II text — government 23.4%, private 35.5%, all 27.1% (2018: 20.9 / 40.6 / 27.3; 2022: 16.3 / 33.1 / 20.5). Std V — government 44.8%, private 59.3%, all 48.8% — [same report, Table 1](https://asercentre.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/ASER_2024_Final-Report_13_2_24-1.pdf). Note: several secondary summaries report 23.4% as the all-schools figure; the report's own table shows it is the government-school figure.
- England's Year 1 Phonics Screening Check: 40 words — 20 real and 20 pseudo-words — read aloud one-to-one to a "check administrator known to the pupils" in a set June week; each pupil scores 0–40; the threshold (historically 32) is **published only after administration**; scores are reported to parents; pupils below standard are considered for a Year 2 retake — [DfE 2026 PSC assessment and reporting arrangements](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/phonics-screening-check-assessment-and-reporting-arrangements-ara/2025-phonics-screening-check-assessment-and-reporting-arrangements); [DfE statistics methodology/release, 9 Oct 2025](https://explore-education-statistics.service.gov.uk/find-statistics/phonics-screening-check-attainment/2024-25)
- PSC results 2025: 80% of Year 1 pupils met the standard (unchanged from 2024; 75% in 2022; 82% in 2019); 89% by end of Year 2; disadvantaged pupils 67% vs 84% for others — [DfE, published 9 Oct 2025](https://explore-education-statistics.service.gov.uk/find-statistics/phonics-screening-check-attainment/2024-25)
- Running records: a generalizability study (19 students, 11 teachers, 10 schools) found raters "highly reliable" but reading *occasion* a major variance source — "approximately eight to ten reads are needed for reliable assessment of students' accuracy and self-correction" — [D'Agostino et al., Reading Psychology, 2021 (ERIC EJ1290680)](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1290680&q=running+records)

**Research accuracy of ASR on children's reading**
- Whisper on US children's speech (Kid-Whisper): zero-shot WER on MyST (grades 3–5) 13.9% (small) / 13.2% (medium) vs ~3.0% on adult LibriSpeech; CSLU Kids scripted 21.3% / 18.6%, spontaneous 32.0% / 31.9%; fine-tuning on MyST brought MyST to ~8.6–9.1% but *worsened* CSLU scripted (33.9% / 47.9%) — a fine-tune on one child corpus did not transfer to another. Authors attribute the gap to developmental inter- and intra-speaker variability; the paper does not address non-native or accented children — [Attia et al., arXiv 2309.07927 v3, May 2024 (AIES)](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.07927)
- Ghanaian classroom ORF (regional accents): Whisper V2 out of the box gave WER 13.5 on the children's reading vs 12.8 average on adult speech, and automated ORF scores correlated **0.96** with expert human scores — no fine-tuning — [Henkel et al., arXiv 2310.17606, Oct 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17606)
- Dutch primary-school miscue detection (Interspeech 2024): Whisper large-v2 word-level WER 9.8% on native children's read speech, but miscue detection precision only 0.52 (F1 0.52); wav2vec2 reached recall 0.83 — i.e., flags many real miscues but also many false ones — [Gao et al., arXiv 2406.07060](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07060)
- Indian children, Hindi–English code-switched speech (HiACC corpus; 20 children aged 10–14 recorded in a school classroom, 2.04 h): WER Whisper-medium 18% (adults 16%), wav2vec2-XLS-R 40% (38%), MMS-1B 36% (31%); code-switching raises WER 30–50% vs monolingual input — [Singh et al., Data in Brief, 17 Jul 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12329218/)
- Pratham's ASER speech dataset: 5,301 children aged 6–14, 81,330 labelled clips in Hindi, Marathi and English, collected via an Android app over 6 months in Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra and Rajasthan; labels are expert judgements of reading level; a baseline classifier on commercial speech-to-text reached **86% accuracy (English)** and 90% precision at 80% recall; **no Hindi or Marathi accuracy is reported** — [Agarwal, Gupchup, Baghel (Pratham), arXiv 1912.04381, 2019–20](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.04381)
- IIT Bombay research line behind TARA: end-to-end wav2vec2 modelling of accuracy, rate and expressiveness from children's story reading (no numbers in the abstract) — [Vaidya, Sahoo, Rao, arXiv 2405.19426, May 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19426); analysis of reading errors of 595 Grade 3 children in English and Hindi, with an automatic phone recogniser tested for diagnostics — [Raman & Rao, Interspeech 2025](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2025/raman25_interspeech.html)

**Products (features and published accuracy)**
- *Wadhwani AI — Vaachan Samiksha / ORF (India)*: ASR "fine-tuned for Indic languages and student voices"; flags missed words, errors and speed; deployed in Gujarat since July 2023 via G-Shala and ConveGenius SwiftChat; 1,20,000 teachers assessed 2.5 million students in Gujarati in batches of 5–10; cites NIPUN benchmarks of 33–52 CWPM for Gujarati — [Wadhwani AI blog, 6 Nov 2025](https://www.wadhwaniai.org/vaachan-samiksha-leveraging-ai-to-bridge-the-literacy-divide/). Programme page (figures "as of January 2026"): Gujarat and Rajasthan, grades 2–8, 34M assessments, 378,901 teachers, 9.2M unique students, 100,255 schools, comprehension feature "new" — [Wadhwani AI ORF page](https://www.wadhwaniai.org/programs/oral-reading-fluency/). **Neither page publishes any accuracy, WER or human-agreement figure.**
- *TARA (IIT Bombay, Prof. Preeti Rao)*: English and Hindi; measures WCPM plus phrasing, intonation and stress; "reliability verified to match that of human experts" (no numbers on the page); adopted by Kendriya Vidyalaya Sangathan — 7 lakh students in 1,200 schools, grades 3–8, baseline completed October 2024; dashboards by child/class/school/region — [IIT Bombay R&D page](https://rnd.iitb.ac.in/tara_mobile_app)
- *Google Read Along (formerly Bolo)*: supports Arabic, Bangla, English, Gujarati, Hindi, **Marathi**, Portuguese, Spanish, Tamil, Telugu, Urdu — [Read Along Help](https://support.google.com/readalong/answer/12281788?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid). Launched 6 Mar 2019 in Hindi and English; offline; "all personal information remains on-device"; an ASER Centre pilot in 200 Uttar Pradesh villages (920 children with the app, 600 control, 3 months) reported 64% of users improved and 39% vs 28% reached the highest ASER level — [TechCrunch, 6 Mar 2019](https://techcrunch.com/2019/03/06/google-introduces-educational-app-bolo-to-improve-childrens-literacy-rates-in-india/). It is a practice tutor; no assessment accuracy is published.
- *Microsoft Reading Progress (Teams)*: a Microsoft staff answer (30 Oct 2023) states auto-detect "is a PREVIEW feature", "is not perfect and may not recognize accents and dialects well. It may also miss some errors or mark some correct words as errors", teachers should "review the student recordings and manually mark", and "Auto-detect is not a substitute for human evaluation" — [Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/1407296/validity-and-authenticity-of-auto-detect)
- *Amira Learning (US; English and Spanish)*: "100% bilingual in English and Spanish"; efficacy claims ("7+ Extra Weeks Growth per year") with no study cited on the page — [Amira, Science of Reading](https://amiralearning.com/science-of-reading); an effect size "0.40—twice as effective as traditional tutoring" is asserted without details — [Amira blog](https://amiralearning.com/blog/isip-orf-saves-time-and-informs-instruction-with-voice-recognition-tech); "approved in 39 states", ">0.8 specificity and sensitivity" — [Amira ISIP Assess](https://amiralearning.com/amira-isip-assess). The independent-ish NCII review shows screening classification accuracy AUC 0.79–0.88 for K–3 and 0.72–0.77 for grades 4–6; validity correlations with i-Ready/MAP "0.70 or higher"; reliability and validity data **not disaggregated** by subgroup (ELLs were 12.6–13.4% of samples) — [NCII tools chart, Amira ISIP Reading](https://charts.intensiveintervention.org/screening/tool/?id=84c77cf7d47622bd). Oklahoma uses it as a state screener: an ARM composite ≥3.45 meets the Grade 3 promotion option; English screening with Spanish directions is available — [Oklahoma SDE technical guidance, updated Sep 2023](https://oklahoma.gov/content/dam/ok/en/osde/documents/services/literacy-policy-and-programs/strong-readers-for-schools/23-24%20Screening%20Technical%20Guidance%20Amira.pdf). A search snippet attributing to Amira a "0.95–0.98 correlation with human experts" and "98–99% WCPM correlation" could not be found on any page opened — **unverified vendor claim**.

### Inferences
- **The accuracy picture splits cleanly by grain size.** Fluency *rate* (WCPM) and level placement can be automated with high aggregate agreement (0.96 in Ghana; 86% level classification in Pratham's English data); *which word* a child misread and *why* (the running-record detail teachers act on) remains unreliable (precision ~0.5). So ASR can pre-fill WCPM and propose miscues, while a teacher confirms any miscue that changes instruction or a report — the same "machine proposes, human confirms" pattern as Cornerstone's maths queue.
- **Marathi/Hindi child ASR is a measured unknown, not a known bad.** The deployments (Wadhwani 9.2M students, TARA 7 lakh) prove operational scale in Indian languages but publish no accuracy; the only Indian child WER found (HiACC) is for 10–14-year-olds and code-switched speech. Cornerstone must build its own gold set (recordings × teacher-marked miscues, per language and grade) before trusting any engine — consistent with its rule that every model output ships with an eval.
- **Stage ladder for reading evidence:**
  - *Foundational (Nursery–G2)*: oral language and listening comprehension by observation; letter-sound and word reading one-to-one (ASER-style levels work across Marathi/Hindi/English); a pseudo-word decoding check near end of G1 (PSC design) adapted to akshara scripts; short audio clips kept as evidence; no written reading test (NCF-FS).
  - *Preparatory (G3–5)*: timed passage reading (WCPM + accuracy + 3–5 comprehension questions) three times a year, ASR-assisted with teacher confirmation; running records only for children below benchmark, and never a single one (8–10 reads for reliability).
  - *Middle/Secondary (G6–10)*: silent reading comprehension on paper (seen/unseen passages, MCQ + constructed response — the CBSE pattern); oral reading kept for diagnosis and for R3 (CBSE's R3 is explicitly oral/reading fluency).
- **Borrow** from PSC: pseudo-words to isolate decoding, a threshold not published in advance, a retake loop. **Borrow** from ASER: a four-level "floor" ladder that is identical across languages. **Avoid**: ASR scores as sole basis for promotion, retention or labelling (Oklahoma's promotion use of Amira shows how fast a screener becomes high-stakes).

### Gaps
- No published word-level accuracy for Wadhwani's Vaachan Samiksha, TARA, or Read Along; no peer-reviewed WER found for 5–8-year-olds reading Marathi.
- EEF toolkit numbers for phonics and oral-language interventions could not be opened (Cloudflare block) and are omitted.
- NIPUN Bharat's official ORF benchmarks could not be opened (`nipunbharat.education.gov.in` closed the connection); the 33–52 CWPM Gujarati figure is Wadhwani's citation only.

## KQ2. Writing: comparative judgement, rubric marking, automated writing evaluation, and reading young children's handwriting of words and sentences

### Takeaway
Comparative judgement (teachers choosing the better of two scripts, many times) is the best-evidenced way to put children's extended writing on a reliable scale, and No More Marking has shown AI judges agreeing with teachers 81–82% of the time against an 87% human–human baseline, with a recommended 90% AI / 10% human split. Automated writing *evaluation/feedback* has moderate effects but its meta-analytic evidence contains **no primary-school studies**. Machine reading of young children's handwritten *words and sentences* is the weakest link: invented spelling, reversals and dysgraphic forms defeat OCR, and even GPT-4o's handwriting reading is "still too low for real-world" grading in one college study.

### Cited Findings
- NCF-FS: writing in the first language develops "through drawing, labelling, inventive spelling" — i.e., early writing evidence is a drawing with labels and invented spellings, not correct orthography — [NCF-FS 2022, language and literacy section](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/focus-group/NCF-FS_2022EN.pdf)
- NCF-SE illustrates Preparatory writing assessment with a Grade 5 teacher using a picture series ("The Fox and the Goat") so students write "in their own words" rather than reproduce memorised sentences — [NCF-SE 2023, Part C, Teacher's Voice 2.6iii](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf)
- No More Marking (NMM), Year 6 "CJ Dynamo" (published 29 May 2025): 3,851 students in 73 primary schools; 449 teachers made 3,825 decisions and the AI 46,844; the AI agreed with 82% (3,118) of human decisions; 90% of disagreements were under 60 scale points — [NMM Substack, D. Christodoulou](https://substack.nomoremarking.com/p/can-ai-assist-the-comparative-judgement)
- NMM Year 7 "CJ Lightning" (31 Mar 2025): 5,251 students in 44 secondary schools; AI agreed with 81% of 3,640 human decisions vs 87% typical human–human agreement; design "10% human judgements and 90% AI", each script seen ~18 times by AI and twice by a teacher; the biggest disagreements involved "teachers being biased by handwriting" or mis-clicks; one head of department validated 269 pupils' AI decisions in 1 h 12 min — [NMM Substack, Christodoulou & Wheadon](https://substack.nomoremarking.com/p/so-can-ai-assess-writing); corroborated by [Schools Week, 31 Mar 2025](https://schoolsweek.co.uk/using-ai-to-judge-writing-could-revolutionise-assessment-trial/)
- NMM help centre: "we suggest a 10% human / 90% AI split"; "Disagreements can be flagged and reviewed"; AI–human agreement "81% in our most recent study, close to the 87% human-human agreement" — [NMM Helpdesk](https://help.nomoremarking.com/en/article/what-is-ai-enhanced-comparative-judgement-cj-uai37s/); vendor claim of 30 essays in "just 6 minutes" vs 2 hours ("95% time saving") — [NMM CJ-AI page](https://www.nomoremarking.com/cj-ai)
- NMM "Assessing Primary Writing 2024–25, Year 1": 10,090 teachers in 894 schools judged 32,742 Year 1 pupils with reliability 0.89 — **unverified**: figures come from a search-result summary; the blog page returned HTTP 503 at every attempt on 2026-09-26 — [NMM blog (not opened)](https://blog.nomoremarking.com/assessing-primary-writing-2024-25-year-1-results-3c711ef7ebd8)
- Automated writing feedback meta-analysis (Fleckenstein, Liebenow & Meyer, 2023): 20 studies, 84 effect sizes, N = 2,828; g = 0.55 (95% CI 0.19–0.91); samples were secondary (g = 0.50) and tertiary (g = 0.58) only — **no primary studies**; heterogeneity large, so AWE "cannot be understood as a single consistent form of intervention" — [Frontiers in AI, 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/artificial-intelligence/articles/10.3389/frai.2023.1162454/full)
- Children's handwriting OCR: on handwriting from Malaysian schoolchildren flagged as potentially dysgraphic, an OCR pipeline reached only ~43.5% character accuracy — [Vydeki et al., arXiv 2411.13595, Nov 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13595)
- GPT-4o grading real handwritten (college) maths solutions: "the model's overall accuracy is still too low for real-world settings"; rubrics improve alignment — [Caraeni, Scarlatos & Lan, arXiv 2411.05231, Nov–Dec 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.05231)
- CBSE 2026-27 requires portfolios and multiple-assessment tasks to be marked with "simple, transparent rubrics ... developed and shared with students in advance" — [CBSE Secondary Curriculum 2026-27, Part 1, §3.3.2–3.3.3](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/CurriculumMain27/SecPart1/Curriculum_SecP1_2026-27.pdf)

### Inferences
- **For Nursery–G2, the photo *is* the evidence.** A drawing-with-labels or an invented-spelling sentence is judged for developmental stage (drawing → labels → phonetic spelling → conventional), not transcribed to "correct text". OCR is the wrong tool; a model may *propose* a transcription and a spelling-stage tag, which the teacher confirms or rejects.
- **For G3–5, run CJ locally before any AI.** A common prompt two or three times a year, teachers judging pairs across sections, gives a school-wide writing scale and exemplars without a rubric fight. AI judging (NMM's pattern) is plausible, but its 81–82% agreement was measured on English scripts from UK children; no evidence exists for Marathi or Hindi writing, or for Indian children's English. Cornerstone would need its own agreement check against its own human–human baseline.
- **For G6–10, rubric marking aligned to CBSE IA** (rubrics shared in advance) with an AI first pass and teacher confirmation; AWE-style revision feedback is defensible here because that is where its evidence lies (secondary/tertiary).
- **Handwriting bias cuts both ways**: NMM found *teachers* biased by handwriting. A typed transcription, confirmed by a teacher, shown to judges could reduce that bias, but only once transcription of children's handwriting is itself validated.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed accuracy found for vision-LLM transcription of 5–8-year-olds' handwritten words and sentences (English or Indic scripts); a Springer chapter testing 10 OCR systems on 33 early-handwriting samples could not be opened (login wall).
- No evidence found on CJ reliability for Marathi/Hindi writing or for Indian primary schools.

## KQ3. Environmental studies (The World Around Us), science and social science: inquiry, practical work, projects — what a photo or short answer can capture and what needs a teacher's eyes

### Takeaway
NCF-SE assesses science and EVS for *concepts plus process* (observe, hypothesise, test, classify, analyse data) and social science for *reasoning with evidence* (sourcing, continuity/change, spatial patterns, caselets rather than general knowledge). Concepts and short reasoning are capturable on paper and photo; process skills, practical technique and collaboration require teacher observation against criteria. CBSE formalises this at G9–10 through internal assessment components (experiments, projects, portfolio).

### Cited Findings
- The World Around Us (Preparatory EVS): students "must be assessed for understanding of concepts and for the ability to demonstrate capacities particular to this subject, e.g., observation, making hypotheses, testing hypotheses via suitable experiments, identification, and classification", "using oral, written and other performance tasks ... answering good questions, making presentations based on group work, creating artefacts, designing or replicating experiments, analysing data and results and participating in discussions" — [NCF-SE 2023, Part C §7.4.5, pp. 373–374](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf)
- Science (Middle/Secondary): assess "the ability to use the scientific method, i.e., observe, ask questions, hypothesise, predict, experiment, collect data, infer ... analyse, decide, and evaluate", through "answering good questions, designing, and conducting experiments, developing models, and participating in debates and discussions"; a Grade 8 teacher voice replaces recall questions ("conditions for life") with application to food preservation — [NCF-SE 2023, Part C, "Assessment in Science", pp. 294–295](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf)
- Social science: assess "sourcing and interpreting evidence, tracing continuity and change, recognising spatial patterns, respect for diversity", through reasoning with evidence, field surveys, map reading; forward-looking proposals are assessed "not for the effectiveness of those proposed strategies ... but for the thinking processes" — [NCF-SE 2023, Part C, "Assessment in Social Science", pp. 328–329](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf)
- Individuals in Society: "Assessment of engagement with current affairs must never be only on the basis of general knowledge"; it "must be based on specific situations, issues, or caselets" — [NCF-SE 2023, Part C §7.5.1.3.2](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf)
- Stage structure: Preparatory has "The World Around Us" as an interdisciplinary area (natural and social world, with aspects of vocational education); the Middle Stage adds Science and Social Science and exposure to Vocational Education — [NCF-SE 2023, Part A §2.3.2–2.3.3](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf)
- Middle Stage: "in addition to a variety of oral and written tests, a variety of practical tests and projects should also be introduced" — [NCF-SE 2023, Part A §3.4.9.3, p. 104](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf)
- CBSE 2026-27 IA: Multiple Assessment (25% of IA) via "observation, oral presentations, discussions, quizzes, experiments, projects, fieldwork, collaborative tasks, concept mapping"; Portfolio (25%) may include "experimental records"; Subject Enrichment (25%) in science and maths "shall promote investigation, hypothesis formulation, experimentation, modelling, and data analysis" — [CBSE Secondary Curriculum 2026-27, Part 1, §3.3.2–3.3.4, pp. 27–28](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/CurriculumMain27/SecPart1/Curriculum_SecP1_2026-27.pdf)
- CBSE 2026-27 introduces "Individuals in Society" and "Environmental Education" in the Class IX scheme with school-based assessment, and "CT & AI" as a compulsory subject in 2027-28 — [same, Tables 2–3, pp. 16–17](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/CurriculumMain27/SecPart1/Curriculum_SecP1_2026-27.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Capturable by photo + machine read (with teacher confirm):** worksheets with short answers, labelled diagrams, classification tables, data tables and simple graphs, map-marking tasks, caselet answers of 1–3 sentences. These fit Cornerstone's existing QR-sheet spine directly; the difference from maths is that answers need a *rubric of acceptable ideas*, not a single computed value, so the machine proposes and a teacher confirms more often.
- **Needs a teacher's observation (human-only judgement):** fair-test design and control of variables in the moment, safe handling of materials, measurement technique, contribution to group inquiry, quality of questions asked during a field visit. Record these as observation-checklist events (criterion met / not yet / not observed) tied to the task, optionally with a photo of the set-up as context rather than proof.
- **Projects** (Middle onward): a rubric shared in advance (CBSE's rule), a process log (dated photos, notes), and a short oral viva; AI may summarise the log for the teacher, never award the grade.
- **Stage ladder:** Foundational — oral explanation and drawing ("what happened to the seed?") captured as audio/photo; Preparatory — TWAU performance tasks plus short written answers; Middle — practical tests and projects enter; Secondary — CBSE IA components plus board-style competency items.

### Gaps
- No sourced evidence found on the accuracy of AI reading children's science notebooks, labelled diagrams or maps; EEF's science guidance could not be opened.

## KQ4. Arts and music: portfolios, process documentation, and the case against scoring young children's art

### Takeaway
Both Indian frameworks define art evidence as *process* (exploring, making, responding) held in a dated portfolio, with students' own reflection; numeric criteria appear only in NCF-SE's older-grade examples (e.g., a Grade 7 music rubric). NCF-FS's arts competencies are exploratory verbs that do not lend themselves to scoring a product. CBSE keeps Art Education as school-based assessment at G9–10. The defensible system is a portfolio with teacher annotation, not a score — and no AI aesthetic judgement.

### Cited Findings
- NCF-FS Curricular Goal 12 (Aesthetic and Cultural Development): competencies are "Explores and plays with a variety of materials and tools...", "Explores and plays with own voice, body, spaces...", "Innovates and works imaginatively to express a range of ideas and emotions", "Works collaboratively in the arts", "Communicates and appreciates a variety of responses" — [NCF-FS 2022, §2.4.5](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/focus-group/NCF-FS_2022EN.pdf)
- NCF-FS uses children's artwork as *evidence of fine-motor development*, compared beginning vs end of term for 3–4-year-olds, stored in a Child Portfolio ("an intentional collection of significant work samples") — [NCF-FS 2022, §6.3, Teacher's Voice 6.2C, p. 183](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/focus-group/NCF-FS_2022EN.pdf)
- NCF-SE: "Assessment in art education must be approached with the fundamental belief that all students can be creative", "based on evidence from the students' art-making processes, creative thinking processes, and their capacity to respond to and appreciate the arts"; a teacher keeps a folder per student plus photographs of 3D work, students date and consolidate folders, self-assess in one-to-one conversations, and curate an informal exhibition that they "don't see ... as an 'exam'"; a Grade 7 music teacher assesses group performances against shared criteria (e.g., originality) on a four-level A–D rubric with peer input — [NCF-SE 2023, Part C §6.7.2, pp. 360–361](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf)
- HPC principle: evidence "beyond performances on tests (e.g., assessment of writing samples, projects, portfolios, artwork, participation in games)", and comparison "with the student's earlier report card and not with the performance of other students" — [NCF-SE 2023, Part A §3.4.10, p. 105](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf)
- CBSE: Art Education is assessed school-based at G9–10 (Tables 2–3), and the 2026-27 curriculum commits to "Recognise Art Education and Physical Education as integral curricular areas with defined learning standards and performance-based assessments" — [CBSE Secondary Curriculum 2026-27, Part 1](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/CurriculumMain27/SecPart1/Curriculum_SecP1_2026-27.pdf); in 2025-26 Art Education was among "subjects having only school based internal assessment" — [CBSE Secondary Curriculum 2025-26](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/CurriculumMain26/Sec/Curriculum_Sec_2025-26.pdf)
- The classic early-childhood portfolio case (ERIC Digest, 1992): portfolios hold "writings, drawings, logs of books read ..., videos or photographs of large projects, tape recordings of the children reading or dictating stories"; anecdotal records are "factual, nonjudgmental notes"; work samples "follow the child's success rather than his failure" — [Grace, ERIC ED351150](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED351150.pdf) (pre-2024)

### Inferences
- **The case against scoring young children's art, as the sources support it:** the Foundational competencies describe exploration and expression, not product quality; a score on the product would measure fine-motor maturity and instruction-following (which NCF-FS already captures elsewhere) and invite comparison between children, which the HPC explicitly rejects. What can be recorded honestly is *participation, exploration of media, persistence, collaboration and the child's own account* — as annotated, dated artefacts.
- **Stage ladder:** Foundational/Preparatory — photo/audio/video of making and performing plus the child's one-line reflection (dictated), teacher annotation against CG-12 verbs, no marks; Middle — introduce shared-criteria rubrics for performances and projects (NCF-SE's Grade 7 example), with self/peer input; Secondary — CBSE school-based Art Education with rubric grades.
- **AI role:** organise, date and caption the portfolio; transcribe a child's spoken reflection; never score aesthetic quality.

### Gaps
- EEF's arts-participation evidence page could not be opened. An arXiv benchmark for multimodal-LLM scoring of children's art ("KidsArtBench") appeared in search but was not opened; its claims are not used here.

## KQ5. Physical education and physical development: motor competence, fitness testing, Indian programmes, wearables and video pose estimation — what is defensible to measure in 3–10-year-olds and what is harmful

### Takeaway
What is defensible for 3–10-year-olds is **observed movement competence** (can the child run, hop, gallop, throw, catch, balance — scored as presence/absence of observable criteria, as in TGMD-3) and, from about age 8, **multi-domain physical literacy** (competence plus motivation, knowledge and daily activity, as in CAPL-2). What the evidence flags as harmful or useless is **BMI reporting** (no weight benefit, more body dissatisfaction) and **using fitness scores to grade, rank or place children**, especially at Grade 3 and below. India's Khelo India battery nonetheless tests 5–8-year-olds on BMI, balance and plate tapping twice a year for a national index and talent identification — which a school should treat as a separate, consented health screen, not learning evidence. Video pose estimation reaches 66–88% agreement with raters on locomotor skills (and ICC 0.96 on one skill under controlled filming); it can aid moderation, not replace the teacher.

### Cited Findings

**Indian frameworks**
- NCF-FS Physical Development goals: CG-1 "Children develop habits that keep them healthy and safe", CG-2 "sharpness in sensorial perceptions", CG-3 "a fit and flexible body" — [NCF-FS 2022, §2.3](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/focus-group/NCF-FS_2022EN.pdf)
- NCF-FS asks the child's file to record "information about the health of the child like height, weight, growth, immunisation" — as background information, not as a learning outcome — [NCF-FS 2022, §6.4.1, p. 186](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/focus-group/NCF-FS_2022EN.pdf)
- NCF-SE PE assessment principles (all stages): "Students must be assessed primarily through demonstrated performance ... through observation of students during field activities, drills, and games against clear criteria for marking"; "Values and dispositions must also be assessed through demonstrated performance"; written tests only for knowledge (body, growth, rules, tournaments); plus teacher records, reflective journals, self-assessment and viva — [NCF-SE 2023, Part C §8.7.2, pp. 415–416](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf)
- NCF-SE Teacher's Voice (Grade 5 dodge ball): observing eight players during a 30-minute game, the teacher focuses on one player for about 4 minutes at a time against a marking scheme — [same, Teacher's Voice 8.1iii](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf)
- NCF-SE diagnosis of the status quo: without a curriculum "with specific learning outcomes and even lesser clarity on assessment possibilities", PE has "too much focus on theoretical aspects of sports ... the dimensions of a football, cricket ground" — [NCF-SE 2023, Part C, PE chapter](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf)
- CBSE: Health & Physical Education was school-based internal assessment only (2025-26); "Physical Education and Well-being" remains school-based at G9–10 in the 2026-27/2027-28 schemes — [CBSE 2025-26](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/CurriculumMain26/Sec/Curriculum_Sec_2025-26.pdf); [CBSE 2026-27 Part 1, Tables 2–3](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/CurriculumMain27/SecPart1/Curriculum_SecP1_2026-27.pdf)

**Khelo India / Fit India fitness assessment**
- CBSE Circular Acad-83/2019 (30 Dec 2019): the Khelo India Mobile Application (KIMA), built by the Sports Authority of India, assesses school children. Ages **5–8**: BMI (height/weight), Flamingo test (static balance), Plate Tap (coordination). Ages **9–18+**: BMI, 50 m standing start (speed), 600 m run/walk (endurance), sit-and-reach (flexibility), partial curl-up 30 s (core), push-ups (boys) / modified push-ups (girls). Assessment "shall be conducted twice in an academic year"; PETs upload data to the app; stated uses: map national fitness, build a "National fitness Index (NFI)", and "identify the potential sports talent amongst the children" — [CBSE circular](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/Circulars/2019/83_Circular_2019.pdf)
- Fit India's "age-appropriate fitness protocols" (reported as launched 24 Sep 2020 for 5–18, 18–65, 65+) — **unverified**: the PIB release returned 403 and the protocol PDF on `yas.nic.in` could not be downloaded this session.

**Fitness testing debates (US) and harms**
- Maryland State Department of Education guidance: health-related fitness testing should **not** be used for "Evaluating students based on their achievement", "Evaluating teacher effectiveness", "Assessing students in third grade and below", or "Course placement"; it cites the SHAPE America position statement on appropriate/inappropriate fitness-testing practice and the FitnessGram Scientific Advisory Board statement — [MSDE, Health-Related Fitness Testing (undated; read 2026-09-26)](https://www.marylandpublicschools.org/about/Documents/DCAA/PE/HealthRelatedFitnessTesting.pdf)
- FitnessGram describes itself as "Criterion-referenced—not competitive—focused solely on individual health benchmarks" (Healthy Fitness Zone standards) — [FitnessGram, About](https://www.fitnessgram.net/about)
- California's PFT (grades 5, 7, 9): since the 2021–22 changes, "Body Composition component is no longer required", schools "are not required to collect or use height and weight, including body mass index", only participation by component and grade is reported (in the School Accountability Report Card), and "The reporting of student performance using the Healthy Fitness Zones are not required"; 2025-26 window 1 Feb–31 May 2026 — [California Department of Education, PFT](https://www.cde.ca.gov/ta/tg/pf/)
- The Fit Study (cluster RCT, California, about 30,000 students in grades 3–8; arms: BMI screening with report to families, screening only, no screening): BMI reports "did not reduce obesity rates", and students showed increased body dissatisfaction (no increase in weight-based bullying or in weight-control behaviours such as skipping meals); lead author: "There is no value in sending BMI reports to parents, and the practice of weighing students at school does have unintended consequences" — [UC Berkeley School of Public Health, 19 Nov 2020](https://publichealth.berkeley.edu/news-media/research-highlights/study-bmi-reports-dont-improve-student-health)
- CHOP PolicyLab (31 Aug 2023): 25 US states conduct school BMI screening, at least 13 by legislation; students labelled overweight are more exposed to bullying and disordered eating; some parents imposed calorie-restricted diets after BMI letters — [PolicyLab](https://policylab.chop.edu/blog/bmi-screenings-schools-failing-report-card)

**Indian school-sports providers (what they actually measure)**
- EduSports (Sportz Village), 2nd annual study (2010–11; 19,797 children aged 7–10 in 73 schools, 39 cities): **skills** — locomotor (run, hop), manipulative (throw, catch), non-manipulative (balance), spatial awareness; **fitness** — 600 m run/walk, 30 m sprint, sit-and-reach, sit-ups, standing long jump, BMI. Findings: 61% lacked fundamental skills; 71% could not throw/catch properly; 43% had unhealthy BMI (24% above normal); a 9-month structured programme for 9,185 children gave "marginal improvement" — [EduSports press release, 29 Sep 2011](https://edusports.sportzvillage.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/EduSports_2nd_Annual_Health_Survey.pdf) (pre-2024)
- EduSports 14th Annual Health Survey (reported 27 Feb 2026): 141,840 children, 333 schools, 112 cities; parameters BMI, aerobic and anaerobic capacity, upper/lower body strength, core, flexibility; 34.4% met aerobic standards and 59.6% had healthy BMI (2025 data); 51% met upper-body and 56% lower-body benchmarks; girls 27% vs boys 41% on aerobic fitness — [Outlook India, 27 Feb 2026](https://www.outlookindia.com/healthcare-spotlight/two-thirds-of-schoolchildren-lack-basic-aerobic-fitness-a-survey-finds). EduSports' own post (28 Apr 2026) says 40% fall outside healthy BMI and a cohort in its structured PE for two years rose from 66% to 82% "overall fitness" (2023→2025) — vendor-reported, no comparison group — [EduSports blog](https://edusports.sportzvillage.com/indias-school-children-are-sitting-their-health-away-heres-a-7-step-framework-every-school-leader-should-act-on/)

**Motor-competence and physical-literacy tools**
- TGMD-3: 13 fundamental motor skills in two subscales (locomotor, ball skills) for ages 3 y 0 m – 10 y 11 m; each performance criterion scored 1 (present) or 0; maximum 100. A reliability study (25 Spanish children, mean age 9.2) found **no significant differences between live and slow-motion video scoring**; intra-rater reliability good-to-excellent, but inter-rater reliability between experts and novices only poor-to-good (locomotor) and moderate-to-good (ball); authors advise raters agree criteria beforehand — [Carballo-Fazanes et al., IJERPH 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7915997/)
- CAPL-2 (Longmuir et al., 2018; ages 8–12): four domains totalling 100 — Physical Competence 30 (PACER shuttle run, plank, CAMSA agility/skill course), Daily Behaviour 30 (step counts plus self-reported active days), Motivation and Confidence 30 (12-item questionnaire), Knowledge and Understanding 10 (5 items). A 2026 Lithuanian validation (846 children aged 8–11) found good fit (CFI .963, RMSEA .047) and alpha .867 — [Frontiers in Sports and Active Living, 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2026.1922697/full)
- PLAY tools (Canada, Sport for Life): PLAYfun/PLAYbasic are observation tools rating movement tasks at "initial, emerging, competent and proficient"; PLAYself (child), PLAYparent, PLAYcoach, PLAYinventory; usable from age 5 (workbooks for 5–12; Pre_PLAY from 18 months); 10–20 minutes per observation — [PLAY FAQ](https://play.physicalliteracy.ca/faq)

**Video pose estimation and wearables**
- Kinect v2 markerless system scoring six TGMD-2 locomotor skills in 203 children aged 3–8: agreement with a rater 66.1% (hop) to 87.5% (run); 72.4% on descriptive ratings; kappa 0.54 (moderate); limits: field of view, occlusion, single camera, no 9–10-year-olds — [Chow et al., J Sports Sci Med 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7429431)
- MoveNet (17 landmarks) plus machine-learning classifiers scoring jump-rope performance (126 primary pupils aged 7–14 and 110 university students, Poland): ICC 0.96, weighted kappa 0.87 vs experts; criteria kappa 0.83–0.87 — but accuracy "depends on controlled testing conditions (precise camera alignment, lighting, contrasting backgrounds)" and covers one skill only — [Makaruk et al., Frontiers in AI 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12358364/)
- Scoping review (F1000Research, v3 24 Apr 2026; search to 30 Jan 2023): 12 studies on AI assessment of basic motor skills in 3–6-year-olds; 71.4% used cameras and 28.6% body-worn sensors; reliability "rarely reported"; no discussion of privacy or ethics — [Figueroa-Quiñones et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13248934/)
- Indian data law: video of a child is personal data; verifiable parental consent is required, and processing "likely to cause any detrimental effect on the well-being of a child" is barred — [DPDP Act 2023, s.9](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Defensible in 3–8-year-olds:** observed FMS against presence/absence criteria (TGMD-3 style), recorded as "shown / not yet", repeated over the year; balance and coordination play tasks; participation and enjoyment; safety habits (CG-1). Physical-development evidence here is observational and developmental, not normative.
- **Harmful or low-value in 3–10-year-olds:** BMI on report cards or letters home, class rankings or "fitness grades", fitness scores in the HPC as marks, talent labels. Height/weight, if collected, belong in a private health record seen by a nurse/doctor and parents, not in the learning graph. This follows the Fit Study and US state practice; India's Khelo India battery points the other way (BMI from age 5, national index, talent scouting), so a school affiliated to CBSE should run KIMA as a separate, consented health-screening track and keep it out of learning evidence.
- **From ~8 (Preparatory):** a CAPL-2/PLAY-style profile — competence (observed), confidence and motivation (short self-report), knowledge (a few written items, as NCF-SE allows), daily activity (self-report) — reported to the child and parents as self-referenced goals (FitnessGram's criterion-referenced, "not competitive" framing), never as a rank.
- **Video:** short clips of a skill trial let a second teacher moderate a first teacher's observation (TGMD-3 reliability holds on video), which directly fixes the weak novice inter-rater reliability. Automated pose scoring is at 66–88% agreement in realistic conditions and should at most *suggest* criteria for a teacher to confirm; filming must be purpose-limited and short-lived under DPDP.

### Gaps
- Fit India protocol text and the Khelo India admin manual (v2.0) could not be opened; the current (2026) status and reporting outputs of KIMA are not verified; the circular is from 2019.
- The JAMA Pediatrics article for the Fit Study (Madsen et al., 2021) was not opened; figures above come from the university's news release.
- No wearable (IMU) study with agreement statistics for primary-age FMS was opened; other Indian school-sports providers beyond EduSports were not researched.
- SHAPE America's own position-statement text was not opened; its content is cited only as summarised by Maryland's education department.

## KQ6. Socio-emotional learning: observational frameworks, self-report at young ages, and the line between observing and monitoring

### Takeaway
For young children the credible SEL evidence is **teacher observation** (anecdotal records; Leuven well-being and involvement scans of ~2 minutes) and the child's **own voice** through simple pictorial self-assessment, which India's HPC already mandates. Validated self-report measures start around age 7–8. Automated monitoring of emotions is legally prohibited in EU education since February 2025, and India's DPDP Act bars "tracking or behavioural monitoring of children" except, under the 2025 Rules, for an educational institution's educational activities or safety. The line: *episodic, purposeful, human-recorded observation that the child and parents can see* vs *continuous, automated inference*.

### Cited Findings
- Leuven scales (Ferre Laevers, Research Centre for Experiential Education, Leuven University): two five-point scales — well-being ("feeling at ease, being spontaneous and free of emotional tensions") and involvement ("intensely engaged in activities"); procedure is "scanning": observe individually or as a group "for about two minutes", then score; "unless children are operating at 4 or 5, learning will be limited"; the initial observation leads to analysis of the quality and suitability of the practitioners' provision — [Leuven scales summary (Moorhouse), citing Laevers (Ed.), *Well-being and Involvement in Care Settings*](https://irresistible-learning.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/The-Leuven-Well-being-and-Involvement-scales.pdf)
- CASEL's five competencies: self-awareness, self-management, social awareness, relationship skills, responsible decision-making, across classrooms, schools, families/caregivers and communities — [CASEL framework](https://casel.org/fundamentals-of-sel/what-is-the-casel-framework/)
- EEF guidance (2019): evidence reviews of "over 700 studies" show SEL associated with ~4 additional months' academic progress ("moderate impact for moderate cost"); "most of the evidence to date is from the US"; recommendation 6 — "Monitor implementation and evaluate the impact of your approaches" (programme-level monitoring) — [EEF, *Improving Social and Emotional Learning in Primary Schools* (ERIC ED612290)](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED612290.pdf)
- Self-report age floor: "research indicates that children as young as 7–8 years old are able reporters of their own mental health"; the Me & My School measure was validated from age 8, while most child measures "do not go below the age of 11" — [Patalay et al., Child Adolesc Psychiatry Ment Health 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4067682/)
- NCF-FS: HPC may include "simple self-assessments by the children themselves" — sample form: "Circle the picture that shows how you worked today" (liked it / found it easy / needed peers, teacher, books: yes, so-so, no); and parents' observations — [NCF-FS 2022, §6.4.3, Figure 6.4A, p. 187](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/focus-group/NCF-FS_2022EN.pdf). NCF-FS Socio-emotional goal CG-4: "emotional intelligence, i.e., the ability to understand and manage their own emotions" — [NCF-FS §2.3](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/focus-group/NCF-FS_2022EN.pdf)
- PARAKH HPC (Foundational): the self-assessment asks children to rate their enjoyment of an activity and its difficulty on a three-level scale and select the resources they needed; a peer gives the same; parents' input is filled "by the teacher in consultation with the parents during the SMCs/PTA meetings" — [PARAKH, How to fill the HPC (Foundational Stage)](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/themes/parakh/hpc-files/how-to-fill-pdf/How-to-fill-the-HPC-(Foundational-Stage).pdf)
- CBSE Foundational HPC guide: attainment levels Beginner (much support) / Progressive (some support) / Proficient (on own), depicted "using any neutral icon such as flower, tree, smiley"; teachers "Focus on and observe one or two competencies per week" — [CBSE HPC Implementation Guide](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/Manuals/HPC_TeacherGuide.pdf)
- EU AI Act Art. 5(1)(f) prohibits "the use of AI systems to infer emotions of a natural person in the areas of workplace and education institutions, except where ... for medical or safety reasons"; prohibitions apply from 2 February 2025 — [AI Act, Article 5](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/5/). Annex III(3) makes high-risk any AI "intended to be used to evaluate learning outcomes, including when those outcomes are used to steer the learning process" and AI "for monitoring and detecting prohibited behaviour of students during tests" — [AI Act, Annex III](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/annex/3/)
- India, DPDP Act 2023 s.9: verifiable parental consent before processing a child's data (under-18s); no processing "likely to cause any detrimental effect on the well-being of a child"; "A Data Fiduciary shall not undertake tracking or behavioural monitoring of children or targeted advertising directed at children", subject to prescribed exemptions — [DPDP Act (MeitY)](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)
- DPDP Rules notified 14 Nov 2025 with an 18-month phased compliance period; children's-obligation breaches attract penalties up to ₹200 crore; parental consent is needed "unless the processing relates to essential services such as healthcare, education or real-time safety" — [PIB explainer, 17 Nov 2025](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf). The Fourth Schedule lets an educational institution process "restricted to tracking and behavioural monitoring (a) for the educational activities of such institution; or (b) in the interests of safety of children enrolled" — [DPDP Rules Fourth Schedule, reproduced by PrivacyLawHub](https://privacylawhub.com/bare-acts/dpdp-rules-2025/schedule-iv-fourth-schedule-exemptions-from-section-9-1-and-9-3-) (secondary reproduction; the official gazette text was not opened)

### Inferences
- **Observing vs monitoring (the ethical line):** observation is *episodic* (a planned 2-minute scan, one or two competencies a week), *purposeful* (tied to a curricular goal), *human-recorded* as a factual note, *visible* to the child and parents, and *used to change provision*. Monitoring is *continuous*, *automated*, *inferential* (emotion or attention from faces/voice), and *used to rate the child*. The DPDP educational exemption is permission, not a mandate: a school that keeps to the first pattern stays well inside it and inside the EU AI Act's ban.
- **Leuven scales are best used as a check on provision** (which activities and settings produce involvement), aggregated by activity/class rather than as a per-child score on a report card.
- **Self-report:** from Nursery, the pictorial three-point "how did it feel / was it easy" is the child's *voice*, recorded verbatim, not a measurement; from about 8 (Grade 3), short validated questionnaires can be used, aggregated for programme review (EEF's recommendation 6), and never as a published per-child SEL score.
- **AI role:** help a teacher turn a term's anecdotal notes into a draft HPC narrative that the teacher edits and signs; no emotion recognition, no cameras for behaviour, no automated "wellbeing scores".

### Gaps
- No validity data found for HPC self/peer ratings of 3–8-year-olds; no Indian validation of Leuven scales found.
- The official DPDP Rules gazette text (Fourth Schedule) was read only via a reproduction; the PIB explainer confirms the education carve-out in general terms.

## KQ7. Stage transitions: what NCF-FS 2022 and NCF-SE 2023 prescribe per stage, the Holistic Progress Card, and what CBSE requires by Grade 10

### Takeaway
The official progression is: **Foundational (ages 3–8)** — no tests; observation and artefacts; portfolio; HPC with self/peer/parent voice. **Preparatory (G3–5)** — formative oral *and written* assessments introduced, plus year-end summative readiness assessment. **Middle (G6–8)** — MCQ and constructed-response tests, projects, practical tests, portfolios. **Secondary (G9–10)** — written, practical, project and open-book tests; board exams offered more than once. CBSE (2026-27) sets 80 marks board + 20 internal in each major subject, with IA split into four 25% components (periodic tests, multiple assessment, portfolio aligned to HPC, subject enrichment) and ~50% of board questions competency-focused; Class X had two board exams in 2026 with the better score counting.

### Cited Findings

**Foundational (Nursery–G2)**
- "Explicit tests and examinations are completely inappropriate assessment tools for this Stage"; assessment must not burden child or teacher, must allow for diversity, and must be recorded through "systematic collection of evidence"; "equal care must be taken not to label children especially based on poorly designed assessments" — [NCF-FS 2022, §6.1.1–6.1.2, p. 176](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/focus-group/NCF-FS_2022EN.pdf)
- Tools: anecdotal records, checklists, event sampling; children's work (artwork, worksheets) in a Child Portfolio; a termly teacher narrative; the HPC "needs to be maintained for every academic year" — [NCF-FS 2022, §6.3–6.4, pp. 179–187](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/focus-group/NCF-FS_2022EN.pdf)
- NCF-SE restates: "At the Foundational Stage, two primary methods of assessment are appropriate. Observations of the child and analysing artefacts"; teachers "primarily drive all assessment activities, which are largely based on observation" — [NCF-SE 2023, Part A §3.4.9.1 and §3.4 principles](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf)
- PARAKH HPC (Foundational): Part A general information; Part B per-domain activity, assessment questions, a rubric across three abilities (Awareness, Sensitivity, Creativity) at three levels (Beginner, Proficient, Advanced), teacher feedback, self/peer assessment, parent observation; Part C year summary — [PARAKH, How to fill the HPC (Foundational)](https://parakh.ncert.gov.in/themes/parakh/hpc-files/how-to-fill-pdf/How-to-fill-the-HPC-(Foundational-Stage).pdf)

**Preparatory (G3–5)**
- Curricular areas: at least two languages, mathematics, art education, physical education and well-being, and The World Around Us; textbooks start for language and mathematics — [NCF-SE 2023, Part A §2.3.2](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf)
- "In addition to observation of students and analysis of artefacts ..., a variety of formative oral and written assessments should be introduced"; "At the end of each year of the Preparatory Stage, there will be a comprehensive summative assessment of the student's readiness to enter the next grade"; assessments are "a combination of observation of students' activity, correcting their worksheets, and short, formal written evaluations" — [NCF-SE 2023, Part A §3.4.9.2 (pp. 103–104) and §2.3.2](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf)

**Middle (G6–8)**
- Adds Science, Social Science and Vocational Education; a third language (R3) is introduced — [NCF-SE 2023, Part A §2.3.3; R3 in the Middle Stage](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf)
- Projects, debates, presentations, experiments, investigations, role plays, journals and portfolios; "Regular assessments comprising MCQs and constructed responses"; "practical tests and projects should also be introduced"; year-end summative readiness assessment; "Formal tests and examinations play a role" — [NCF-SE 2023, Part A §3.4.9.3 (p. 104) and §2.3.3](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf)

**Secondary (G9–10)**
- "Case-based questions, simulations, and essay-type questions"; "written tests, practical tests, projects, and openbook tests"; self-assessment "will also play a key role"; year-end summative "which in relevant cases would be the Board examinations" — [NCF-SE 2023, Part A §3.4.9.4, pp. 104–105](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf)
- Board examinations: three language exams (R1–R3) and one each in mathematics & computational thinking, science, social science and interdisciplinary areas; art, PE and well-being, and vocational education may be assessed "locally at the school level with external examiners"; boards "must offer these examinations multiple times ... in the same academic year" with certification on best performance — [NCF-SE 2023, Part A §2.3.4.7, pp. 68–69](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf)

**Holistic Progress Card across stages**
- HPC is "disaggregated reporting" based on "evidence of student work beyond performances on tests (e.g., assessment of writing samples, projects, portfolios, artwork, participation in games)"; comparison "with the student's earlier report card and not with the performance of other students" — [NCF-SE 2023, Part A §3.4.10, p. 105](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf)
- CBSE's Foundational HPC guide includes self, peer and parent input, levels shown with neutral icons, and weekly observation of one or two competencies — [CBSE HPC Implementation Guide](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/Manuals/HPC_TeacherGuide.pdf)

**CBSE requirements by Grade 10 (verified from CBSE documents)**
- "Presently, approximately 50% of the questions are competency-focused—such as case-based, source-based, integrated, data interpretation, situational, and application-oriented. Remaining items include select-response (MCQs) and constructed-response"; "This structure may be reviewed periodically" — [CBSE Secondary Curriculum 2026-27, Part 1, p. 24](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/CurriculumMain27/SecPart1/Curriculum_SecP1_2026-27.pdf). (Secondary sources give the split as 50% competency / 20% MCQ / 30% constructed response; the CBSE text I opened states only the ~50% share.)
- Each major subject: Board (Class X) or school annual exam (Class IX) of 80 marks, plus IA of 20 marks in both classes; pass requires 33% separately in external and internal components — [same, p. 24](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/CurriculumMain27/SecPart1/Curriculum_SecP1_2026-27.pdf)
- IA 2026-27: Periodic Assessment 25% (three tests, best two averaged, at least one competency-based), Multiple Assessment 25% (observation, orals, experiments, projects, fieldwork; rubrics shared in advance; self/peer assessment), Portfolio 25% ("align with the philosophy of the Holistic Progress Card"; "Document both process and product"; "a limited number of thoughtfully selected artefacts"), Subject Enrichment 25% — [same, §3.3.1–3.3.4, pp. 26–28](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/CurriculumMain27/SecPart1/Curriculum_SecP1_2026-27.pdf). In 2025-26 the same four components were worth 5 marks each — [CBSE Secondary Curriculum 2025-26, §4.2](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/CurriculumMain26/Sec/Curriculum_Sec_2025-26.pdf)
- Grading: Class IX absolute school grades (91–100 A1 ... 33–40 D); Class X 9-point grades awarded by rank order of passed students — [CBSE 2026-27 Part 1, pp. 25–26](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/CurriculumMain27/SecPart1/Curriculum_SecP1_2026-27.pdf)
- Coming changes: three languages (at least two native Indian) from Class IX 2026-27, with R3 school-assessed and "Qualified/Not Qualified"; optional Mathematics Advanced and Science Advanced at Class X from 2027-28 (marks not added to aggregate); "CT & AI as a compulsory subject in session 2027-28" — [same, pp. 8–9, 16–20](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/CurriculumMain27/SecPart1/Curriculum_SecP1_2026-27.pdf)
- Two Class X board exams, first run in 2026: Main exam 17 Feb–11 Mar 2026; Second exam 15–21 May 2026; "the better of the two performances is considered"; 5,13,955 candidates sat for improvement and 3,08,095 (59.95%) improved; overall pass rate after both 96.78% — [CBSE press release, 18 Jul 2026](https://www.cbse.gov.in/cbsenew/documents/Press_Release_18072026.pdf)

### Inferences
- **When written tests begin:** not before Grade 3. Preparatory introduces *short* formal written evaluations alongside observation; Middle makes tests routine; Secondary adds open-book tests and board rehearsal. Cornerstone's QR-sheet spine fits Preparatory onward for every subject, and fits Foundational only as a *worksheet artefact* (NCF-FS lists worksheets as artefacts) — never as a test score.
- **The portfolio is the continuous thread** from Nursery (NCF-FS Child Portfolio) to Grade 10 (CBSE's 25% portfolio component "aligned with the HPC"). An evidence spine that stores dated, typed artefacts per child and competency can produce both the HPC and CBSE IA records from one store.
- **Transition risk:** CBSE's ~50% competency-focused items and case/source-based questions reward reading-heavy reasoning in science and social science; the Middle-stage evidence spine should track reading comprehension of *subject texts*, not only literacy-period reading.

- Which board Cornerstone will affiliate with for Grades 9–10 is not stated in the brief; the CBSE findings apply only if it is CBSE.

## KQ8. Likely role of AI by 2028, subject by subject: which capture and marking steps are plausibly automated with human confirmation, and which are not

### Takeaway
By 2028 the realistic automation frontier is: **transcribe and pre-score** (reading-aloud WCPM, short written answers, CJ judgements of extended writing, organisation and captioning of portfolios, draft narratives), always with a human confirming consequential outcomes. What stays human: miscue diagnosis that changes teaching, early-writing stage calls, practical-skill and PE observation scores, art judgement, and all socio-emotional judgement. Regulation points the same way (EU AI Act: learning-outcome evaluation is high-risk; emotion inference in schools prohibited; India DPDP: children's data, consent, no behavioural monitoring beyond education/safety).

### Cited Findings (anchors for the forecast)
- Aggregate ORF automation already matches experts closely in accented English (r = 0.96) — [Henkel et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17606); word-level miscue detection does not (precision 0.52) — [Gao et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07060); vendors themselves say auto-detect "is not a substitute for human evaluation" — [Microsoft Q&A, 2023](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/1407296/validity-and-authenticity-of-auto-detect)
- Indian-language ORF runs at state scale without published accuracy — [Wadhwani AI](https://www.wadhwaniai.org/programs/oral-reading-fluency/); [IIT Bombay TARA](https://rnd.iitb.ac.in/tara_mobile_app)
- AI comparative judgement of writing: 81–82% agreement with teachers vs 87% human–human, with 10% human judgements retained — [NMM, Mar 2025](https://substack.nomoremarking.com/p/so-can-ai-assess-writing); [NMM, May 2025](https://substack.nomoremarking.com/p/can-ai-assist-the-comparative-judgement)
- AWE feedback evidence covers secondary/tertiary only — [Fleckenstein et al. 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/artificial-intelligence/articles/10.3389/frai.2023.1162454/full)
- Handwriting reading remains a failure point (college maths: accuracy "too low for real-world settings"; dysgraphic child handwriting ~43.5% character accuracy) — [arXiv 2411.05231](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.05231); [arXiv 2411.13595](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13595)
- Pose-estimation scoring of motor skills: 66–88% agreement (Kinect, 3–8-year-olds) and ICC 0.96 for one skill under controlled filming — [Chow et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7429431); [Makaruk et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12358364/)
- Regulatory frame: [EU AI Act Art. 5](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/5/), [Annex III](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/annex/3/); [DPDP Act s.9](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf); [DPDP Rules explainer](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf)

### Inferences (forecast to 2028, by subject; confidence in brackets)
- **Reading aloud** — automated WCPM and level placement with teacher spot-check in English (high); in Marathi/Hindi, plausible if a local eval passes (medium); miscue-level diagnosis stays teacher-confirmed (high).
- **Early writing (Foundational)** — AI proposes transcription and spelling-stage tag, teacher confirms (medium); no automated scoring (high).
- **Extended writing (Preparatory+)** — AI does most pairwise CJ judgements, teachers do ~10% plus review of flagged disagreements (medium for English; low for Indian languages until local evidence exists); AI first-pass rubric marking with teacher confirmation in Middle/Secondary (medium).
- **EVS/science/social science** — short answers and diagrams machine-read and pre-marked against a rubric of acceptable ideas, teacher confirms doubtful and all consequential ones (medium-high); practical and inquiry skills observed by teachers (high).
- **Arts** — AI organises and captions portfolios and transcribes children's reflections (high); no AI judgement of quality (high).
- **PE** — timing, distances and counts are code, not AI (high); pose estimation as a moderation aid for a small set of skills filmed under set-up conditions (medium-low by 2028); no automated rating of children's bodies (high).
- **SEL** — AI drafts HPC narratives from teacher notes for teacher edit/sign-off (medium); no emotion inference, attention tracking or automated wellbeing scoring (high — legally and ethically).

### Gaps
- No longitudinal evidence found on whether AI-assisted assessment in these subjects changes learning outcomes for 3–10-year-olds; forecasts above are extrapolations from accuracy studies and product trajectories.

## Council position

### Takeaway
Keep one evidence spine (child × competency × dated, append-only event, human-confirmed), but make the *event types* subject- and stage-appropriate: observation notes, audio, photo, short video, rubric judgements, CJ scale positions and the child's own voice, alongside the existing machine-read QR sheets. Tests start at Grade 3; scores for art, PE fitness and SEL never do.

### Position bullets
1. **Extend the evidence spine to typed artefacts, not new systems.** Every event carries: child_id, competency, stage, capture mode (sheet / audio / photo / video / observation / rubric / CJ / self-voice / parent-voice), who proposed it (model or person) and who confirmed it. Confidence **high** — NCF-FS demands "systematic collection of evidence" through observation and artefacts ([NCF-FS §6.1.2](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/focus-group/NCF-FS_2022EN.pdf)); HPC and CBSE portfolios need dated process-and-product artefacts ([NCF-SE §3.4.10](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf); [CBSE 2026-27 §3.3.3](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/CurriculumMain27/SecPart1/Curriculum_SecP1_2026-27.pdf)).
2. **Foundational (Nursery–G2): no tests, no marks.** Evidence = teacher observation (1–2 competencies per week), artefacts photographed with the child's QR, short audio; the HPC shows Beginner/Progressing/Proficient icons plus narrative. AI may transcribe, tag and draft; the teacher confirms every entry that reaches the HPC. Confidence **high** — "Explicit tests and examinations are completely inappropriate" ([NCF-FS](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/focus-group/NCF-FS_2022EN.pdf)); weekly-competency cadence and icons ([CBSE HPC guide](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/Manuals/HPC_TeacherGuide.pdf)).
3. **Reading: capture audio, let ASR pre-score, keep the teacher's ear on anything consequential.** ASER-style level checks in Marathi/Hindi/English from late Foundational; a pseudo-word decoding check near end of G1; WCPM + comprehension passages three times a year from G2/G3. ASR may fill WCPM and propose miscues; activation per language only after a local gold-set eval; miscue diagnosis and any report or support decision confirmed by the teacher; running records only as repeated reads for children below benchmark. Confidence **medium** — [Henkel 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17606), [Gao 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07060), [Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/1407296/validity-and-authenticity-of-auto-detect), [DfE PSC ARA](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/phonics-screening-check-assessment-and-reporting-arrangements-ara/2025-phonics-screening-check-assessment-and-reporting-arrangements), [D'Agostino 2021](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1290680&q=running+records); no Indian vendor publishes accuracy ([Wadhwani](https://www.wadhwaniai.org/programs/oral-reading-fluency/)).
4. **Writing: photo in Foundational; comparative judgement from Preparatory; rubrics in Middle/Secondary.** Early writing is judged for developmental stage by the teacher (no OCR score). From G3, common prompts judged by teacher CJ across sections; AI judges may take up to ~90% of pairs only after the school's own AI–teacher agreement matches its teacher–teacher baseline, with disagreements routed to a person. Confidence **medium** — [NMM 2025](https://substack.nomoremarking.com/p/so-can-ai-assess-writing), [NMM help](https://help.nomoremarking.com/en/article/what-is-ai-enhanced-comparative-judgement-cj-uai37s/), [Fleckenstein 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/artificial-intelligence/articles/10.3389/frai.2023.1162454/full), [arXiv 2411.13595](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13595).
5. **EVS/science/social science: paper + photo for concepts, teacher observation for process.** Short answers, diagrams, tables and caselets go through the same QR-sheet queue as maths but are marked against a rubric of acceptable ideas (more human confirmations expected). Inquiry, practical technique and field work are teacher observation-checklist events; projects use rubrics shared in advance. Confidence **medium-high** — [NCF-SE Part C science/TWAU/social science principles](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf); [CBSE IA components](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/CurriculumMain27/SecPart1/Curriculum_SecP1_2026-27.pdf).
6. **Arts and music: portfolio and reflection, no score before Middle stage, never an AI score.** Dated photos/recordings of making and performing plus the child's own dictated reflection, annotated by the teacher against CG-12 verbs; shared-criteria rubrics for performances from Grade 6; CBSE school-based Art Education at G9–10. Confidence **medium** — [NCF-SE §6.7.2](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/NCFSE-2023-August_2023.pdf), [NCF-FS §2.4.5](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/focus-group/NCF-FS_2022EN.pdf), [ERIC ED351150](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED351150.pdf).
7. **PE and physical development: observe movement; do not grade bodies.** Ages 3–8: TGMD-style presence/absence criteria for run, hop, gallop, jump, throw, catch, balance, recorded as shown/not yet, with short clips for a second teacher to moderate; no fitness scores, no BMI in the HPC or the learning graph. From ~8: a CAPL-2/PLAY-style profile (competence, confidence, knowledge, activity) with self-referenced goals, never ranks. If CBSE/Khelo India KIMA testing is required, run it as a separate consented health-screening track visible only to parents and the PE/health staff. Pose estimation only as a teacher's moderation aid. Confidence **medium-high** — [Maryland/SHAPE guidance](https://www.marylandpublicschools.org/about/Documents/DCAA/PE/HealthRelatedFitnessTesting.pdf), [Fit Study](https://publichealth.berkeley.edu/news-media/research-highlights/study-bmi-reports-dont-improve-student-health), [California PFT](https://www.cde.ca.gov/ta/tg/pf/), [TGMD-3 reliability](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7915997/), [CAPL-2](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2026.1922697/full), [CBSE KIMA circular](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/Circulars/2019/83_Circular_2019.pdf), [Chow 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7429431).
8. **SEL: observe, don't monitor; record voice, don't score it.** Teacher anecdotal notes and class-level Leuven scans (used to improve provision); pictorial self/peer assessment as the child's voice from Nursery; validated questionnaires only from ~8, aggregated for programme review; no emotion AI, no behavioural camera analytics; HPC shows narrative, not an SEL number. Confidence **high** on the prohibitions, **medium** on the tools — [EU AI Act Art. 5](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/5/), [DPDP s.9](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf), [Leuven scales](https://irresistible-learning.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/The-Leuven-Well-being-and-Involvement-scales.pdf), [Patalay 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4067682/), [EEF SEL guidance](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED612290.pdf).
9. **Grades 9–10: build to CBSE's IA as a by-product of the spine.** Each subject's IA (four 25% components: periodic tests, multiple assessment, HPC-aligned portfolio, subject enrichment) should be *computed* from confirmed evidence events with rubric links, so the school can defend every internal mark; practice papers should mirror the ~50% competency-focused share; plan for two Class X board attempts. Confidence **high** (if CBSE-affiliated) — [CBSE 2026-27 Part 1](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/CurriculumMain27/SecPart1/Curriculum_SecP1_2026-27.pdf), [CBSE press release 18 Jul 2026](https://www.cbse.gov.in/cbsenew/documents/Press_Release_18072026.pdf).
10. **Consent and retention for audio, photo and video of children.** Verifiable parental consent covering each capture mode; audio/video kept only as long as needed for confirmation and moderation, then reduced to the confirmed event; no child media used to train external models without separate consent; names stay in `pii`. Confidence **high** — [DPDP Act s.9](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf), [DPDP Rules explainer](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf), [Fourth Schedule (reproduction)](https://privacylawhub.com/bare-acts/dpdp-rules-2025/schedule-iv-fourth-schedule-exemptions-from-section-9-1-and-9-3-).
11. **Every model in these subjects gets a local gold set and a human–human baseline before it is switched on.** Report model–teacher agreement next to teacher–teacher agreement (NMM's method), per language and stage; a model that does not reach the human baseline stays in "suggest" mode. Confidence **high** on the method — [NMM](https://substack.nomoremarking.com/p/so-can-ai-assess-writing); [TGMD-3 inter-rater findings](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7915997/); [NCII shows even a leading screener lacks subgroup reliability data](https://charts.intensiveintervention.org/screening/tool/?id=84c77cf7d47622bd).
