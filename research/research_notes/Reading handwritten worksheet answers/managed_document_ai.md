# Managed cloud document-AI services for reading short handwritten answers on fixed-layout worksheets (AWS Textract, Azure AI Document Intelligence, Google Document AI)

Retrieval date for all sources: 2026-09-21 unless another date is given. Prices are list prices in USD. "Vendor claim" means the number comes from the vendor. "Independent" means a third party measured it. Items older than 2025 carry their date.

## 1. Which feature fits "known layout, extract field X at location Y", how is the layout taught, and how many labelled documents are needed?

### Takeaway
All three vendors now have a template or custom-extraction mode that learns from a handful of labelled whole pages. Google's template-based Custom Extractor needs the fewest: 3 training + 3 test documents, taught by drawing boxes, and empty fields can be labelled. Azure's custom template model needs about 5 labelled samples per layout, trains in 1-5 minutes and trains for free. Textract has no position-based template model. Its closest option is Custom Queries (an adapter trained on question-and-answer pairs): minimum 5 training + 5 test documents, training takes 2-30 hours, and it is capped at 30 queries per page.

### Cited Findings
**AWS Textract**
- DetectDocumentText returns only PAGE, LINE and WORD blocks. Fields, tables, selection elements, QUERY/QUERY_RESULT and signatures come only from AnalyzeDocument. — [AWS API_Block](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/textract/latest/APIReference/API_Block.html)
- Adapters (Custom Queries) customise the pretrained **Queries** feature only. You teach one by uploading sample documents, adding queries, and "annotat[ing] your documents by linking these queries to the correct response elements". Labels can be auto-generated by the pretrained Queries and then corrected, or made by hand. — [AWS: Customizing your Queries responses](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/textract/latest/dg/textract-using-adapters.html)
- Minimum is 5 training and 5 test documents. Maximum is 2,500 training pages and 1,000 test documents. Training takes "2-30 hours, depending on the size of the dataset and the AWS Region". Only one adapter can be used per page. — [AWS: Customizing your Queries responses](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/textract/latest/dg/textract-using-adapters.html)
- Hard limits for adapters: training 5-2,500, test 5-1,000, maximum query response length 128 characters, maximum 30 queries per page. Plain Queries allow 15 queries per page synchronously and 30 asynchronously. — [AWS: Set quotas](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/textract/latest/dg/limits-document.html)
- Best-practice guidance: "A minimum of five samples (per query level)… The more samples used for training, the better". Include the key in the query when one exists. Use hierarchical questions for keys that repeat. Label consistently. "Use the exact query used in training for inference." — [AWS: Best practices for Custom Queries](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/textract/latest/dg/best-practices-adapters.html)
- "Query detection is only available in English". Handwriting recognition is English only. — [AWS: Set quotas](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/textract/latest/dg/limits-document.html)

**Azure AI Document Intelligence**
- The custom template model "accurately extracts labeled key-value pairs, selection marks, tables, regions, and signatures". It is "suitable to extract fields from highly structured documents with defined visual templates". — [MS Learn: Custom template](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/train/custom-template?view=doc-intel-4.0.0) (page dated 2026-06-09)
- Template variations: "include at least five samples of each template and train a model for each of the variations. You can then compose the models into a single endpoint." Training data is capped at 500 pages per template model. — [MS Learn: Custom template](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/train/custom-template?view=doc-intel-4.0.0)
- "You only need five examples of the same form or document type to get started." Template training takes 1-5 minutes. Neural training takes 30 minutes to 12 hours. Template models support "coordinates". For low-quality images, "use 10 to 15 images". — [MS Learn: Custom document models](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/train/custom-model?view=doc-intel-4.0.0)
- Region labelling works differently by model: "With template models, synthetic data is generated at training time. With neural models, existing text recognized in the region is selected." — [MS Learn: Custom document models](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/train/custom-model?view=doc-intel-4.0.0)
- Custom neural models: "start with a neural model", "Add at least five labeled samples for each of the different variations". They support selection marks, tables, signatures and overlapping fields. v3.x is limited to 20 builds per month. — [MS Learn: Custom neural](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/train/custom-neural?view=doc-intel-4.0.0) (page dated 2026-08-15)
- Custom generative (LLM-based) field extraction was announced in preview (API 2024-07-31-preview) and at announcement was available only in North Central US. **Dated Aug 2024.** Current GA status and regions were not verified. — [MS Tech Community blog](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/azure-ai-foundry-blog/azure-ai-document-intelligence-now-previewing-field-extraction-with-generative-a/4219481)

**Google Document AI**
- Custom Extractor has three training methods. Generative AI needs "0-50+ documents" and handles high-to-low layout variation. Custom model needs "10-100+ documents". Template-based needs "Low (3 documents)" and requires fixed layouts with no variation. — [Google: Custom extractor overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/document-ai/docs/custom-extractor-overview)
- Template-based mode: "draw bounding boxes around the entire area you expect data to be in (per label)… even if the label is empty". Minimum is 3 training documents, 3 test documents and 3 schema labels per variation. "Adding more documents than the minimum of three needed typically doesn't improve quality for template-based training." Evaluation gives F1, precision and recall, with exact or fuzzy matching. — [Google: Template-based extraction](https://docs.cloud.google.com/document-ai/docs/ce-template-based)
- Template mode is aimed at "W9, 1040, ACORD, surveys, and questionnaires". — [Google: Template-based extraction](https://docs.cloud.google.com/document-ai/docs/ce-template-based)

### Inferences
- Of the three, Google template-based CE and Azure custom template are closest to "draw a box where the answer goes and read what is inside". Both are taught on whole pages with drawn regions. Both would replace most of the home-made geometry rules for binding answers to question slots.
- Textract Custom Queries is question-driven, not position-driven. A worksheet with more than 30 answer slots cannot be covered in one call per page, and slot binding depends on how well a natural-language query names the slot (e.g., "What is written after 'Answer =' in question 3?"). Queries run on English only, which is fine for digits.
- With 1-6 children per layout today, Azure (5 per layout) and Google template (3+3 per layout) are reachable only for some of the 16 layouts. The school's own engine prints the layouts, so it could produce blank or synthetic filled samples, but I found no vendor guidance on training with synthetic filled forms. Azure's neural guidance warns "Synthetic value like a random string can affect model performance."
- Azure composes one model per template into a single endpoint, and Google allows multiple variations per template processor. Each new printed layout would still need a fresh labelled set and retraining. That is an ongoing operational cost per new layout.

### Gaps
- Maximum number of component models in an Azure composed model, and whether the classifier routes 16+ near-identical worksheet layouts correctly: not checked.
- Whether Google template-based CE works with handwritten digits in boxes (no field-type list in the doc fetched), and the per-processor limit on variations: not found.
- Azure custom generative extraction: current GA status, minimum samples and Central India availability not confirmed after the 2024 preview blog.

## 2. Published or independently measured accuracy on handwritten text and digits

### Takeaway
No vendor publishes a numeric handwriting accuracy (CER/WER) for these OCR engines in the docs reviewed. Independent 2025-2026 comparisons are small, cursive-prose based and methodologically weak for digits. In one single-sample test Azure scored slightly better than Textract, and Google Document AI scored worst. Frontier vision LLMs lead the larger semantic-similarity benchmark. Nothing public measures children's pencil digits, so the only reliable number will come from the school's own labelled crops.

### Cited Findings
**Vendor claims**
- AWS (blog, **Nov 2023**): with a Custom Queries adapter, "you will see the accuracy metric (F1 score) improve from 68% to 92%". The dataset was checks, including handwritten and printed checks. — [AWS ML blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/customize-amazon-textract-with-business-specific-documents-using-custom-queries)
- AWS: "Amazon Textract can detect printed text and handwriting from the Standard English alphabet and ASCII symbols". Handwriting is English only. — [Textract FAQ](https://aws.amazon.com/textract/faqs/)
- Azure Read v4.0 handwriting languages: English, Chinese Simplified, French, German, Italian, Thai, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese, Spanish, Russian, Arabic. No Indian languages. — [MS Learn: OCR language support](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/language-support/ocr?view=doc-intel-4.0.0)
- Google Enterprise OCR's latest stable version `pretrained-ocr-v2.1-2024-08-07` claims "better text recognition, precise checkbox detection" with no numbers given. — [Google: Enterprise Document OCR](https://docs.cloud.google.com/document-ai/docs/enterprise-document-ocr)

**Independent or third-party measurements**
- Handwriting OCR blog (Jan 20 2026, modified May 22 2026). **The author sells the winning product.** Test set: a single 100-word legible cursive English prose sample scanned at 300 DPI, which the authors call "deliberately easy". WER results: Handwriting OCR 0.9%, **Azure Document Intelligence 8.67%, AWS Textract 10.5%**, Claude Sonnet 4.6 11.2%, GPT-5 14.4%, **Google Document AI 23.3%**, Transkribus (untrained) 47.7%, Tesseract 95.4%. — [handwritingocr.com](https://www.handwritingocr.com/blog/best-ai-handwriting-ocr)
- AIMultiple OCR benchmark (testing Nov 2025, updated Jul 29 2026). 100 cursive handwriting documents, scored by Sentence-BERT cosine similarity rather than CER. Handwriting results: GPT-5 95%, olmOCR-2-7B 94%, Gemini 2.5 Pro 93%, range 46-95%. Cloud-OCR handwriting scores were not in the fetched extract. — [AIMultiple](https://aimultiple.com/ocr-accuracy)
- arXiv 2604.16504 (Pather et al., Apr 14 2026). 17 multimodal LLMs on a real handwritten medical form. Top models reached about 85% overall accuracy and weighted F1 of about 90% on discrete fields. Gemini 3.1 had the lowest free-text error (WER 0.50, CER 0.31). GPT 5.4 had the lowest hallucination rate (6%). Textract and Azure were not in the abstract. — [arXiv 2604.16504](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16504)

### Inferences
- The two benchmarks that include cloud OCR (Azure, Textract, Google) test cursive prose, not isolated digits in boxes. A language model helps with prose and helps little with digits, so these rankings should not be carried over to "43" written in pencil.
- The 68%→92% F1 adapter result is on checks, which also mix printed and handwritten fields with numbers. It is the closest vendor evidence that a small trained adapter materially improves field extraction, but it is still a vendor claim on a different document type.
- The team's symptoms (neat digits tagged PRINTED, low confidence on legible handwriting, crop misreads) are not addressed in any vendor documentation found. They must be measured on a local labelled set before choosing a vendor.

### Gaps
- No public CER/WER for any of the three services on handwritten digits, children's handwriting, or pencil on worksheets.
- Aggregator and SEO sites seen in search snippets (imagetotable.ai, eklavvya.com, suparse.com) made claims such as "Google Cloud Vision 63.4% on cursive", "Textract ~70% on handwriting" and "Google 80-95% on answer sheets". Their methods were not verifiable, so they were excluded.
- AWS re:Post threads about the reliability of TextType (HANDWRITING vs PRINTED) returned HTTP 403 and could not be read. I found no documented accuracy for Textract's or Azure's printed/handwritten classifier.

## 3. Selection marks, checkboxes and ticks

### Takeaway
All three services detect printed checkboxes and radio buttons: Textract through AnalyzeDocument, Azure through Layout and custom models, and Google through a paid OCR add-on. None of the documentation says it detects a free-hand teacher's red tick drawn anywhere on the page. That is a different problem from a filled or empty box.

### Cited Findings
- Textract: `SELECTION_ELEMENT` is "an option button (radio button) or a check box". `SelectionStatus` is `SELECTED | NOT_SELECTED`. It is returned only by analysis operations (AnalyzeDocument), not by DetectDocumentText. — [AWS API_Block](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/textract/latest/APIReference/API_Block.html)
- Azure: the Layout model extracts "selection marks (like radio buttons and checkboxes)". — [MS Learn: OCR language support](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/language-support/ocr?view=doc-intel-4.0.0). Custom template and neural models both support selection-mark fields. — [MS Learn: Custom document models](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/train/custom-model?view=doc-intel-4.0.0)
- Azure: "Each selection mark has a confidence score representing the confidence of the selection mark and selection state detection." — [MS Learn: Accuracy and confidence](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/concept/accuracy-confidence?view=doc-intel-4.0.0)
- Google: the Selection Mark Detection add-on will "Extract all checkboxes and radio buttons in the document". It is only available on Enterprise Document OCR v2 and costs $6 per 1,000 pages. — [Google: Enterprise Document OCR](https://docs.cloud.google.com/document-ai/docs/enterprise-document-ocr); [Google pricing](https://cloud.google.com/document-ai/pricing)
- Textract Custom Queries "does not support questions related to signatures". There is no equivalent statement about checkboxes. — [AWS best practices](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/textract/latest/dg/best-practices-adapters.html)

### Inferences
- A child ticking a printed box can be read by all three. Teacher red ticks and crosses beside answers are not "selection elements", so no service documents support for them. The school would need colour segmentation or a vision model for those.
- Moving from DetectDocumentText to AnalyzeDocument FORMS (or Layout) is the minimum change for Textract to return checkbox states. FORMS costs $0.05 per page versus $0.0015 for DetectDocumentText.

### Gaps
- No published accuracy numbers for checkbox detection from any vendor.
- Whether Google template-based CE supports checkbox-type fields was not stated in the template doc fetched.

## 4. Confidence scores, calibration, thresholds and human review

### Takeaway
Azure is the only vendor whose documentation makes an explicit calibration claim: 0.95 means "correct 19 out of 20 times". It also tells you to combine field-position confidence with word OCR confidence. Textract gives a 0-100 confidence with no calibration statement. Its built-in human-review service, Amazon A2I, is **no longer open to new customers**, and it only integrated natively with AnalyzeDocument key-value pairs, not with Queries or DetectDocumentText.

### Cited Findings
- Textract `Confidence` is "the confidence score that Amazon Textract has in the accuracy of the recognized text and the accuracy of the geometry points", as a float from 0 to 100. — [AWS API_Block](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/textract/latest/APIReference/API_Block.html)
- A2I: "**Amazon SageMaker A2I is no longer open to new customers.** Existing customers can continue to use the service… we do not plan to introduce new features." (No date is given on the page.) — [AWS SageMaker docs: A2I with Textract](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sagemaker/latest/dg/a2i-textract-task-type.html)
- A2I "directly integrates with Amazon Textract's `AnalyzeDocument`". Activation conditions can trigger on specific form-key confidence, missing keys, all keys within a confidence range, or random sampling. They use "Identification confidence" (the key-value pair was detected) and "Qualification confidence" (the text inside the key or value). A2I resources must be in the same Region as the AnalyzeDocument call. — [AWS SageMaker docs: A2I with Textract](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sagemaker/latest/dg/a2i-textract-task-type.html)
- Azure: "Field confidence indicates an estimated probability between 0 and 1 that the prediction is correct. For example, a confidence value of 0.95 (95%) indicates that the prediction is likely correct 19 out of 20 times… confidence can be used to determine whether to automatically accept the prediction or flag it for human review." — [MS Learn: Accuracy and confidence](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/concept/accuracy-confidence?view=doc-intel-4.0.0) (dated 2026-04-08)
- Azure: field-level confidence "reflects the model's confidence on the position of the value extracted… Evaluate the OCR results for text extraction or selection marks… to generate a composite confidence score." With API 2024-11-30, field confidence includes word confidence for custom models. Read confidence and key-value confidence are independent, so "you should check both". — [MS Learn: Accuracy and confidence](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/concept/accuracy-confidence?view=doc-intel-4.0.0)
- Azure custom template training reports an estimated accuracy score. "It's best to target a score of 80% or higher", and close to 100% for sensitive cases. "Custom neural and generative models don't provide accuracy scores during training." A result of high accuracy with low confidence means the model needs "at least five more labeled documents" or a new model. — [MS Learn: Accuracy and confidence](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/concept/accuracy-confidence?view=doc-intel-4.0.0)
- Azure Read marks each line with `isHandwritten` and a style confidence, e.g. 0.95. — [MS Learn: Read model](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/prebuilt/read?view=doc-intel-4.0.0)
- Google Custom Extractor confidence is "between zero and one". — [Google: Custom extractor overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/document-ai/docs/custom-extractor-overview). Enterprise OCR image quality scores run from 0 to 1 across 8 defect types (blur, noise, dark, faint, small text, document cutoff, text cutoff, glare), and a defect is reported when the score is below 0.5. — [Google: Enterprise Document OCR](https://docs.cloud.google.com/document-ai/docs/enterprise-document-ocr)

### Inferences
- Azure's "19 out of 20" wording is a vendor calibration claim, not an independent measurement. For grading children's answers, set thresholds from the school's own labelled data (a reliability curve per field type) rather than from any vendor default.
- A2I is closed to new customers and never covered Queries or DetectDocumentText natively, so it is not a practical human-review path for this project. The existing teacher-approval screen ("engine prepares, person approves") is the review loop.

### Gaps
- No independent study found on calibration of Textract, Azure or Google confidence for handwriting.
- No date found for A2I's closure to new customers.

## 6. Phone photos (skew, curvature, lighting) vs flat scans

### Takeaway
All three accept photos and handle rotation. Textract states it supports all in-plane rotations, and Google offers rotation correction plus image-quality defect scores. No vendor documents dewarping of curved pages or perspective correction. Azure's guidance is to include photo and scan samples in custom-model training, at least 5 of each variation.

### Cited Findings
- Textract "supports all in-plane document rotations, for example 45-degree in-plane rotation". Maximum is 10,000 px per side. Minimum text height is "15 pixels. At 150 DPI, this would be the same as 8 point font." Synchronous JPEG/PNG limit is 10 MB. — [AWS: Set quotas](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/textract/latest/dg/limits-document.html)
- Textract: "provide as high quality an image as you can, ideally at least 150 DPI." — [Textract FAQ](https://aws.amazon.com/textract/faqs/)
- Azure: "For best results, provide one clear photo or high-quality scan per document". Images must be 50×50 to 10,000×10,000 px. Minimum text height is 12 px for a 1024×768 image (about 8-point at 150 DPI). Each page returns its rotation `angle`. — [MS Learn: Read model](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/prebuilt/read?view=doc-intel-4.0.0)
- Azure custom models: "For subtle variations, like digital PDF documents and images, it's best to include at least five examples of each type in the same training dataset." — [MS Learn: Custom template](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/train/custom-template?view=doc-intel-4.0.0). For lower-quality images, "use 10 to 15 images". — [MS Learn: Custom document models](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/train/custom-model?view=doc-intel-4.0.0)
- Google Enterprise OCR has "Rotation correction" preprocessing and image-quality scores that detect blur, glare, darkness, faintness, cutoff and similar defects. — [Google: Enterprise Document OCR](https://docs.cloud.google.com/document-ai/docs/enterprise-document-ocr)

### Inferences
- The school should keep its own deskew, perspective-correction and crop-to-page step for phone photos regardless of vendor. Template models depend on stable geometry, and none of the vendors documents correction for curvature or perspective.
- Google's quality score could serve as an automatic "retake this photo" gate before any answer is read.

### Gaps
- No vendor accuracy figures comparing photos with scans. No documented handling of page curvature or perspective distortion for any of the three.

## 7. Single cropped regions vs whole pages

### Takeaway
Every trained or layout-aware feature is designed around whole pages: template models learn the page's visual template, and Queries use nearby keys as context. No vendor recommends sending isolated answer crops. Crops also risk falling below the minimum text or image size limits.

### Cited Findings
- Azure template models "rely on a defined visual template" and "use layout cues to extract values". — [MS Learn: Custom template](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/train/custom-template?view=doc-intel-4.0.0)
- Azure Read is "optimized for large text-heavy documents" and supports "single character boxes". Microsoft points to a separate service, Azure Image Analysis Read, for "external images like labels, street signs, and posters". — [MS Learn: Read model](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/prebuilt/read?view=doc-intel-4.0.0)
- Textract Custom Queries: "If a value that needs to be extracted has an associated key, include the key in the query for better results". Annotate "across all pages within a document". — [AWS best practices](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/textract/latest/dg/best-practices-adapters.html)
- Google template CE is labelled by drawing boxes on full documents, including empty areas. — [Google: Template-based extraction](https://docs.cloud.google.com/document-ai/docs/ce-template-based)
- Size limits that apply to crops: Textract minimum text height is 15 px. — [AWS quotas](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/textract/latest/dg/limits-document.html). Azure minimum image is 50×50 px, and minimum text is 12 px at 1024×768. — [MS Learn: Read](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/prebuilt/read?view=doc-intel-4.0.0)

### Inferences
- The observed failure where the crop "Answer=43" came back as "wer-"/"L" fits a crop that is too small or cut mid-glyph. A likely fix is to run whole-page OCR (or a template model) and assign words to slots by geometry, or to upscale and pad crops generously. This is inferred, not vendor-documented.
- Whole-page input also lets one call return every slot and checkbox. That reduces per-page cost compared with one call per crop, since Textract bills each image as a page.

### Gaps
- No vendor documentation or independent study compares crop and whole-page accuracy. A search snippet from an AWS re:Post thread said a user got better results on single-line crops than on the full page, but the page returned 403 and could not be verified.

## 8. Data residency and India region availability

### Takeaway
All three have an India option for the basic OCR. AWS Textract (including Custom Queries) is in Mumbai (ap-south-1). Azure Document Intelligence is priced in Central India, and neural training is supported there. Google has "limited support" in asia-south1 (Mumbai) for OCR, Form Parser and Custom Extractor (custom/template), but **not** for the generative Custom Extractor.

### Cited Findings
- Textract is available in Asia Pacific (Mumbai), among other Regions. — [Textract FAQ](https://aws.amazon.com/textract/faqs/). Custom Queries SKUs are priced for Asia Pacific (Mumbai) (`APS3-SyncCustomQueriesPagesProcessed`). — [AWS Price List API, ap-south-1](https://pricing.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/offers/v1.0/aws/AmazonTextract/current/ap-south-1/index.json)
- Textract data use: it "may store and use document and image inputs… to improve and develop the quality of Amazon Textract". You can opt out through an AWS Organizations policy. — [Textract FAQ](https://aws.amazon.com/textract/faqs/). Adapters stay "limited to the customer's account" (**Nov 2023**). — [AWS ML blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/customize-amazon-textract-with-business-specific-documents-using-custom-queries)
- Azure: Central India pricing meters exist for Read, Layout, custom extraction, custom generative and training. — [Azure Retail Prices API](https://prices.azure.com/api/retail/prices?$filter=serviceName%20eq%20'Foundry%20Tools'%20and%20armRegionName%20eq%20'centralindia'). Custom neural training is supported in Central India, and trained models can be copied to "any other region". — [MS Learn: Custom neural](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/train/custom-neural?view=doc-intel-4.0.0)
- Google: asia-south1 (Mumbai) has "limited support". OCR, Form Parser and Custom Extractor (custom) are available there. Custom Extractor with GenAI is limited to Global, eu, us, europe-west4 and us-central1. — [Google: Document AI regions](https://docs.cloud.google.com/document-ai/docs/regions)

### Inferences
- If children's work must stay in India, Textract with Custom Queries in ap-south-1, Azure custom template or neural in Central India, and Google template-based CE in asia-south1 all qualify. Google's generative extractor does not. Azure's generative extraction was US-only at its 2024 preview, and current status is unverified.
- On AWS, the account should opt out of Textract's use of inputs for service improvement, since the inputs are children's work.
