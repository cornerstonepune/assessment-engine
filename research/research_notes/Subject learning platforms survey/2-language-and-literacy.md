# Language and literacy platforms for Grades 1–8 (English reading, writing and grammar; Hindi and Marathi) — sourced survey, scored against Cornerstone's buying rules

Status date: 26 September 2026. Every URL below was opened during this research session; anything not opened is marked "unverified". Vendor claims and independent evidence are labelled separately. Prices are as published on the page cited, on the date cited, in the currency shown.

Buying rules applied (from the brief): (1) per-child, per-skill data export and the standard it uses — decisive; (2) child-facing AI limits; (3) skill map and alignment; (4) independent efficacy evidence; (5) data protection (pseudonymous accounts, hosting region, COPPA/GDPR/DPDP, voice retention, model training); (6) teacher view and assignment control; (7) cost; (8) practicalities (offline, devices, Indian-English fit, Hindi and Marathi).

Cornerstone constraints that shaped the verdicts: school tablets in school for Grades 4–6, home use optional; nothing screen-based below Grade 4 except teacher-run oral checks; all results must flow into the school's own per-skill evidence record; children's names must not reach vendors.

## KQ1 — English reading platforms: what each offers against the eight criteria

### Takeaway
Only one reading platform surveyed publishes a machine-readable, per-child, per-activity data feed (Lexia: nightly SFTP CSV with activity, reading component, accuracy, rate, unit and Skill Check score, plus OneRoster 1.2 and LTI 1.3 certification). The two free tools with Hindi and Marathi speech support (Google Read Along, Microsoft Reading Progress/Coach) give teachers dashboards but only per-class spreadsheet export (Microsoft) or no export at all (Read Along in Classroom). Amira has the richest AI tutor and the best RCT evidence but is English/Spanish only, hosted in the US, and its structured-export route could not be verified from this environment.

### Cited Findings

#### Amira Learning (AI reading tutor and screener, K–4/6)
- Evidence for ESSA rates Amira "Moderate", grades K–4, 2 studies, 15,602 students; the randomised study (Mostow et al. 2013) had 178 struggling readers in grades 1–4 over seven months and reported +0.64 on the Woodcock Reading Mastery Test; the 2024–25 matched-comparison study in an urban Texas district (Jeans 2025, 15,424 K–1 students) reported +0.26 (K) and +0.06 (grade 1) on DIBELS; average reported effect +0.15. — [Evidence for ESSA: Amira](https://www.evidenceforessa.org/program/amira/)
- 1EdTech certifications for "Amira Learning v2024": LTI 1.3 (certified 30 Apr 2024, status "OUT OF DATE"), Data Privacy (31 Jul 2024, Active), OneRoster 1.1 "CSV Rostering Import Bulk" and "Import Delta" (15 Oct 2024, "OUT OF DATE"). No OneRoster export capability is listed. — [1EdTech: Amira Learning](https://site.imsglobal.org/certifications/amira-learning/amira-learning)
- California Department of Education overview (last reviewed 3 Mar 2025): screener only $4.99 per student; "Amira Suite Subscription for $20 per student" (tutoring, assessment, screener); HMH bundle $9 per student; recommended for K–2 in English and Spanish; "Amira supports Single Sign On (SSO) with Clever and Classlink natively, and can support most SIS systems through OneRoster import"; districts get "an interface for entering and viewing scores, including various reports at the student, classroom, and district level". — [CDE Amira overview](https://www.cde.ca.gov/ci/cl/amirainfooverview.asp)
- Privacy policy (last updated 4 Aug 2026): collects voice recordings "as directed by the district", first and last names supplied by educators, usage data, grade and class; hosted on AWS ("secure AWS facilities", region not stated); the product is "100% bilingual" English and Spanish; deletion on request; the policy does not explicitly state whether recordings train models. — [Amira Privacy Policy](https://amiralearning.com/amira-privacy)
- Privacy and Security page: "Voice recordings are stored in a de-identified format and never used to publicly train AI models"; districts can disable audio collection (with functional limits); "Only anonymized data-derived features, not recordings or identities, are used to improve Amira's models"; SOC 2 Type II; FERPA "school official" exception; COPPA via school consent; Canadian data stored in Canada (no India/EU hosting stated). — [Amira Privacy and Security](https://amiralearning.com/privacy-and-security)
- Real-world retention issue: Las Cruces Public Schools (New Mexico) disabled voice-recording retention for 2026–27 and asked the state to have the vendor delete prior recordings after parents raised data concerns (article 16 Aug 2026; the article does not name the vendor). — [Organ Mountain News](https://www.organmountainnews.com/lcps-amira-reading-assessment-voice-recordings-parent-data-concerns-2026/)

(search listing), but the help domains (support.amiralearning.com, help.amiralearning.com) did not resolve or returned a script-error page from this environment — contents **unverified**. Search snippets describe a CSV of "student assessment data for the last administered assessment period" downloadable by admins, expiring after 24 hours; no API — **unverified**.
- Indian-accent accuracy: no published figure found (see KQ4).

### Inferences
- For the decisive criterion, Lexia is the only reading product whose feed can populate a per-skill record without teacher hand-work: `reading_focus`, `activity_name`, `accuracy`, `Activity_Skill_Check` and `levelname` per student per activity can be crosswalked to Cornerstone's skill ids by a lookup table maintained in the seed.
- Microsoft Reading Progress is the only free tool that yields structured oral-reading data (accuracy, WCPM, error types) in Hindi, Marathi and Indian English, but the export is per-class Excel by a teacher — workable as a weekly file drop, not as an API.
- Read Along in Classroom is a dashboard-only tool; unless Google adds export, its results would have to be hand-copied, which fails the "results flow back" rule; it remains valuable as the only free child-facing Marathi reading buddy.
- Lexia's required "first name, last name" fields can be filled with the school's pseudonyms (the policy lists the fields, not a legal-name requirement) — inference, to be confirmed in contract.
- Amira's English/Spanish-only speech models and US hosting, plus the unverified export path, make it a poor fit despite the strongest RCT.

### Gaps
- Amira: contents of the export articles and whether any API/SFTP exists (help domains unreachable); Indian-accent accuracy; pricing outside US public documents.
- Reading Eggs: CSV export contents (KB blocked); rostering integrations; Common Sense rating (page 404); no independent RCT found.
- Raz-Plus: Learning A-Z pricing and report-export pages blocked; no independent efficacy study found; Common Sense rating page 404.
- Read Along: no published word-error or accuracy figures for children's Hindi/Marathi/Indian English; whether Classroom dashboards can be exported (none documented).
- Microsoft: no published accuracy for Indian children's speech; whether the Insights export includes per-word error lists (only per-class aggregates documented).
- Freadom/Stones2Milestones: privacy policy, hosting, data export, current pricing — none could be opened.
- BOOKR Class: pricing, export, integrations undisclosed.

## KQ2 — Writing and grammar platforms (Quill, NoRedInk, Writable, Grammarly for Education, No More Marking; additions IXL and Mindspark English)

### Takeaway
Quill.org is the strongest fit on the decisive criterion at the lowest cost: free core, Premium at $115 per teacher per year with Skills, Standards and Concepts reports plus a raw CSV data export, a 92% Common Sense privacy pass, and an independent College Board RCT. No More Marking fits a paper-first school for writing quality (scanned scripts, pupil-level scaled scores, CSV download) but is a measurement service, not a practice tool, and its prices are not public. Writable exports per-assignment CSVs with skill and standard tabs; NoRedInk's exports could not be verified; Grammarly's terms exclude under-13s and it offers no per-skill data.

### Cited Findings

## KQ4 — How accurate is automatic speech recognition on Indian children's speech, and what do vendors publish?

### Takeaway
No reading-tutor vendor surveyed publishes an accuracy figure for children's speech, let alone Indian children's Hindi, Marathi or Indian English; Microsoft explicitly warns that pronunciation detection "may not recognize accents and dialects well". Independent benchmarks show adult Indian-accented English roughly triples Whisper's error rate versus US read speech (7.2% vs 2.7% WER), and the one peer-reviewed Indian children's study (Hindi/Marathi L1, ages 5–15) reports ">95% accuracy" only with a custom model and dictionary.

### Cited Findings
- Svarah (AI4Bharat, 2023): 9.6 hours from 117 speakers in 65 locations; WER on Indian-accented English — Whisper large 7.2% (vs 2.7% on LibriSpeech clean), Whisper medium 8.3%, Conformer large 14.6%, Azure US 20.9%, Azure IN 21.3%, Google IN 20.7%, Google US 30.0%; wav2vec2 large 24.9%. — [Svarah abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15760); [Svarah results (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.15760v1)
- Kasture and Jain (Universal Access in the Information Society, published 23 May 2025): corpus of Indian children aged 5–15 with Hindi or Marathi as L1 speaking English; hybrid CNN with a customised pronunciation dictionary; "accuracy of above 95%" and improvement over standard speech-to-text; dataset size and WER not in the abstract; limitations include lack of public non-native children's corpora. — [Springer article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10209-025-01225-3)
- ASER dataset (2019): 81,330 clips from 5,301 Indian children in Hindi, Marathi and English; reading-level classifier accuracy 86% for English. — [arXiv 1912.04381](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.04381)
- IIT Bombay ORF research: two teacher raters agreed at 0.76 on comprehensibility ratings of 10–14-year-old Indian ESL readers — the human ceiling any automatic scorer is compared against. — [arXiv 2405.19426](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19426)
- Microsoft: "Pronunciation detection for each language is generalized based on common pronunciation and may not recognize accents and dialects well"; auto-detect is a preview feature; teachers can correct every marked error manually. — [Getting started with Reading Progress](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/education/learning-accelerators/getting-started-with-reading-progress-in-teams)
- Google: no accuracy statement on the Read Along help pages opened ([Classroom help](https://support.google.com/readalong/answer/14174515?hl=en); [languages](https://support.google.com/readalong/answer/12281788?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid)).
- Amira: English and Spanish only; no accuracy statement on the pages opened ([privacy policy](https://amiralearning.com/amira-privacy)).

### Inferences
- Any oral-reading score produced by these tools for Cornerstone's children should enter the evidence record as "provisional" until a teacher confirms it — which matches Rule 4 (graph reads only `confirmed`) and Microsoft's own manual-correction design.
- A short in-house validation (20–30 children per grade, tool score vs teacher WCPM) is needed before any vendor's oral-reading numbers count as evidence; no vendor supplies that validation for Indian children.

### Gaps
- No vendor-published child-speech accuracy for Hindi, Marathi or Indian English.
- Kasture and Jain's corpus size and WER (behind paywall abstract).
- Exact model-vs-rater correlations in the IIT Bombay paper (PDF text extraction incomplete).
