# Science and social science platforms for Grades 3–8: sourced, scored survey

Researcher notes for the report writer. Scope: child-facing learning platforms, simulations and content libraries for science (incl. EVS / "The World Around Us" for Grades 3–5) and social science (history, geography, civics, economics) that Cornerstone (Pune; Nursery–Grade 4 today, adding 5–8) could buy or adopt, scored against eight fixed buying rules, with an integration pattern per shortlisted tool and a reasoned position on whether social science needs a different model from maths-style adaptive practice.

Conventions used throughout:
- Date of survey: 26 September 2026. Every dated policy or price is the version visible on that day.
- Every claim carries the URL that was actually opened. Where the only source was a search-result snippet and the page itself could not be opened (403/404/JS-rendered), the claim is tagged **[S — snippet only, unverified]** and must not be treated as verified.
- Prices are quoted in the vendor's currency exactly as shown; no INR conversions are invented.
- "Export" means per-child, per-skill data leaving the vendor by API or file. "Content-only" means no child data is created, so nothing needs exporting; the teacher assesses and the school's own engine records the evidence.
- India's DPDP Act 2023 / Rules 2025 context is summarised in Question 4 and applied in the scoring.

## Question 1 — Science candidates: what each one is, what it exports, how it handles AI, alignment, evidence, privacy, teacher control, cost and practicalities

### Takeaway
No science candidate offers per-child, per-skill export by an open analytics standard (xAPI/Caliper) that could be verified; the verifiable interoperability is LTI 1.3 grade passback (Labster, final grade only), Clever/ClassLink rostering (BrainPOP, Legends of Learning, Khan Districts) and CSV/report download (Kahoot-style tools). The strongest science content for Grades 3–8 is either open and content-only (PhET — but note CC BY-NC, not CC BY; Oak; DIKSHA) or US-curriculum subscription content with teacher-assessed investigations (Mystery Science K–5, Generation Genius K–8, Stile 6–8, Gizmos K–12); the Indian commercial offerings (Tata ClassEdge, LEAD) are whole-school bundles with undisclosed pricing and no published data export, and Byju's is in insolvency.

### Inferences
- For a school whose evidence record lives in its own engine, the science tools that fit best are content-only (PhET, Oak, DIKSHA, Mystery Science/Generation Genius-style teacher-run investigations): they create no child data at the vendor and need no export, and the teacher's marking flows into the school's own capture path.
- Where a vendor does hold per-child results (BrainPOP, Legends, Stile, Labster), the only verified standards-based hand-back is LTI 1.3 grade passback of a single final grade (Labster) or rostering/SSO (Clever/ClassLink); none of them documents item-level xAPI/Caliper. Per-skill evidence therefore has to be reconstructed on the school side (question-to-skill tagging held in the school's item table; vendor supplies raw right/wrong per question or a CSV).
- PhET's CC BY-NC licence is workable for classroom use but rules out bundling PhET into any paid product the school might later sell or license.
- Labster is the wrong level for Grades 3–8; Gizmos could not be verified at all this session; Byju's is not a viable vendor; Tata ClassEdge and LEAD are whole-school bundles that would compete with, rather than feed, Cornerstone's own curriculum engine.

### Gaps
- Gizmos: pricing, privacy, integrations and the ESSA Tier 3 report could not be verified (site 403; PDF text unextractable; legacy help domain gone).
- PhET-iO pricing/API terms; PhET Hindi/Marathi translation availability (footer shows 50+ languages but the list was not opened).
- Mystery Science and Generation Genius: no independent efficacy study found; ESSA/WWC searches returned nothing specific.
- Stile: actual per-student price; Australian year levels; any Clever/ClassLink/OneRoster support.
- Khan Academy: rostering/export details and India NCERT course pages (all JS-rendered or 403).
- Legends of Learning: whether reports export as CSV/API; Common Sense rating (404).
- Tinkercad help pages (JS-rendered): nickname/class-code claims rest on snippets.
- Tata ClassEdge and LEAD: pricing, data export, hosting, AI safeguards — none published.

## Question 2 — Social science candidates: same eight criteria

### Takeaway
Social-science content that is free, well-sequenced and openly licensed exists (Oak history/geography under OGL; Gapminder/Dollar Street CC BY 4.0; Google Earth; QGIS GPL; DIKSHA/NCERT QR content) but is either country-specific (Oak, BBC Bitesize, iCivics, Newsela are UK/US in history and civics) or content-only with no child data. The one social-science-capable tool with a verified 1EdTech certification and grade passback is Kahoot! (LTI 1.3 Advantage, AGS 2.0), which is a retrieval-quiz layer, not a curriculum. Newsela is the only leveled-reading source with an explicit social-studies product but is US-hosted, requires student name and email, and its efficacy evidence is largely vendor-run.

## Question 4 — Data protection constraints in India (DPDP) and what they mean for buying

### Takeaway
The DPDP Rules 2025 were notified on 14 November 2025 with an 18-month phase-in for core obligations (to about mid-May 2027), require verifiable parental consent for all under-18s, and carve out educational institutions for tracking/behavioural monitoring limited to educational activities and child safety — which favours pseudonymous, school-controlled data flows over vendor-held named accounts.

### Cited Findings
- Rules notified by MeitY on 14 November 2025; "verifiable guardian consent for certain processing"; official text at MeitY — [Wikipedia: DPDP Rules 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Rules,_2025)
- Phasing: immediate (Data Protection Board); 12 months (Consent Managers); 18 months for "consent notices, purpose limitation… children's data requirements, retention/erasure workflows, and security safeguards"; "Mandatory verifiable parental consent for all data of children under 18… Exemptions apply for healthcare, safety, and education-related processing" (article dated 17 Nov 2025) — [India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/dpdp-rules-2025-india-data-protection-law-compliance-40769.html/)
- Rule 12: "The provisions of sub-sections (1) and (3) of section 9 of the Act shall not be applicable to processing of personal data of a child by such class of Data Fiduciaries as are specified in Part A of Fourth Schedule…" and "for such purposes as are specified in Part B of Fourth Schedule…"; Part A includes schools/educational institutions, Part B safety-related purposes — [dpdpa.com Rule 12](https://www.dpdpa.com/dpdparules/rule12.html)
- Exact Fourth Schedule wording restricting educational institutions to "tracking and behavioural monitoring of educational activities and in the interest of the safety of a child" **[S — MediaNama/law-firm snippets; MeitY and PIB pages returned 403]**.

### Inferences
- A foreign vendor holding named child accounts in the US (BrainPOP, Newsela, Legends) is a Data Fiduciary processing children's data with no educational-institution exemption of its own; the school would need verifiable parental consent per vendor and a cross-border transfer position. Nickname/pseudonym-only tools (Kahoot child mode, Legends nickname, Tinkercad nickname classes) and content-only tools (PhET, Oak, DIKSHA, Google Earth logged-out) minimise this.
- The school's own record (child_id only, names in `pii`) sits inside the educational-institution exemption for educational tracking, so the integration direction should always be "vendor → pseudonymous ID → school engine", never the reverse.

### Gaps
- Official Fourth Schedule text (MeitY PDF and PIB releases were blocked). Whether "educational institution" covers ed-tech vendors acting for a school is unsettled in the commentary opened.

## Question 5 — What works in social science for Grades 3–8, and what a platform can do versus the teacher and the school's own curriculum engine

### Takeaway
The best evidence for social science at these ages is for knowledge-rich, sequenced curricula (Core Knowledge lottery study), project-based units with explicit standards coverage (Duke et al. 2021, ES 0.48 in social studies at Grade 2), document-based disciplinary reading (Reisman 2012, significant effects on historical thinking, transfer, factual knowledge and reading comprehension) and cognitive-apprenticeship instruction in historical argument writing (De La Paz et al. 2017, ES 0.32–0.60) — all teacher-delivered, discourse- and writing-heavy, and assessed by rubric; no adaptive-practice platform was found in the evidence base, and WWC has no social-studies topic at all.

### Cited Findings
- Core Knowledge (Grissmer et al., July 2023): kindergarten lottery, 2,310 applicants, nine Colorado charter schools; Reading/ELA grades 3–6 "ITT (0.241***) and TOT (0.473***)"; science grade 5 "ITT (0.15*) and TOT (0.300*)"; in a low-income district ELA "ITT=0.944**, TOT=1.299**" — [EdWorkingPapers ai23-755](https://edworkingpapers.com/ai23-755)
- Project-based learning in Grade 2 social studies (Duke, Halvorsen, Strachan, Kim & Konstantopoulos, AERJ 2021): cluster RCT, 48 teachers in within-school pairs, low-SES districts; "higher growth in social studies and informational reading"; writing and motivation grew only with higher fidelity — [ERIC EJ1284043](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1284043); brief reports "a 63-percent gain in social studies learning, translating to five to six months" and "a 23-percent gain in informational reading" — [Lucas Education Research](https://www.lucasedresearch.org/publication/the-impact-of-project-based-learning-on-social-studies-and-literacy-learning-in-low-income-schools/); effect sizes 0.482 (social studies) and 0.181 (informational reading) **[S — snippet of the article; abstract does not print them]**.
- Reading Like a Historian (Reisman 2012, Cognition and Instruction): "quasi-experimental control design with 236 eleventh-grade students in five public high schools", six months (105 instruction days); MANCOVA main effect for treatment on all four outcomes — Historical Thinking F(1,171)=17.37, p<.001, partial η²=.09; Transfer of Historical Thinking F=14.95, p<.001, η²=.08; Factual Knowledge F=5.65, p=.019, η²=.03; Reading Comprehension (Gates-MacGinitie) F=8.70, p=.004, η²=.05; treatment teachers used the curriculum 42–72% of instructional time — [Stanford PDF](https://stacks.stanford.edu/file/druid:by786ht6640/a%20reisman_ReadingLikeaHistorian_CognitionandInstruction.pdf) (text extracted locally). Note: high-school sample, so an extrapolation to Grades 6–8.
- Historical writing apprenticeship (De La Paz, Monte-Sano, Felton, Croninger, Jackson & Piantedosi, RRQ 2017): Grade 8, "18-day history and writing curriculum intervention, taught over the course of one year"; effect sizes for basic readers 0.45 (historical writing), 0.32 (writing quality), 0.60 (essay length); "moderate to large effects for all participants" — [ERIC EJ1124455](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1124455)
- Constructive controversy (Johnson & Johnson 2009): meta-analysis of their research; "intellectual conflict can have important and positive effects on student learning and well-being" — [ERIC EJ883319](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ883319)
- Civics games (iCivics): 2011 pre/post study, 253 students, gains largest in Grade 4 — [ERIC EJ991517](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ991517); AIR 2024–25 evaluation reports attitudinal outcomes only, no effect sizes — [iCivics research post](https://vision.icivics.org/civics-isnt-a-given-and-other-exciting-takeaways-from-our-research/)
- WWC topic areas: Literacy, STEM, English Learners, SEL and Behavior, etc.; "Social Studies, History, and Civics do not appear as distinct topic areas" — [WWC](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/)
- Primary science comparison (EEF Improving Primary Science, 28 Nov 2023, six recommendations): "Develop students' scientific vocabulary"; "Encourage students to explain their thinking, whether verbally or in written form"; "Guide students to work scientifically"; "Relate new learning to relevant contexts"; "Use assessment to support learning and responsive teaching"; "Strengthen science teaching through effective professional development" — [Evidence for Learning mirror](https://evidenceforlearning.org.au/education-evidence/guidance-reports/improving-primary-science). EEF's Thinking, Doing, Talking Science: +3 months in the first trial, no average effect in the two larger trials **[S — EEF pages 403]**.

### Inferences — what a platform can do vs what stays with the teacher and the school engine
- Platforms can do (and the evidence supports as a component): (a) supply sequenced, knowledge-rich content and sources — Oak (geography; API), NCERT/DIKSHA (the spine), Newsela-type leveled texts (US-centric), Gapminder/Dollar Street data, Google Earth/QGIS maps; (b) run low-stakes retrieval quizzes on factual knowledge with results passed back — Kahoot (LTI AGS, nickname mode), BrainPOP quizzes; (c) give data and map tools for investigations. These are the "knowledge first" and "sources" parts.
- Platforms cannot do (in any candidate found): the document-based reading protocol with feedback on sourcing/contextualising/corroborating (Reisman); the modelled-then-faded argument-writing apprenticeship (De La Paz); structured controversy/debate (Johnson & Johnson); project units with an authentic audience (Duke). Their outcomes are extended writing, oral argument and source analysis, assessed by rubric — judgment work that, under the school's own rule ("a model where judgment is needed… a number is never a model's last word"), belongs to the teacher with model-assisted reading at most.
- Therefore the school's curriculum engine should hold: the NCERT-theme sequence and skill map, the source packs and argument tasks, the rubrics and the three-signal evidence record (blank / wrong / wrong-with-working generalises to "no claim / claim without evidence / claim with evidence"), while platforms are limited to knowledge retrieval and content delivery.
- The founders' suspicion is supported: social science should not be run as maths-style adaptive item practice. The adaptive layer, if any, is a thin spaced-retrieval quiz on facts and vocabulary; everything else is teacher-assessed evidence recorded per skill in the school's own system.

### Gaps
- No Grades 3–8 RCT of a social-science software platform with achievement effect sizes was found. Duke's exact effect sizes and Johnson & Johnson's effect sizes were only available via snippets. Reisman's sample is Grade 11.

### Social science verdict
Different model, confirmed. The evidence for Grades 3–8 social science favours a knowledge-rich, sequenced spine (Core Knowledge lottery: ITT 0.24 in reading, 0.15 in Grade-5 science), project units aligned to the standards (Duke 2021: significant social-studies and informational-reading gains), document-based disciplinary reading (Reisman 2012: effects on historical thinking, transfer, facts and reading comprehension) and explicit apprenticeship in argument writing (De La Paz 2017: 0.32–0.60). Every one of these is teacher-delivered and rubric-assessed; none is adaptive item practice, and no social-science platform with achievement evidence was found (WWC has no social-studies topic). Platforms should be confined to delivering content and running spaced factual retrieval; the sequencing to NCERT's five themes, the source packs, the argument and debate protocols, the rubrics and the per-skill evidence record stay with the teacher and the school's own curriculum engine.
