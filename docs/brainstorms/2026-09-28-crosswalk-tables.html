What makes the mapping accurate

  • Official text is never typed. It comes only from the importer. The checker finds every statement word for word on its page, in the copy whose fingerprint is recorded. A trigger refuses edits.
  • A model never signs. A prompt may propose a claim. Only an educator can decide it.
  • Unsigned is visible and counts for nothing. The views count approved claims only.
  • Both ends of every link exist. Foreign keys replace today's list of links, which only a script checks.
  • Age, not the grade number, lines frameworks up. Cambridge Primary is "typically for learners aged 5 to 11" over six stages, so matching grade numbers would put a child a stage behind their age. A departure from a framework's age is a signed claim.
  • Complete means a command passes. For a grade and subject, every objective must have a signed statement, a signed NCF-SE link, and a signed Cambridge link or a signed reason there is none. Every outcome must have a signed line at every level, and nothing may be left in framework_gap.

How the missing link gets built

  1. Import Cambridge Primary (Stages 1–6) and Lower Secondary (Stage 7), word for word, with fingerprints. Move NCF-SE and NCERT, already checked, into framework_statement.
  2. Draw each strand's steps with their ages, and sign them.
  3. Write a small gold set by hand: alignments, statements, outcomes and rubrics for one strand (Maths · Number). It is what the drafting prompts are measured against before they are used.
  4. Draft the rest, strand by strand. For each objective: its alignments, with a relation and a reason, and its combined statement. For each strand: the outcome and a line at every level. Everything lands as a proposed claim.
  5. Code checks what code can: every cited statement exists at the right level, every outcome has every level, lines describe what a child does, nothing is duplicated, and the gaps are listed.
  6. Educators sign on one screen per objective card: approve, reject or revise.
  7. Each strand reaches 100% before the next begins.

Rubrics sit per strand, not per objective. Grades 1–4 have 1,750 objectives, about 2.3 per unit. Four levels each would be 7,000 lines, too many to observe or sign. Outcomes per strand, each gathering its objectives, come to roughly 40–60 a grade. That figure is an estimate until the strands are drawn.

Decisions, as of 28 Sep

  1. Cambridge files. The official copies need the Cambridge School Support Hub login; Akanksha is checking whether Cornerstone is registered. Meanwhile the structure is built from copies of Cambridge's own publications (English 0058, Science 0097) and the official PE framework in the Drive, and compared with the official files when they come.
  2. The rubric scale (recommended). Four levels inside the school: Beginning, Developing, Secure (the target, and the signal to move on) and Extending. They report onto the national Holistic Progress Card's three: Beginner, Proficient, and Advanced for both Secure and Extending. Every question already carries one of four difficulties (Easy, Medium, Hard, Advance), so the engine can suggest a level and the educator confirms it. Four to seven levels give the most reliable judgements (Guskey, 2024).
  3. Rubrics per step of a strand. Decided. Questions keep reporting per objective.
  4. Signatory. Akanksha, for every subject, to start. Nobody writes: she approves or corrects what the drafting method produces.
  5. Build. Start now, as data shaped like these tables. The database tables wait for their place in the build order.
