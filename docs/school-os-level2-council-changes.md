## What each seat would say across the table

**rights.** The draft is careful with words, and the banned list did its job: almost every sentence describes an act. What no word-list can catch is what the record becomes. A file kept ten years of a child's good moments is also, in the kind and unafraid behaviours, a file of the hard moments that occasioned them, and in a few lines a file of other children's hard moments. Decide three things before the first record is made. One: a note holds the child's act and nothing else, no incident, no cause, no other child's name, and anything a child tells an adult about themselves or about a peer in trouble goes to your safeguarding lead and never into this system; I have skipped the two lines that would make a disclosure into evidence. Two: a recording (audio, video, self_voice) is the justified exception, taken under parental consent given for a stated purpose, never of a child under eight's feelings or body, and from the Preparatory stage the child knows what is kept in their own voice and can ask for it to be removed; the draft rightly uses no peer_comment or parent_voice, and I would keep it so. Three: every behaviour can be shown in the child's own language, mode and aids, with their agreed accommodations, and the school supplies the contacts, devices and materials, so that no line quietly measures the family instead of the child. Write down your answers to 'why ten years', who may see a record, what a parent may correct, and what a child takes with them or has deleted when they leave; the DPDP Act will ask you these (verify the current rules for schools), and so will the first parent who reads a line about a hard morning in a report about their four-year-old.

- Recording is the default where a note would do: audio, video and self_voice of children as young as three, of their feelings, bodies at play, home news and playground disputes, each needing verifiable parental consent under the DPDP Act and sitting identifiably in a file for ten years.
- In Self & relationships and Society nearly every kind or unafraid record is also a record of the conflict, hurt, exclusion, mistake or private difficulty that occasioned it, sometimes another child's; without a rule that a note holds the child's act alone, naming no incident and no other child, the file becomes a ten-year conflict log, and two lines turn confidential disclosures into routine evidence.
- Around a dozen behaviours can be earned only by a child who speaks, writes by hand, moves typically, works to a fixed time or needs no reminders, so a child with a disability, another first language or an executive-function difference accumulates absences across all eight capabilities that will later be read as deficit.

**development.** This draft is better than most instruments I have reviewed: the sentences are behaviours, the counter-examples mostly teach the right discrimination, and the thinking chain from predicting at four to testing a model at sixteen is a real developmental sequence. Three things I would fix before your own pass. First, the age band: a stage from three to eight is two developmental worlds, and unless you split it or mark the earliest fair age on each foundational line, parents of a Nursery child will read a page of 'not yet' that says nothing about their child. Second, the patterns: whenever a line says 'over a term' or 'for a week', an educator is being asked to summarise, which is exactly the judgement the one-occasion rule was written to keep out of the record; I have rewritten those so the occasion is the moment a dated log or plan is shown, and the pattern lives in the record where it belongs. Third, double filing: about a dozen moments sit under two capabilities, so the same afternoon will count twice and the capability profile will lie; decide one owner per moment and use the freed slots for what is missing, chiefly pretend play, oral vocabulary and a three-year-old saying 'stop' in words. I skipped only three lines: a duplicate, a program filed twice, and a private disclosure to a trusted adult that no observer can judge and that should not be a routine record. The could_not entries are honest as far as they go; knowledge and learning should have had one each, and technology's should have said that Grade 3, with no screen, cannot show five of its six preparatory behaviours. Finally, be plain with educators that a behaviour first seen at six is not late: the stage sets the ceiling, not the age at which a child should reach it.

- The Foundational stage runs from three to eight, so one set of six behaviours is either out of reach for Nursery (reading with the picture covered, exchanging tens, writing numerals, reflecting on what was learned) or trivial for Grade 2, and every young child will show 'not yet' on half of most cells by design; split it into three-to-five and six-to-eight, or mark each foundational behaviour with the earliest age at which it is fair to expect.
- About fifteen behaviours, most under Self, Society and Learning, are written as patterns over a week, a term or a year ('across a term', 'for a week without reminders', 'kept through the year'), which is the frequency rule re-entering by the back door and turns the record into a summary judgement instead of one dated observation; each should be the single moment at which a dated artefact makes the pattern visible, as my edits show.
- Roughly a dozen behaviours record the same moment under two capabilities (tidying, turn-taking, keeping a promise, settling a dispute, coding a program, presenting publicly, running an investigation), so one occasion will be filed twice and the profile will be inflated on kind and on investigation; give each moment one owner and fill the vacated slots with the constructs the draft lacks: pretend play, oral vocabulary, a class vote, a child holding their ground in words, and emotional regulation at 14 to 16.

**educator.** Most of this I could see in an ordinary week, and the counter-examples are better than I expected: the draft knows what over-counting looks like. Three things before your pass. First, decide what one record is. The draft keeps sliding from a moment to a week, a term, a year, and I cannot carry a term in my head with thirty children; where the child keeps a dated artefact (a plan, a log, a training record) one record of that artefact is fine, but where it is my memory of seven days it is not, so let me write the day and let your system show the pattern. Second, the capture modes were chosen for the evidence and not for the room: a dated note is what I will actually write; keep clips for the debate, the outside presentation and movement, and stop asking for audio in circle time. Third, a few lines are not classroom evidence at all: a private disclosure to a trusted adult, a habit kept at home, a peer supported in confidence. I have skipped them, not because they are untrue but because I will not see them and should not file them. Beyond that: adult_sees mostly repeats the statement; tell me when to stand by the bins, which group to sit with, what to look for on the sheet. Use your own difficulty words, keep Grade 3 under the no-screen rule, and remember that in Pune a third of my timetable is Marathi and Hindi, which this draft does not know exists.

- About a dozen behaviours name a week, several sessions, a term or a year as the thing to record; no educator with thirty children can hold that as one dated occasion, so write the day and let the records show the pattern.
- Thirteen video clips and seven audio recordings are asked for moments a dated note holds; in a circle or a playground of thirty the phone comes out and the moment is gone, so observation should be the default and clips kept for set pieces and movement.
- Fourteen pairs across capabilities record the same moment twice (turn-taking, tidying, promises, investigations, public presenting), so one act would count for two capabilities and the record would say more than the child did.

**parent.** I read all 192 and understood nearly every one at first sight, which is more than I can say for most school documents, and I would sign most of them into my child's file as they stand. Three things before your own pass. First, the three words are not equal here: at every stage capable sits on 35 to 45 of the 48 lines and about half carry it alone, while kind sits on 11 to 16 and is nearly absent from Knowledge, Thinking, Learning and Creation, so the report you send me will be a strong academic report with a thin column for the child you say you are raising; either add kind and unafraid lines to those capabilities (I have offered six) or tell parents plainly that those two words are evidenced mainly in two areas. Second, the counter-examples read as if no parent will ever see them, and we will: 'grabs or hits', 'to get them in trouble', 'hands in AI-assisted work as fully their own', 'refuses to argue a side they do not hold' are portraits of a bad child, not the near-miss an educator honestly confuses, and I have rewritten the ones that read as accusations. Third, keep the file to what happened at school: do not record my four-year-old's words about their feelings in their own voice, do not file 'a personal difficulty' or 'a peer's serious difficulty' as routine evidence, do not reach into home habits, and do not assume every child comes with a didi. Two smaller things: parent_voice is never used, so nothing I see at home counts, which I can accept but you should say is a choice; and a handful of words (register, limiting case, unplugged, library) need their plain version in whatever the parent reads.

- The three words are not honoured equally: at every stage capable sits on 35 to 45 of the 48 lines and about half carry it alone, while kind sits on 11 to 16 and is nearly absent from four of the eight capabilities, so my child's report will be an academic report with a thin kindness column.
- Many counter-examples are written as if no parent will read them ('grabs or hits', 'to get them in trouble', 'hands in AI-assisted work as fully their own', 'refuses to argue a side they do not hold'): portraits of a bad child rather than the near-miss an educator honestly confuses.
- Four lines make the school the keeper of what is not its to keep: a four-year-old's recorded words about their feelings, 'a personal difficulty', another child's 'serious difficulty', and a habit at home, while 'didi' assumes what kind of home my child comes from.
