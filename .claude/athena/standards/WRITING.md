# Writing standard

Open this before writing anything a person will read: a README, a handoff, a pull request summary, website or app copy, an email, a report, a message.

## In Nimish's name, in his voice

When the text goes out in Nimish's name (an email, a post, a proposal, copy for his ventures), use his voice skill, `nimish-voice`, where it's installed. Everything else follows the rules below.

## Plain English

- Lead with the answer or the ask; context comes after.
- Short sentences, one idea per paragraph.
- Concrete beats abstract: a number, a name, an example.
- Explain a term the first time you use it, or don't use it.
- Say what is uncertain and how sure you are.

## Never write like an AI

- Words: delve, leverage, seamless, robust, elevate, unlock, empower, cutting-edge, game-changer, testament, tapestry, synergy, holistic, streamline; "landscape" and "navigate" used figuratively; "in today's fast-paced world", "it's worth noting", "I hope this finds you well", "please do not hesitate".
- "It's not X, it's Y" contrasts.
- Lists of three by reflex.
- A bold label opening every bullet in a short piece.
- Em dashes as default punctuation.
- Emoji in headings or bullets, and exclamation marks.
- Superlatives and claims nobody can back up.
- Closing by summarising what was just said, or with "Let me know if you have any questions!".
- Stacked hedges ("may potentially help to") and praise that wasn't earned.

The test: read it aloud. If Nimish wouldn't say it to a client across a table, rewrite it.

## Formats

- Money with ₹ and lakh/crore; dates as DD-MMM-YYYY.
- Headings only when the text is long enough to need finding your way around.
- No client names, PANs, holdings or children's names in anything that leaves production.
